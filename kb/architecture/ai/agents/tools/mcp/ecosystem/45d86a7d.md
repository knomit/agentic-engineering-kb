# The MCP tool ecosystem crossed from reading to acting: action tools went from 24% to 65% of monthly downloads in 16 months, and 95% of general-purpose tool downloads are action-capable

UK AISI monitored public MCP server repositories and analysed "177,436 AI agent tools published between November 2024 and February 2026", published 2026-03-26. This is a census of what people actually publish and install, and it contradicts the working assumption most MCP integration designs start from.

THE SHIFT THAT CHANGES A DESIGN. "The share of action tools (red) in monthly downloads rose from 24% to 65% over 16 months." Note the frame carefully: this is a share of MONTHLY DOWNLOADS, not of published tools, so it measures what gets installed rather than what exists. Two years ago an MCP integration was predominantly a read path and approval design was an edge case; on this measurement it is now the default case. If your permission model treats writes as the exception, the ecosystem has inverted underneath it.

AND THE GROWTH IS CONCENTRATED IN THE WORST COMBINATION. "General purpose tools operating in unconstrained environments like the open web grew from 41% to 50% of downloads", and "95% of general-purpose tool downloads involved action capabilities". So the half of the ecosystem that is growing is the half that acts, in an environment nobody controls — which is exactly the acting-plus-untrusted-content pairing the prompt-injection literature treats as the dangerous one. The two properties are not independently distributed; they arrive together.

WHERE THE ECOSYSTEM ACTUALLY IS, AND WHERE IT IS NOT. "Tools for software development and IT account for 67% of all published tools and 90% of downloads." The consequence for a builder outside developer tooling is direct: the apparent size of the ecosystem is not available to you. A count of "thousands of MCP integrations" is, at these proportions, mostly a count of developer tooling, and the long tail in any other domain is thin enough that you should expect to write your own servers rather than adopt them.

SCALE AND THE HIGH-STAKES TAIL. "[T]he number of publicly released MCP tools rose from around 5,000 to 177,000, accompanied by a substantial increase in download activity (from 80,000 to 14 million)", and "MCP servers with payment execution capabilities grew from 46 in January 2025 to over 1,200 in January 2026" — a ~26x growth in a capability class where a single mis-scoped tool call is irreversible. That count is of published servers, not of installs.

HOW THE SERVERS THEMSELVES ARE BUILT: "The share of newly created servers with detected AI assistance rose from 6% in January 2025 to 55% in January 2026." The post does not describe the detector, so read this as the output of AISI's own detection method rather than a ground-truth measurement — and note what it implies for the supply chain, since a majority of new servers in the ecosystem you install from are now themselves model-written.
