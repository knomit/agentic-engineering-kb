# Over half of successful 4-8 hour agent tasks at OpenAI's research org involved at least one human intervention — and high-level planning stays a minimal fraction of what gets delegated

OpenAI published internal measurements of coding-agent use inside its research organisation through August 2026. Read as a shape rather than as benchmarks, three of the findings change how you would design an agent workflow.

THE INTERVENTION RATE IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS. Success rates on researcher-assigned tasks rose from January to July across difficulty buckets, bucketed by the estimated time a human would take. But "agents still require significant human steering to be successful, especially as task complexity rises. In the last 6 months, over half of successful 4-8 hour tasks involved 1 or more interventions." Long-horizon delegation at a frontier lab is majority-interventional even in the cases that SUCCEED. A design that assumes fire-and-forget on multi-hour work is designing for the minority case; the engineering question is how cheap and how well-placed you can make the intervention, not how to eliminate it.

WHAT GETS DELEGATED IS THE MIDDLE, NOT THE TOP. Classifying agent tokens under Epoch AI's six-phase AI R&D taxonomy — Decide, Design, Build, Run, Analyze, Communicate — every category grew between January and August 2026, with research and infrastructure code dominant throughout and notable growth in technical help and monitoring runs. But "High-level planning still remains a minimal fraction of agent output tokens." OpenAI states that people "still set our research priorities, judge which ideas and results to pursue, and decide whether to scale, pause, or deploy systems."

THE ADOPTION SHAPE. By mid-August the median researcher ranked by agent usage was integrating agents daily at more than $600 per day of inference at API prices, with the 90th percentile above $7,000 per day. Total agent runtime across the research organisation was below total human labour before June 2026 and passed it thereafter; by mid-August, on an 8-hour-workday basis, "the research organization uses 3.1 agent-workdays of effort for every workday of human labor." Highly concurrent workflows (four or more agents at once) are increasing, and the concurrency counts include subagents created downstream of the ones a user launched directly — not just top-level sessions.

A SECOND-ORDER EFFECT WORTH INSTRUMENTING. Multiple teams that ran office hours to help researchers troubleshoot experiments reported declining attendance in 2026, and one stopped holding sessions entirely. Top-level posts per day to a main internal channel where researchers seek technical support from other teams declined, and OpenAI states that to its knowledge that decrease "has not been offset by queries shifting to another technical support channel run by humans." Peer-to-peer support traffic is an already-collected, cheap proxy for whether agents are genuinely absorbing work rather than displacing it sideways.

SCOPE AND HEDGING, supplied by OpenAI itself and load-bearing here. The measurement effort is "still preliminary"; code volume and experiment counts are "relatively easy to measure, but can be hard to interpret"; available compute grew significantly since 2025, which confounds the rise in experiments per active experimenter; "Metrics of coding agent use cover most, but not all, usage"; task-success classification was done by an agentic classifier and excludes uncertain outcomes and thin cells; and OpenAI cautions that "the overall pace of progress likely won't keep pace with these specific metrics" because the least automatable tasks become the binding bottleneck as automation advances. "Researcher" is defined broadly, covering infrastructure builders and research project managers as well as scientists.
