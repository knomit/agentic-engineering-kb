# Train the attacker by self-play, and reward the defender for finishing the task as well as resisting — otherwise the defender learns to refuse

OpenAI's GPT-Red (published 2026-07-15) is a dedicated attacker model trained by self-play reinforcement learning against a population of defender LLMs trained at the same time. Four design decisions in it are transferable to anyone hardening an agent, and they are the part worth copying rather than the headline scores:

1. THE DEFENDER'S REWARD IS TWO-SIDED. The attacker is rewarded for eliciting a valid failure; the defenders are rewarded for resisting the attack AND completing their original tasks. Without the second clause the cheapest defender policy is to refuse or do less. OpenAI names this failure directly: "A model can appear safer by refusing more requests or becoming less capable. A model that does less is naturally harder to attack, but that is not useful robustness." They report evaluating general capability and purpose-built over-refusal tasks alongside robustness, and say capability was unaffected.
2. EACH ENVIRONMENT CARRIES AN EXPLICIT THREAT MODEL naming what the attacker controls and what counts as success — part of a local file, a webpage banner, an email body, or the output of a tool. The channel is a parameter of the scenario, not an assumption baked into the harness.
3. THE ATTACKER IS NEVER DEPLOYED. "We keep GPT-Red separate from the models we deploy" — the offensive capability is trained deliberately and kept out of shipped weights, so hardening the product does not also ship the attack tool.
4. IT IS AN ARMS RACE BY CONSTRUCTION: as defenders get more robust the attacker is forced to find stronger and more diverse attacks, which is what generates training data at a volume human red-teaming cannot reach. OpenAI's stated motivation is that human red-teaming "cannot generate the volume and diversity of adversarial data needed to improve model robustness through training."

REPORTED RESULTS, each with its frame, because they measure different things:
- ATTACKER GENERALISATION TO NOVEL SCENARIOS: on an internal replica of the indirect-prompt-injection arena of Dziemian et al. (2025), with scenarios and goals distinct from GPT-Red's training, GPT-Red succeeded on 84% of scenarios against GPT-5.1 versus 13% for human red-teamers attacking the same target.
- AGAINST LIVE THIRD-PARTY AGENTS: GPT-Red iterated attacks in simulation and transferred them to a production vending-machine agent built by Andon Labs, achieving all three malicious objectives (reprice an expensive in-stock item to the $0.50 floor; order a $100+ item and offer it at $0.50; cancel another customer's order). OpenAI says the vulnerabilities were disclosed and safeguards are being tested. Separately it beat a Codex CLI agent backed by GPT-5.4 mini on 10 held-out data-exfiltration scenarios, more effectively and with fewer tokens than a prompted GPT-5.5 baseline.
- DEFENDER IMPROVEMENT: GPT-5.6 Sol is reported at 6x fewer failures than OpenAI's best production model four months earlier on their hardest direct prompt-injection benchmark; a "Fake Chain-of-Thought" attack class an early GPT-Red discovered went from upwards of 95% success on GPT-5.1 to below 10% on GPT-5.6 Sol; and GPT-5.6 Sol "fails on only 0.05% of GPT-Red's direct prompt injections", a figure the chart caption scopes to held-out ENVIRONMENTS.

WHAT THIS FACT DOES NOT CLAIM: that 0.05% is a general robustness rate. GPT-5.6 was adversarially trained on GPT-Red's attacks, so the environments were held out but the attacker was not. See the companion decisions fact on what a number measured against your own trained adversary establishes. Also note that the 84%-vs-13% comparison targets GPT-5.1, not the hardened model, so it evidences the ATTACKER's generalisation and not the DEFENDER's.
