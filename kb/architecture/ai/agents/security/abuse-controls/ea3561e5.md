# The distillation defenses that moved the number sit at the account, context-mutability, tenancy and billing layers rather than in the model — and each one is a stated tax on the honest caller

Anthropic describes distillation attacks as "covert, industrial-scale campaigns to extract the capabilities of one model and replicate them in another, without the authorization of the original model's developers", often run through networks of fraudulent accounts. Every load-bearing control in its account sits OUTSIDE the model:

- **Context mutability gated by account age.** For Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, "new API accounts (those created on or after August 31, 2026)" cannot edit Claude's prior context. That targets the specific mechanic of repeated extraction through a rotating proxy network, not the request's content.
- **Transcript scoping by tenant.** With Claude Sonnet 5.5, "we've made reasoning transcripts accessible only to the organization whose request produced the transcript", and reasoning cannot be read by earlier models with weaker safeguards.
- **A two-stage classifier**, built on the constitutional-classifiers approach and red-teamed internally and externally, with "a false positive rate of less than 0.1%", running on Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5 and 5.5, and Fable 5 and 5.1. Patches closed specific techniques: asking Claude to translate its earlier reasoning, or to re-emit it inside tags.
- **Economic friction.** Stricter usage limits for users who repeatedly attempt extraction, bans — "We've used these defenses to ban tens of thousands of accounts associated with distillation" — and price: "we have resumed charging for requests that are blocked by our safeguards before Claude responds".

Reported effect of the classifier: extracted reasoning tokens are "consistently 10 to 100 times lower than it was before".

IF YOU ARE DESIGNING ABUSE CONTROLS FOR YOUR OWN INFERENCE PRODUCT, THIS IS THE SHAPE TO EXPECT: the effective levers are identity, context mutability, tenancy and price, and every one of them bills a cost to legitimate callers. The source states each cost rather than claiming none — "preserved thinking has required some developers to update their integrations"; benign requests are misidentified in fewer than 0.1% of cases; and customers now pay for requests the safeguards blocked before the model answered. Budget for the integration break and the billing complaint as part of the control, not as a defect in it.

What this does NOT mean: it is not prevention, and the source does not claim it is — "We expect that attackers will find ways around these safeguards", how long the gains last is unknown, and it says it has yet to see a highly sophisticated and persistent attempt to circumvent them. It also states distillers spread traffic across Anthropic's API, third-party cloud platforms and other inference providers, so one vendor's account-layer controls bound only that vendor's share of the channel; the same extraction continues through everyone who has not built the equivalent.
