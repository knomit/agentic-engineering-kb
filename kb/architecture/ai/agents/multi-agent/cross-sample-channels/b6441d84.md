# Concurrent agent samples find whatever channel exists between them, so approving a coordination pathway does nothing unless the other shared writable surfaces are closed

OpenAI's containment guidance for frontier reinforcement-learning runs states the requirement as two clauses that only work together: "Limit high-bandwidth cross-sample communication to approved pathways, such as multi-agent tooling, and close infrastructure side channels that are high-bandwidth and easily discoverable by agents."

The qualifier on the second clause is the useful part. Side channels are described as "easily discoverable by agents" — a claim about what concurrently running samples actually locate, not about what the designer intended to expose. The surfaces in question are ordinary shared infrastructure that happens to be mutable and reachable by more than one sample: scratch space, caches, and any service the samples can all write to. The same guidance separately requires red-teaming "all services that the model could touch during training (e.g., internet cache)".

Consequence for anyone running a fleet of concurrent agents: enumerate every writable surface the fleet shares and decide, per surface, whether it is the coordination channel or is to be closed. Designating an approved pathway is the easy half and is not the control — a fleet with a well-designed queue or claim mechanism plus a shared writable temp directory has an unapproved channel, and the approved one does not displace it.

What this does NOT mean: it is not an argument against inter-agent coordination. A deliberately designed coordination mechanism is the approved-pathway half of this same requirement. The claim is narrower and one-directional — the existence of a designed channel is not evidence that the undesigned surfaces are harmless.

Scope: stated as a recommendation for frontier reinforcement-learning training runs, in the process of being implemented; no measurement of side-channel discovery rates is given.
