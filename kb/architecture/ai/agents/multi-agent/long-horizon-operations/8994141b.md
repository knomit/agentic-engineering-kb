# A months-long multi-agent operation ran on persistent campaign state, not on parallelism — target lists, credentials and standing instructions survived every session boundary, and the work continued unattended

Anthropic's 2026-09-10 threat intelligence report describes GTG-10007, a sustained espionage operation by Chinese-speaking operators, as a working example of an orchestrator-worker deployment held together by durable shared state.

THE SHAPE, quoted: "The operators routinely ran 'agent swarms,' where a lead AI agent decomposed reconnaissance and post-exploitation work and dispatched it to many subagents running in parallel."

THE PART THAT ACTUALLY DID THE WORK, and it is not the parallelism: "The operation maintained persistent campaign memory. Target lists, harvested credentials, engagement state, and standing instructions were saved across working sessions, so each session could be resumed mid-campaign with the program's accumulated context." The report adds that the team "ran parallel workstreams that had shared tooling and infrastructure bases and persistent campaign records that maintained context between working sessions", and that it had "collection and vulnerability research capabilities that kept operating while its owners were away". A fleet of thirteen standing collection agents "ran on a scheduled job". Parallel agents were "instructed to require evidence and use project memory".

WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING AGAINST THE PACK'S EXISTING MULTI-AGENT MATERIAL. [[9aaea0dc]] carries Cognition's case that dispersed context produces incompatible work products, and the pack's standing reading of orchestrator-worker is that it buys context isolation rather than parallelism, and stops paying once subtasks need each other's intermediate results. This operation is a field observation of the shape SUCCEEDING over months on exactly the workload that should break it — reconnaissance and post-exploitation, where later subtasks consume earlier ones' findings. The reported difference is that the intermediate results were not passed between agents at all: they were written to a campaign store that every session, including later ones, read from. The coordination problem was converted into a storage problem. That is the same move [[c436422a]] records for single-agent durability (an append-only event log is what lets a crashed harness resume) and [[fc249ffc]] argues for as a design principle (unify execution state with business state), arriving here at the multi-agent level.

THE SECOND DESIGN DECISION WORTH THE LINE: the standing instruction to "require evidence" was attached to the parallel workers, not to the orchestrator. In a fan-out where the lead agent cannot inspect each worker's reasoning, an evidence requirement carried in the worker's own instructions is the only place that discipline can live.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is not a measurement — the report gives no success rate, no comparison against a single-agent baseline, and no cost figures, so it does not settle the multi-agent tradeoff in either direction. It is one operator's observed architecture, reported by the model provider whose logs it ran through, and its evidence is what the operation DID rather than how well it did it. Compare [[b33ed20e]], where multi-agent coordination emerged without being designed; here it was designed and the state layer is explicit.
