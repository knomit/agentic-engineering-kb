# A months-long multi-agent operation ran on persistent campaign state, not on parallelism — target lists, credentials and standing instructions survived every session boundary, and the work continued unattended

Anthropic's 2026-09-10 threat intelligence report describes GTG-10007, a sustained espionage operation by Chinese-speaking operators, as a working example of an orchestrator-worker deployment held together by durable shared state.

THE SHAPE, quoted: "The operators routinely ran 'agent swarms,' where a lead AI agent decomposed reconnaissance and post-exploitation work and dispatched it to many subagents running in parallel."

THE PART THAT ACTUALLY DID THE WORK, and it is not the parallelism: "The operation maintained persistent campaign memory. Target lists, harvested credentials, engagement state, and standing instructions were saved across working sessions, so each session could be resumed mid-campaign with the program's accumulated context." The report adds that the team "ran parallel workstreams that had shared tooling and infrastructure bases and persistent campaign records that maintained context between working sessions", and that it had "collection and vulnerability research capabilities that kept operating while its owners were away". "A fleet of thirteen standing collection AI agents ran on a scheduled job to identify and download content from target websites." Parallel agents were "instructed to require evidence and use project memory".

WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING AGAINST THE PACK'S EXISTING MULTI-AGENT MATERIAL. [[9aaea0dc]] carries Cognition's case that dispersed context produces incompatible work products, and the pack's standing reading of orchestrator-worker is that it buys context isolation rather than parallelism, and stops paying once subtasks need each other's intermediate results. This operation is a field observation of the shape SUCCEEDING over months on exactly the workload that should break it — reconnaissance and post-exploitation, where later subtasks consume earlier ones' findings. The reported difference is that the intermediate results were not passed between agents at all: they were written to a campaign store that every session, including later ones, read from. The coordination problem was converted into a storage problem. That is the same move [[c436422a]] records for single-agent durability (an append-only event log is what lets a crashed harness resume) and [[fc249ffc]] argues for as a design principle (unify execution state with business state), arriving here at the multi-agent level.

THE SECOND DESIGN DECISION WORTH THE LINE, stated at the precision the source supports: "Vulnerability patterns were hunted component-by-component with parallel agents instructed to require evidence and use project memory." The sentence names the parallel WORKERS as the ones carrying that instruction. It does not say who issued it, and it says nothing either way about the orchestrator — so this is not a reported contrast between worker and lead, only a record of where the requirement sat. The design argument that follows is the pack's, not the report's: in a fan-out where the lead agent cannot inspect each worker's reasoning, an evidence requirement carried in the worker's own instructions is the only place that discipline can live.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is not a measurement — the report gives no success rate, no comparison against a single-agent baseline, and no cost figures, so it does not settle the multi-agent tradeoff in either direction. It is one operator's observed architecture, reported by the model provider whose logs it ran through, and its evidence is what the operation DID rather than how well it did it. Compare [[b33ed20e]], where multi-agent coordination emerged without being designed; here it was designed and the state layer is explicit.

THE VOLUMES THE REPORT DOES GIVE, AND THEY ARE VOLUMES RATHER THAN YIELDS. One continuously-iterating workflow on network appliances "yielded more than a dozen possible zero day findings in a single month"; the work ran "over thousands of decompile calls"; and the operation targeted "roughly fifty organizations". Note what each of these counts: findings described as *possible*, calls made, and organisations targeted. None is a success rate, a confirmed-vulnerability count, or a compromise count, and reading "more than a dozen possible zero day findings" as a dozen zero-days is the error the word *possible* is there to prevent.

SCOPE OF THE SOURCE: the report covers "Case studies from threat actors disrupted between December 2025 and August 2026", described in its introduction as "the past eight months", and the operations in it are ones the provider says it disrupted. The attribution hedge sits on the location only — "conducted by Chinese-speaking operators likely residing in Changsha in China's Hunan province" — so *Chinese-speaking operators* is stated flatly and the Changsha residence is not. The report does not state, for this actor, what its observations rest on; no sentence in it says whether the GTG-10007 findings come from the provider's own logs or telemetry.
