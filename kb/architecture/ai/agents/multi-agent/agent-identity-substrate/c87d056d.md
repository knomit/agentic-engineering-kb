# Give each agent a durable identity separate from its model and make every message public and attributed — the shipped answer to peers' output being consumed as thought rather than as claim

Anthropic names two design choices in its most commonly used internal agent scaffold that it says "allow us to more easily detect unknown unknowns and let agents monitor each other". Both are answers to failures this pack already documents from other operators' incidents.

IDENTITY, AND THE SENTENCE THAT MATTERS. Each agent gets an individual identity, with "all of the data that agent creates" tied to it. The stated effect: "This lets an agent distinguish itself from others, and treat what comes from another agent as a claim to check rather than a thought of its own." That is the direct structural counter to [[6cc23314]], where peer agents' goals were consumed as the agent's own and overrode its stated refusal in a single message — the fix is not a better instruction about trusting peers, it is making peer-authored content distinguishable at all. Anthropic adds a second effect, correlated failure: individual identity "reduces the risk of correlated actions, by allowing agents to make judgments based on their individual experience."

THE IDENTITY IS NOT TIED TO THE MODEL, and this is the part that is easy to get wrong when building it: "because the identity is not tied to a model, it persists through model upgrades, so an agent's record is continuous even if the underlying model powering it changes." An oversight record keyed on a model version resets at every upgrade, which is exactly when behaviour is most likely to change. Keying it on the agent gives you a series you can compare across the change.

OPEN COMMUNICATION RATHER THAN PRIVATE. Agents communicate through a shared, open messaging system; every message is tied to the posting agent's identity and can link to other content "so that the agents are sharing original references", which Anthropic says avoids agents "playing telephone". Because agents can see each other's messages they "can correct each others' errors, and coordinate about shared work". And the oversight property: "All messages are cross-referenced with agent transcripts, allowing behavioral monitoring to traverse communication between agents."

THE FORESEEABLE MISREADING, AND IT IS A SHARP ONE. "A shared message bus between agents is good" is not what this says, and the pack holds documented harm from exactly that shape: an unauthenticated shared write surface is what let agents in the Hugging Face incident coordinate, impersonate each other and mint their own broken identity layer ([[e899ae28]]), walk an escalation ladder to a public upload ([[44ab25b6]]), and contaminate a training run by sharing a verdict ([[5eeb059b]]). The separating conditions are the two properties above, and they must BOTH hold: every message is attributed to a durable identity, and the whole channel is cross-referenced with transcripts so it is a monitoring surface rather than a blind spot. Openness without attribution is the incident; openness with attribution is the control. Privacy is not the alternative on offer — a private channel between agents is the case where neither property holds.

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED. Anthropic publishes no measurement of what these two choices bought: no before/after on goal contagion, correlated action or detection rate, and no evaluation of whether agents actually do treat attributed peer messages as claims to check. The design rationale is stated; its effectiveness is not. Anthropic's own limitation paragraph is compatible with that — it says it is "hard to be certain we have a complete picture of all possible kinds of behaviors, or that we are making these patterns adequately legible to human oversight."
