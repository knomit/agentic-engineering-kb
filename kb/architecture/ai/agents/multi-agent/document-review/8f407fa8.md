# Google's Deep Review partitions the ASSIGNMENT, not the context — every parallel verifier reads the whole document, and compute is budgeted per section by information density

Deep Review reviews a long technical document as a four-stage pipeline, and two of its choices cut against the default orchestrator-worker shape.

The stages: a segmenter agent splits the document into logical sections (introduction, methodology, experiments) with segments allowed to overlap and required to cover the whole input; an adaptive budgeting step measures information density and allocates more compute to dense sections such as proofs than to introductions or related work; specialised reasoning agents then verify each segment IN PARALLEL; and a synthesis agent merges the segment reports, runs cross-section consistency checks, reruns logic checks where needed, and performs external grounding through tool calls.

The first non-default choice: "Each agent sees the full document to track cross-references but concentrates on its assigned section." What is partitioned is the assignment and the compute, not the context. That is the opposite of the usual justification for workers — context isolation — and it is the design answer to a task whose subtask outputs must be mutually consistent: give every worker the whole input, then put the consistency check in a synthesis stage rather than hoping the parallel workers agree.

The second: compute per segment is a function of measured information density, not of segment length or count. A uniform per-section budget overspends on front matter and underspends on the sections where the errors are.

What this buys, per the source's own account of what a single long prompt does wrong: it stops the run "exhausting their token reasoning budget", stops cross-section dependencies being overlooked, and stops nuanced claims being accepted without close scrutiny. The authors claim the approach transfers to design docs, slide decks and legal or financial documents.

Scope, and what is NOT established. This is a design description with deployment scale but no accuracy measurement: the tool ran as the Paper Assistant Tool in pilots at STOC 2026, ICML 2026, NeurIPS 2026 and ICLR 2027 across more than 16,000 opted-in manuscripts, and feedback was private and decoupled from official reviews — but the post reports no accuracy, precision or error-rate figures, and no ablation showing that full-document context or density budgeting is what helped. Higher-level judgement is left to humans. Treat the shape as a credible pattern with a large deployment behind it, not as a measured improvement over a single long call.
