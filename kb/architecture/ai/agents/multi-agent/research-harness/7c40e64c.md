# A published research harness makes checking a separate session with an adversarial brief, not a step inside the session that produced the result

A first-person account of one months-long project, not a controlled comparison. Source modality matters here: the post reports what its author did and found useful, with no claim that the topology was measured against alternatives. Disclosure stated on the page: "During this project, Schwartz has been working as a visiting researcher at Anthropic." The harness is named in the post — "BootLoops functions as a kind of harness for the LLM" — and "It is also open-source, so it can be used with whatever model you like."

THE TOPOLOGY. Separate Claude Code sessions ran on cloud virtual machines wired to GitHub and Overleaf repositories, one session per project, above them "a master session that coordinates the others, allocates compute, and validates results". Background subagents ran the computations and stored intermediate results in markdown files. Further sessions were dedicated to writing, to tool development, and to "checking and rechecking results as an adversarial referee".

THE DISTINGUISHING DECISION, and the reason this is worth copying rather than a restatement of orchestrator-worker: verification is a different session with an adversarial brief, and validation also sits in the coordinator — not a step appended inside the session that produced the result. The producing session has already committed to its own framing, and the post's companion failure modes are exactly the ones a self-check misses: declaring victory early, and conclusions that are wrong while the calculations under them are right. A separate referee with a different context is the structural answer to that, and it is cheap to add because it needs no new tooling, only a session with a different instruction.

TWO SUPPORTING CHOICES. Intermediate results go to markdown files rather than staying in context, which is what makes the subagent work survivable — "compaction would often kick in, causing Claude to lose important context", so files must be consolidated regularly and the files, not the transcript, are the state. And the problem SELECTION was done to fit the topology: the work centred on exact calculations anyone can verify by rerunning the numerics, so that the referee has something decidable to check. Pick checkable problems and the adversarial session has teeth; pick open-ended ones and it has an opinion.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. The separation is not free of its own failure: the post is explicit that automated checks cannot be fully trusted, and the author still inspected plots himself and questioned qualitative claims such as "good agreement". The referee session reduces the self-review problem, it does not remove the human from the loop — and the projects were compute- and token-intensive, so the topology buys reliability with spend. Some of the work also hit safety classifiers, and subagents were used to contain the resulting shutdowns.
