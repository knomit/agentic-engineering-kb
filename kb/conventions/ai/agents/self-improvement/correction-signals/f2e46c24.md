# A field-level diff between what the agent predicted and what the human filed is not a failure label — four different causes produce the same diff, so without end-to-end provenance the correction stream cannot be turned into evals

In OpenAI and Thrive Holdings' Tax AI deployment with the Crete accounting network, practitioners corrected the agent's drafted returns, but the correction by itself was not a usable training or eval signal. The post states that "a changed value before filing might reflect a true extraction miss, a mapping problem, missing product support" "or expected workflow noise." Four distinct causes, one observable diff.

The operational consequence is that the pipeline has to be instrumented BEFORE the correction stream is worth anything: capture the whole path from the source documents through the extracted fields and their provenance to the submitted return and the expert's correction. Only then can field-level review rows — predicted value against filed value — be grouped into recurring product failures as against expected noise, and only the recurring, actionable groups become evals carrying representative source packages and expected outputs.

Among the named causes of a diff is the measuring apparatus itself: one of the root causes to check is "if expected workflow noise is being counted as a failure" — a grader defect, not a product defect. A correction-driven loop therefore has to be able to conclude that the grader was wrong, or it will spend engineering effort on phantom regressions.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not mean collecting more human corrections improves the agent. The volume of corrections is not the bottleneck; attributability is. A high-volume correction stream with no provenance yields the same four-way ambiguity on every row, and the post's own account is that early on "most corrections were manual" and engineers had to follow up case by case to sort the causes out. It also does not license routing every finding to the agent: the described loop sends ambiguous cases back to the product team rather than to Codex.
