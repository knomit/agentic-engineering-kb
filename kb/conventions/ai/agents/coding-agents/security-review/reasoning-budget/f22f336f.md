# Route an agent's next step by the evidence gap it closes, not by token cost — a proof of concept averaged $3.61 and 21.5 minutes across 182 confirmed crash findings, and filtering early to save tokens discards real bugs

In Microsoft's account of running frontier models for vulnerability research, the stated optimisation target is explicitly not cheapness: "But minimizing tokens alone is the wrong objective." The rule offered in its place is that each next action must close a NAMED uncertainty — which caller reaches this path, which build configuration compiles it, whether a reproducer runs, what the causal explanation is — and that where a question is answerable by execution, an executable check "can provide evidence that more prose cannot". Work is then routed by the open question rather than by a flat per-step budget.

The cost side is measured, which is what makes this a budget decision rather than a slogan. On the Linux kernel: "Across 182 confirmed crash findings, generating a PoC averaged $3.61 in model cost and 21.5 minutes." For automatic exploit generation used in local privilege-escalation testing, "For six selected cases" it "averaged $8.56 and 25.4 minutes". Both exclude initial screening, failed or filtered candidates and human investigation, and the work used GPT-5.5 without extensive tuning — so these are the cost of the SUCCESSFUL TAIL, not the cost per candidate entering the pipeline, and they are outcomes rather than a controlled comparison against a cheaper configuration.

THE BOUNDARY CONDITION, from the same source: "Early filtering can also discard real bugs". A saving taken by cutting candidates early has to be measured against coverage and not only against spend, and cheaper-model routing and context reuse are to be treated as hypotheses to test rather than settled wins.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not that more reasoning is always better. The rule is symmetric — a step earns its tokens by naming the uncertainty it resolves, and a step that cannot name one is precisely the step to cut.
