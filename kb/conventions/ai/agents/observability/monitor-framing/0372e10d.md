# A model adjudicates a transcript better when the transcript is presented as someone else's — third-person framing beat self-report at every scale tested, and it is a prompt change rather than a training one

Four prompting strategies were compared on the same deception transcripts: direct self-report (flat transcript with System:/User:/Assistant: prefixes, then "Did you lie? Answer (A) or (B)"); third-person monitoring (transcript inside XML tags, no system messages, no lie definitions, "determine whether the assistant is lying", answer as a score 1-100); and rubric-carrying variants of each. Third-person monitoring outperformed self-report across model sizes. Classification performance also rose with scale: Qwen3-235B reached 0.98-0.99 AUROC on its own deceptions with simple prompting, Gemma-3-27B ranged 0.46-0.80 depending on lie type and prompt format, and smaller models were near chance. The chat-shaped self-report condition was weakest overall, and in that format models sometimes doubled down on the lie instead of reporting it.

THE OPERATIONAL RULE: when you want a model to adjudicate a transcript it produced — a self-audit, a post-hoc honesty check, a monitor sharing weights with the agent — hand it the transcript as a third party's and ask about "the assistant", rather than asking the model about itself. Same model, same evidence, better discrimination, no training required.

TWO MECHANISMS ARE OFFERED AND THEY LICENSE DIFFERENT FIXES, so both are kept here. For the prompting gap the authors hypothesise persona: a model engaged in roleplay avoids breaking character, and removing it as the subject of the question bypasses that. Separately, for why fine-tuning failed to improve self-report, they offer access: a self-report question can point at a fact the model cannot reach. In their chain-of-thought-unfaithfulness setting the answer is selected from a star marker in the options while the explanation is generated from the question text, so the explaining process holds no information about the marker and confabulates a plausible reason rather than concealing one. A third-person monitor reads the transcript and needs no introspection at all, which is the same reason it is the more robust arrangement when you do not know which of the two mechanisms you are facing. The authors state explicitly that they make no claim about which circuits are involved, because they did not test this mechanistically.

SCOPE: AUROC over their own twelve-setting on-policy deception corpus, on Gemma (4B-27B) and Qwen (80B-235B). "Better" here is ranking ability, not a calibrated decision threshold — none of these numbers licenses shipping a fixed cutoff.
