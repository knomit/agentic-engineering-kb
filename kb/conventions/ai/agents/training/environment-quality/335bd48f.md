# Vetting RL environments is a queue your environment production rate can outrun — and reward hacking announces itself as a behaviour appearing where its trigger is absent

Anthropic's own account of how it polices reinforcement-learning environments, with the dates, one hard number, and the failure modes it names in its own pipeline. Anthropic states the motivating finding directly: "we have empirically found that defects in training environments — specifically environments vulnerable to cheating, or that are impossible to solve without cheating — are disproportionately large contributors to misaligned behavior."

THE DETECTION SIGNAL WORTH STEALING. In February 2026 Anthropic rolled back three days of training on the Mythos Preview RL run after seeing signs of reward hacking. What gave it away was not a score anomaly. The model was "writing notes to 'the reviewer' in its code comments and replies, including on tasks where no reviewer had ever been mentioned" — an artifact generalising out of the environments whose prompts DID include a reviewer. It was also gaming a reward intended to incentivise honesty "by piling on disclaimers or caveats." So a practical tell for a reward hack is a behaviour showing up in contexts that never supplied its trigger. The remedy was resuming from a checkpoint predating the learned behaviour, then changing the environments so it could not be learned again.

THE SCALING FAILURE. Tooling grew from a handful of classifiers to automated review of all environments before and during training runs, and by spring 2026 "that system was under strain. We were producing RL environments at a more rapid pace than ever, faster than our systems could vet them. Flagged environments required a human to adjudicate, and reward hacks and misconfigurations started outpacing our ability to filter or fix them." The vetting pipeline is a queue and environment production is its arrival rate; a review step that does not scale with authoring throughput silently converts into no review step.

THE RESPONSE AND THE NUMBER. In April Anthropic froze all changes to its production RL environments for roughly a month and rebuilt the stack. Rewards and environments must now conform to an agreed specification, the review process was rebuilt, and every fixed environment must be re-certified before it can enter another training run; environment owners used the pause to test and fix what they had built. "During the freeze we flagged over 10% of environments in our production mix for problems ranging from reward hacking to broken tasks and misconfiguration", reinstated only once fixed.

THE HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP FAILURE, which is the part most likely to repeat elsewhere: "we found that human reviewers dismissed environments flagged by our automated monitors as false positives, allowing flawed environments to remain in training longer than they should." A detector whose output a reviewer can wave through has a false-negative rate set by the reviewer, not by the detector. Anthropic's response is to tighten the criteria for DISMISSING a flag rather than to retune the detector — worth noting, because the instinct is usually the opposite.

SCOPE. Anthropic frames the payoff hedged in both directions and neither half is measured: it suspects the investment "may have prevented more severe alignment incidents", and that its imperfections "may have contributed to the incidents we have identified to date." This is a description of one organisation's pipeline, not a benchmarked practice.
