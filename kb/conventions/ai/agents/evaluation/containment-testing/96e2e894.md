# To test whether containment holds, put the flag in the ring between the inner and outer boundary — that makes escape an artifact the agent submits rather than an event you hope to notice

UK AISI publishes the design of the controlled-escape exercises it uses to validate its own evaluation environments: "instructing an agent with a large token budget to try to break out of a compute environment", "in settings where real-world risk is strictly reduced (for example by using simulated environments or nested sandboxing)".

THE LOAD-BEARING CHOICE IS WHERE THE SUCCESS CRITERION LIVES: "Instructing the model to find and submit a target 'flag' that is placed outside the target environment, though still within an outer layer of containment". The flag sits in the gap between the boundary under test and the boundary that is not under test. A successful escape therefore produces a submitted string — positively observable, attributable to a run, and checkable without trusting the monitor — while the escape itself never leaves the outer sandbox. Without that construction, "did containment hold?" is answered by an absence of evidence, which is the same observation you get from an environment where the control silently was not in place at all.

The rest of the published protocol, which is what keeps the exercise from being the incident it simulates: a static phase "using frontier models to find issues in our codebase" and a dynamic phase of "human researchers working with AI advisors to probe a compute environment" before an agent is turned loose; "We begin with less capable models and progress to more capable ones"; "A synchronous monitor that halts the exercise on a successful escape"; "Full isolation from production systems, with an additional cloud security group around the sandbox"; "automated network monitoring, active human supervision, and manual kill-switches"; and "Human expert review of agent runs and findings".

AISI states one further precondition, which closes the gap the flag design exposes: "Adding automated checks before an evaluation begins to confirm that key controls are in place". An evaluation that runs with a control absent produces results indistinguishable from a compliant run, so control presence belongs in the start condition rather than in a post-hoc audit.

WHAT A RESULT FROM THIS EXERCISE DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH. A flag recovered is evidence the inner boundary failed. A flag NOT recovered bounds this model, this token budget and this environment, and nothing further — escape success is known to scale log-linearly with the token budget granted ([[84a43a60]]), so a negative result is a function of the budget chosen rather than a property of the sandbox. AISI says as much about its controls generally: "These controls reduce risk, but they do not eliminate it", and "Security measures that are sufficient for today's models may not hold for the next generation".
