# Three eval levels by cost, and build your own trace viewer before anything else

Hamel Husain's structure: Level 1 unit-test-style assertions, cheap and run on every change; Level 2 human and model evaluation on a set cadence; Level 3 A/B testing with real users, reserved for significant product changes. Cost runs Level 3 > Level 2 > Level 1, and that ordering is what dictates cadence — not how much you trust each.

The specific, frequently-skipped recommendation: build your own data viewing and labelling tool rather than relying only on a generic trace platform. The reason is that useful review needs the trace joined to your domain context — CRM records, user history, whatever the judgement actually depends on — and a general-purpose viewer cannot join those. He reports such tools take less than a day with Shiny or Streamlit. Friction in looking at data is the binding constraint on eval quality, so removing it is the highest-return hour available.

Two related claims worth carrying:
- Fine-tuning teaches syntax, style, and rules; RAG supplies knowledge and current information. Choosing fine-tuning to inject facts is the common miscategorisation.
- 99% of the labour in fine-tuning is assembling high-quality data. Budget accordingly — if a plan treats the training run as the hard part, it is mis-scoped.
