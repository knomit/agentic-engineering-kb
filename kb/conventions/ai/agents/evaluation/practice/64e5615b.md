# Three eval levels by cost, and build your own data viewing and labelling tool instead of buying a generic trace platform

Hamel Husain's structure: "Level 1: Unit Tests", assertion-style and cheap enough to run on every change; "Level 2: Model & Human Eval (this includes debugging)", on a set cadence; Level 3 A/B testing with real users, reserved for significant product changes. Cost runs Level 3 > Level 2 > Level 1, and that ordering is what dictates cadence — not how much you trust each.

The specific, frequently-skipped recommendation: build your own data viewing and labelling tool rather than relying only on a generic trace platform. The reason is that useful review needs the trace joined to your domain context — CRM records, user history, whatever the judgement actually depends on — and a general-purpose viewer cannot join those. He reports such tools "can be built with lightweight front-end frameworks like Gradio, Streamlit, Panel, or Shiny in less than a day". The accompanying instruction is about procurement order rather than project order — "Keep it simple. Don't buy fancy LLM tools. Use what you have first." — so this is a preference for building over buying, not a claim that the viewer precedes the rest of an eval programme. Pack analysis: friction in looking at data is plausibly the binding constraint on eval quality, which is why the build-versus-buy answer here differs from the usual one.

Two related claims worth carrying:
- Fine-tuning teaches syntax, style, and rules; RAG supplies knowledge and current information. Choosing fine-tuning to inject facts is the common miscategorisation.
- "99% of the labor involved with fine-tuning is assembling high-quality data that covers your AI product's surface area." Budget accordingly — if a plan treats the training run as the hard part, it is mis-scoped.
