# Critique shadowing: bind an LLM judge to ONE domain expert using binary pass/fail plus written critiques

Hamel Husain's method for building a judge that means anything. The steps, with his specifics:

1. Identify a single principal domain expert — not a committee. Multiple evaluators without consensus produce misaligned standards, and this person's judgement is the ground truth the whole system is calibrated to.
2. Have them grade roughly 30 examples, and keep going until no NEW failure modes appear rather than to a fixed count. Each grade is binary pass/fail plus a written critique explaining why.
3. Build the judge prompt using those critiques verbatim as few-shot examples.
4. Measure judge-vs-expert agreement with precision and recall, NOT raw accuracy — accuracy is misleading on imbalanced pass/fail data. Iterate to >90% agreement; he reports 2-3 iterations typically suffice.
5. Then do error analysis: compute error rates across features, scenarios, and personas, and classify traces by root cause to decide what to fix.

The anti-pattern he attacks hardest is the 1-5 Likert dashboard across many dimensions. It is not actionable — nobody knows what to do with a 3 versus a 4 — and it manufactures false precision while hiding what actually matters. Binary plus critique forces the disagreement into the open.

The standing warning: "You can never completely eliminate looking at your data" — the judge automates repetition of a judgement a human has already made explicit, it does not replace forming one.
