# Critique shadowing: bind an LLM judge to ONE domain expert using binary pass/fail plus written critiques

Hamel Husain's method for building a judge that means anything. The steps, with his specifics:

1. Identify a single principal domain expert — not a committee. Multiple evaluators without consensus produce misaligned standards, and this person's judgement is the ground truth the whole system is calibrated to.
2. Have them grade examples binary pass/fail plus a written critique explaining why. His stopping rule is stated as a heuristic, not a count: "My heuristic is that I start with around 30 examples and keep going until I do not see any new failure modes." The 30 is a starting point; saturation of failure modes is what actually ends the pass.
3. Build the judge prompt using those critiques verbatim as few-shot examples. The critique must be "detailed enough so that you can use it in a few-shot prompt for a LLM judge" — that requirement is what makes the critique format load-bearing rather than decorative.
4. Measure judge-vs-expert agreement with PRECISION AND RECALL SEPARATELY. In his words, "using raw agreement is generally not recommended and can be misleading when classes are imbalanced. Instead, you should typically measure precision and recall separately."
5. Then do error analysis: compute error rates across features, scenarios, and personas, and classify traces by root cause to decide what to fix. Build specialised judges only where the error analysis shows they are needed.

The anti-pattern he attacks hardest is the 1-5 Likert dashboard across many dimensions. It is not actionable — nobody knows what to do with a 3 versus a 4 — and it manufactures false precision while hiding what actually matters. Binary plus critique forces the disagreement into the open.

The standing warning, verbatim: "However, you can never completely eliminate looking at your data! This is precisely the step that most people skip." The judge automates repetition of a judgement a human has already made explicit; it does not replace forming one.

STALENESS CHECK 2026-08-09 — SOURCE LIVE (published 2024-10-29, no update notice). Claims re-verified; TWO CITATION-FIDELITY DEFECTS CORRECTED, both of the kinds this pack keeps finding:
  (1) WRONG TERM ATTRIBUTED. The fact said to prefer precision and recall over "raw accuracy", and called accuracy "misleading on imbalanced pass/fail data". Husain's term is raw AGREEMENT, not accuracy. Close enough to look harmless, and wrong in the way that makes a fact unsearchable against its own source.
  (2) A SINGLE REPORTED INSTANCE GENERALISED INTO A RULE. The fact said "Iterate to >90% agreement; he reports 2-3 iterations typically suffice." He reports ONE case — "It took us only three iterations to achieve > 90% agreement between the LLM and Phillip" — and explicitly declines to make it a target, adding "Your mileage may vary". Neither "2-3" nor "typically suffice" nor a >90% target is his. Removed rather than rewritten: there is no published target to quote, and inventing one turns an anecdote into an acceptance criterion someone will hold a team to.
