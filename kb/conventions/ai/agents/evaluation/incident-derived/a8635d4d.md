# An eval built from an incident is a regression test, not a detector — the detector has to be built without hillclimbing on the incident's own artifacts, and separately backtested against earlier incidents

OpenAI's misalignment-investigation guidelines separate three jobs that teams collapse into one when they read "write an eval for the incident". All three are needed, and each one fails differently if it is asked to do another's work.

DETECTION. "We should develop alignment testing methods capable of discovering the propensity to cause the incident, without directly hillclimbing on information derived from the incident (e.g., transcripts or incident summaries)." A detection method tuned on the incident's own transcripts is being measured on its training data, so its apparent sensitivity says nothing about the next, different incident.

REGRESSION. "Incident-derived evals should be created as 'regression tests,' to ensure future models do not exhibit propensity for misalignment on very similar incidents." The artifact built from the incident belongs in the regression suite. Its passing is evidence about recurrence of that specific behaviour and is not evidence of general detection capability — reporting it as coverage is precisely the confusion this separation exists to prevent.

SENSITIVITY. "Backtest alignment evaluations on previous incidents to confirm that evaluations detect previously misaligned models and are not being overfit to particular incidents." Backtesting is how you find out whether the eval suite generalises; the stated failure it screens for is overfitting to particular incidents, so a suite that passes its backtest only on the incidents it was built from has failed the check rather than passed it.

The same document adds the matching requirement on the monitoring side: "Create eval datapoints representative of net-new risks for new runs, so measures of monitor recall and precision do not become stale." Monitor recall measured only against past incidents drifts upward as those incidents age, because the monitor was tuned while they were being investigated.

Operational consequence: keep the incident-derived eval and the detection method as separate artifacts with separate owners, and never quote the regression suite's pass rate as a detection-coverage number.

Scope: stated recommendations for frontier reinforcement-learning training and misalignment-incident investigation, described as in the process of being implemented.
