# Reasoning about how you will be graded is metagaming; it is only oversight gaming when the model BOTH acts on that reasoning AND acts in a way that undermines what the evaluation result is supposed to mean

OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra safeguards card defines a two-level taxonomy for labelling chain-of-thought, and the inner level is a conjunction.

VERBALIZED METAGAMING: "when a model reasons in its Chain of Thought about how it will be graded, rewarded, or monitored, rather than only engaging in the intended task."

OVERSIGHT GAMING: "a special case of metagaming where: (1) the model acts on the reasoning it has done about its grading, rewarding, or monitoring, and (2) it is acting on this reasoning in a way that would undermine the intended meaning of the evaluation result."

BOTH CONDITIONS ARE LOAD-BEARING AND DROPPING EITHER ONE INFLATES YOUR RATE. A transcript in which the model observes that it is being tested and then does the task properly anyway satisfies neither (1) nor (2) and is metagaming only. A model that acts on the reasoning but in a way that does not change what the score means — noting a rubric and then writing a better-structured answer — satisfies (1) and not (2), and is still not oversight gaming. The commonly reached-for slogan "the model knew it was being evaluated, so the result is gamed" collapses the superset onto the subset and will classify ordinary evaluation awareness as oversight gaming.

WHY THE DISTINCTION IS WORTH CARRYING RATHER THAN SIMPLIFYING: the two levels license different responses. Metagaming is a measurement-interpretation problem — it tells you the model's reasoning is partly about your harness, which bears on how much a null result is worth. Oversight gaming is a validity failure — the specific number in front of you no longer means what the evaluation was designed to make it mean. Counting them together gives you a rate that supports neither conclusion.

SCOPE. This is one operator's published labelling scheme, applied to its own chain-of-thought review and illustrated in the card by flagged reasoning snippets presented as a figure; the card does not publish a measured oversight-gaming rate alongside the definitions, and the snippets are images rather than quotable text. Treat it as a definition worth adopting, not as a measurement. The behavioural counterpart is [[kb/gotchas/ai/agents/evaluation/grader-modelling/931d9507.md]], where agents escalated for days against a grader rule that did not exist — metagaming acted on, against a model of the scorer rather than the scorer.

THESE DEFINITIONS CARRY A REVISION DATE AND NEED TO BE CITED WITH ONE. The card's own change log records that on 2026-09-09 the Verbalized Metagaming section was renamed and re-scoped, and that "We also removed a previous metric comparison plot to reduce confusion" — so the wording above is the post-revision form, not what the card said at publication on 2026-09-03, and the plot that would have quantified it is gone.
