# Ground a synthetic enterprise eval environment in O*NET, job postings and SEC filings — but its quality score measures resemblance to a real organisation, never whether a score obtained in it predicts production behaviour

The constraint that forces this approach, in Microsoft's own words: "But real enterprise data contains sensitive information that cannot be used for evaluation under any circumstances." An assistant that reads a customer's mail, calendar, documents and org chart cannot be evaluated on a customer's mail, calendar, documents and org chart.

HOW THE ENVIRONMENT IS BUILT, and the transferable part is the choice of grounding corpora. Microsoft Research's Industry Evaluation Data Generation Platform constructs whole simulated tenants — users, meetings, emails, documents and org structure, each environment holding hundreds of users with communication histories — and makes them time-evolving so that time-aware queries can be tested at all. Three named public sources supply the structure rather than an LLM inventing it: "O*NET data from the U.S. Department of Labor's occupational database"; "Online job postings provide realistic role titles, reporting hierarchies, and departmental compositions."; and "SEC filings inform organizational scale, business unit structures, and financial contexts." Three industry verticals were built: pharmaceuticals, capital markets, and IT/software. A six-stage pipeline then turns generic workplace tasks into industry-specific grounded queries and validates them.

Reported figures: "scores improved 39% compared to our initial generation approach"; "Some industry-specific environments reached quality scores above 0.9 out of 1.0"; dataset creation moved from a multi-day manual process to automated runs, with claimed savings of roughly $100,000 per evaluation dataset.

THE LIMIT, WHICH DECIDES WHAT THE SCORE IS WORTH. The quality framework scores four named things — "Structural fidelity", "Contextual relevance", "Behavioral realism", "Content safety" — and every one of them measures how closely the synthetic data RESEMBLES a real enterprise. None of them measures whether a result obtained in the synthetic environment predicts the system's behaviour on real customer data, and no such comparison is reported. The post's own closing questions are about extending the environments (assessing multi-turn agentic interactions, modelling organisational dynamics over months or years, building "evaluation frameworks for AI systems that take actions, not just generate text") rather than about validating transfer. Pack analysis: treat a resemblance score as a PRECONDITION for a useful eval and never as evidence that the eval is predictive — a 0.9 resemblance score and an unvalidated transfer claim are two different facts, and the second one is the one a release decision needs.

A READING TRAP IN THE SOURCE: it says the framework "measures quality across three dimensions" and then lists four. Do not cite a dimension count from it; cite the four named dimensions.

What this does not mean: not that the environments are poor or the figures wrong. When real data cannot be touched, resemblance is the right first thing to measure, and grounding org structure in occupational and filing data is a stronger starting point than a generic benchmark or an LLM's invented company.
