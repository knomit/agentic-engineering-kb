# Neither shortening the clock nor adding depth restored a human-vs-model gap in a hiring test — only moving the problem out of distribution did, and the intermediate attempts are documented failures

An engineer maintaining a performance-optimisation take-home (originally designed November 2023 with a 4-hour limit, later cut to 2 hours) documents what did and did not preserve a measurable human advantage as models improved. Scores are cycle counts, where fewer is better.

THREE FIXES THAT FAILED, each stated by the source as an attempt that did not work:

- SHORTENING THE TIME LIMIT. Cutting 4 hours to 2 did not help, because "Claude Opus 4.5 in a casual Claude Code session" reached 1790 cycles, "approximately matching the best human performance in 2 hours". A casual single session, not a tuned harness, matched the best human inside the limit.
- ADDING DEPTH. Where problems had "far more depth than anyone could explore in 4 hours", models still solved the immediately accessible portion faster than humans. Unbounded depth rewards whoever gets through the shallow part quickest.
- PICKING A HARDER WELL-KNOWN PROBLEM. Problems built on well-documented challenges — the source names data transposition and bank conflicts — saturate, because the training data contains worked examples.

THE ONE THING THAT WORKED was distribution novelty, and the author states the requirement directly: "I needed a problem where human reasoning could win over Claude's larger experience base: something sufficiently out of distribution." The successful redesign used a constrained puzzle-game instruction set, where humans outperformed through reasoning patterns with no training precedent.

HOW BAD THE BASELINE GOT: "over 50% of candidates would have been better off delegating to Claude Code entirely." An evaluation that would score higher if the candidate stopped working and delegated is measuring delegation, whatever it was designed to measure.

A TRAP IN CITING THIS SOURCE. It reports several cycle counts for the SAME model under different harnesses and time budgets — 2164 for Claude Opus 4 after many hours in the test-time compute harness (tested May 2025); 1790 for Claude Opus 4.5 in a casual Claude Code session; 1579 for Claude Opus 4.5 after 2 hours in the harness; 1548 for Claude Sonnet 4.5 after many more than 2 hours of test-time compute; 1487 for Claude Opus 4.5 after 11.5 hours in the harness; 1363 for Claude Opus 4.5 in an improved test-time compute harness after many hours. Do not quote any one of these as "Claude's score": the spread within a single model version exceeds the gap between versions, which is the configuration-dependence problem documented at [[kb/gotchas/ai/agents/evaluation/benchmarks/comparability/8193c07b]].

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: not that out-of-distribution problems make better evaluations in general. The source's other stated criteria pull the other way — representative of real work, high signal with a wide scoring distribution, no narrow domain knowledge required. Novelty is what buys discrimination against a model, and it costs representativeness. Scope: this is one engineer's hiring take-home, not a capability benchmark, and the numbers are that test's own.
