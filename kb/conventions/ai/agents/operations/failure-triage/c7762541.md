# Label every failure artifact in the population mechanically before forming a hypothesis — two failure populations mixed into one bug story make the evidence look self-contradictory, and the clustering is what separates them

OpenAI's investigation of a long-running crash in Rockset turned on a method rather than an insight. Two steps, in this order.

FIRST, the population came from the artifacts, not from the logs. The team states "We weren't able to construct a log query that didn't have both false positives and false negatives" — note both directions — so instead a script scanned every production core from the prior year, extracted register state, and "automatically labeled the crash as return-to-null, misaligned-stack, or other." The discriminator lived in the artifact's register state and did not survive into the log text, and logs could themselves be corrupted by the crash.

SECOND, the labels revealed two populations where the team had assumed one, and that assumption was what had stalled the work: "Because we had been mixing counterexamples from both bugs, we couldn't find a single coherent explanation." Once separated, each population's distribution identified it. The misaligned-stack crashes came from one region, had a clear start date, and clustered on a single physical host — denylisting that host ended them. The return-to-null crashes were spread across many clusters and regions with no clean start date, which is what marked them as a software race rather than bad hardware, and they ran at "more than a dozen return-to-null crashes per day across the fleet."

The operational consequence for agent fleets: when triaging a recurring agent failure, classify every captured trajectory or crash artifact by a mechanical discriminator first, then look at how each class distributes over host, region, version and start date. A clean start date plus concentration in one locus means an environmental or hardware cause; diffuse and undated means a latent defect in the code path.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it does not mean more logging would have solved it. The failure of the log query was not a volume problem — it was that text matching over log lines could not express the distinction, in either direction. It also does not mean the classifier must be correct to be useful: a third bucket, "other", carries the cases the discriminator cannot place, and keeping it explicit is what stops a partial classifier from silently re-mixing the populations.
