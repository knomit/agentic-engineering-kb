# In OpenAI's worked agent-cost reduction, prompt, output, tool and context controls delivered 87 of the 95 percentage points, and routing, caching and workflow-splitting added 8 between them

OpenAI's cost-and-quality optimization cookbook runs one support agent through four rounds in a fixed order and reports cost per ticket at each. The distribution of the savings is the finding, and it is lopsided:

- Baseline, one large model for all steps: $0.03813 per ticket, p50 latency 4.88s, mean 11,935 total tokens, quality 0.51, policy compliance 10%.
- Round 1, prompt, output, tool and context controls: $0.00512 (87% below baseline), p50 2.32s, mean 1,379 tokens — and quality rose to 0.98 with policy compliance at 100% in the same round.
- Round 2, model routing (nano for triage and tags, mini for routine resolution, the full model for high-risk cases): $0.00302 (92%), p50 1.87s.
- Round 3, prompt caching: $0.00244 (94%), mean 1,779 cached tokens, p50 1.85s.
- Round 4, splitting real-time from follow-up work: $0.00204 (95%), mean 2,412 synchronous tokens, p50 1.41s.

Projected at 100,000 tickets a month, that is $3,813 falling to $204. One component is costed directly: a verbose tool schema measured 550 tokens against 397 for a slim one.

So the unglamorous round — tightening the prompt, capping the output, cutting the tool surface and trimming tool payloads — carried 87 of the 95 points, and the three levers that attract attention added eight between them. It also improved quality rather than trading against it, which is the opposite of the usual assumption about cost work.

THE METHOD MATTERS AS MUCH AS THE ORDER. The page gates every round on quality before comparing savings: accept savings only when the agent still uses the right facts, follows policy, takes the required action and escalates correctly, and compare p50/p95 latency and total cost only after those checks pass. Its own checks are deterministic phrase and forbidden-claim tests plus an optional LLM judge over 50 simulated traces, and it warns that phrase checks can reject valid paraphrases and cannot establish factual correctness.

SCOPE, AND IT IS NARROW. These figures come from a dry-run simulation, and the page states its numbers are not a production benchmark. Routing in particular was not measured at all — routing and optimized actions come from the fixture labels — and the page says a production router needs separate evaluation and a fallback for low-confidence routing; for what it costs to actually measure one, see [[766706aa]]. The caching round rests on a simplified warm-cache assumption and a 1,024-token eligibility threshold. Treat the ORDER as the transferable claim and the percentages as one worked example.
