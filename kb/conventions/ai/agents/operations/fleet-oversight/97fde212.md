# An operator published its agent-fleet oversight numbers: 100% of ~30,000 agents' actions monitored, 0.002% blocked online, 1-2 transcripts per 1000 flagged offline, and ~50 per week reaching a human

Anthropic published operating metrics for monitoring its own internal agent fleet, proposing three measures any developer could report: COVERAGE (share of an agent's actions that pass a monitor before or after execution), REVIEW LATENCY (time between an action and its review, first automated then human), and ESCALATION RATE (share blocked/redirected by online monitors, or flagged by offline ones).

THE SNAPSHOT, and every number here is scoped to Anthropic's most-used internal research-and-engineering platform in August 2026 — not to Anthropic as a whole and not to deployed products. Approximately 30,000 agents were doing research and engineering work on that platform at any one time.

ONLINE MONITORS. 100% of those agents' actions pass through before execution, "usually within seconds". Their stated purpose is harms "that would happen quickly and could not be undone, such as an agent copying model weights out of our systems" — they block or steer automatically. Humans review any blocked action within one week. Anthropic "analyzed over a billion decisions from our research & engineering agents over August 2026 and found that 0.002% of them (about 1 in 47,000) were blocked by this monitor."

OFFLINE MONITORS. 100% of actions are ingested after the fact. Their stated purpose is "dangerous behaviors that develop slowly and can be reversed, like signs of model misalignment"; the response to a large imminent danger would be to "shut down the dangerous parts of the agent's deployment". This monitor "flags roughly one to two transcripts in every thousand for further review" and "roughly 100,000 transcripts per week", which are then screened by multiple classifiers for false positives; "the highest priority flags (approximately ~50 per week) are escalated to human review."

THE DESIGN PRINCIPLE IS REVERSIBILITY AND SPEED, NOT SEVERITY. What decides whether a control sits inline or after the fact is whether the harm can be undone once it happens and how fast it lands — weight exfiltration goes online because it is irreversible and quick; misalignment goes offline because it accretes and can be rolled back. That is a cheaper sorting rule than trying to rank harms by badness, and it explains why a 100%-coverage offline monitor is not a control: it is a discovery mechanism, which is the same conclusion [[7aecb3c3]] reaches from OpenAI's 20%-sampled monitor by a different route.

COVERAGE IS NOT THE BOTTLENECK; TRIAGE IS, AND THIS IS THE OPERATIONAL PAYLOAD. Both monitors are at 100% coverage, so nothing is lost at ingestion. PACK ARITHMETIC over two published figures, marked as derived: ~50 escalations out of ~100,000 weekly flags is about 0.05% — roughly one flag in two thousand reaches a person, and the intervening reduction is done by more classifiers. So an oversight programme's real capacity question is not "what fraction of actions do we see" but "what fraction of flags can a human adjudicate, and what does the classifier cascade in between do to the ones that do not". Reporting coverage alone makes a programme look complete when the binding constraint is downstream of it. A reader should also not compress the two escalation figures together: 0.002% is the ONLINE block rate over a billion decisions, 1-2 per 1000 is the OFFLINE flag rate over transcripts, and they are different denominators for different mechanisms.

THE OPERATOR'S OWN CAVEAT ON THE RARITY, which is the reason to publish the numbers at all: "In our monitoring data to date, individual agents rarely misbehave. But when there are millions or billions of agents operating in the economy, even rare events can happen regularly." A per-action rate of 1 in 47,000 is a large absolute count at a billion decisions a month.

SCOPE AND MODALITY. These are self-reported measurements of the operator's own systems, published as prototypes for a reporting standard rather than as audited figures; Anthropic states METR "has independently red-teamed our offline monitoring platform" in the past and that it is "setting up external third party evaluators". Anthropic also says plainly that at this scale "it is hard to be certain we have a complete picture of all possible kinds of behaviors" and that it has "been doing this kind of monitoring for months, not years".
