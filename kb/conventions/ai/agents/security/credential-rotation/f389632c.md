# Rotating a stolen AI key does not end the compromise while the harvester is still on the device — rotation converts a permanent leak into a repeating one, and the clean rotation log reads as remediation

Anthropic's 2026-09-10 threat intelligence report describes a criminal AI supply chain built on fraudulent resellers, and records one design detail that defeats the standard incident response.

THE DELIVERY. Actors "stood up websites that purported to be an intermediary service between multiple AI models and offered discounted access to frontier AI models", and compromised visitors most persistently "by having the victims download and install malicious client side applications often spoofing as popular AI harnesses including Claude Code but were in fact credential harvesters that would gather all of the victim's credentials and authenticated session tokens on their device and send them to the attacker".

THE STEP THAT MATTERS FOR RESPONSE, quoted: "As the victim's API keys or account may be identified as compromised and reset, the credential harvester continued to identify any new sessions on the device and sent them to the actor." The harvester is resident and anticipates rotation. So rotating the key performs the visible half of remediation — the leaked credential is dead, the rotation is logged, the incident looks closed — while restoring the exact precondition the attacker needs, and the newly issued credential is exfiltrated as soon as it is created.

THE RULE, AND BOTH HALVES ARE REQUIRED: treat a leaked AI credential as evidence about the ENDPOINT as well as about the credential, and do not close on rotation alone — establish how the key left before issuing its replacement, and issue the replacement only to a host you have cleared. Where that is not possible, the replacement has to be assumed compromised from the moment of issue. This is the credential-side companion to [[6b8f82c2]], where the response that did work was explicitly broader than rotation: the instance stopped and imaged, the laptop imaged and wiped, an external consultant running an independent compromise assessment, with rotation as one item among them rather than the conclusion.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It is not an argument against rotating; rotation is still necessary and still kills the specific stolen credential. It means rotation is not SUFFICIENT, and that its success signal is actively misleading — a working new key and a quiet leak look identical from the provider's side, and the cover property of stolen AI credentials means continued misuse stays attributed to the legitimate owner throughout.

THE REPORT'S OWN GUIDANCE, which generalises past this case: "Organizations should treat AI keys and agent integrations with the same level of seriousness as they do production credentials—because attackers treat them with the same level of seriousness, too. AI access should be purchased only through authorized channels. An alleged discount that requires routing traffic and credentials through an unknown intermediary introduces tremendous risk to user data and systems." Note the scope is AI KEYS AND AGENT INTEGRATIONS TOGETHER — the report names "sandboxes, proxies, and resellers" as part of the attack surface alongside the keys themselves, so a rotation policy covering the secret but not the integrations that hold it is scoped too narrowly.
