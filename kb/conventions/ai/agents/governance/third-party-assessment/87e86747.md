# Pre-register the safety claims before an external assessment starts, label who proposed each one, and make the report state what it did not assess

Two terms, defined by the operator proposing the practice, and the distinction is load-bearing:

- A **safety claim** is a specific assertion about a model's or system's capabilities, behaviour or safeguards that bears on safety and can be assessed against evidence. A claim must identify the risks and conditions it addresses along with its assumptions and limitations.
- A **safety case** is a structured, evidence-supported argument that risks are adequately managed *for one specified activity* — training, evaluation, internal deployment, or external deployment — connecting individual claims to their evidence and making assumptions, uncertainties and residual risks explicit.

A case is scoped to an activity, not to a system. "Our safety case" with no activity named is not a case.

The rules that make an external assessment produce a usable report:

- **Scope is mutually agreed and the claims are pre-registered before assessment activities begin.** Selecting claims after the evidence is in is how a favourable finding becomes *the* finding.
- **Each claim is labelled by who proposed it** — a claim the lab put forward for assessment, or a claim the assessor chose independently and the lab agreed to be assessed against. The two carry different evidential weight, and a report that does not distinguish them cannot be read correctly.
- **A pre-agreed process covers significant risks found outside the original scope**, including whether they warrant further investigation. Without one, the scope agreement doubles as a gag.
- **Conclusions state what was and was not assessed** relative to the agreed scope. Claims can be legitimately out of scope for stated reasons — access infeasible for a third party, assessor expertise, time constraints when an assessment is urgent — and saying so is what stops silence reading as a pass.
- **Reports separate direct findings from interpretation**, and where a lab requests redaction of sensitive material the assessor notes that substantive redactions were made and their impact on the assessment.
- Access is proportionate to the claims being assessed, with "grey box" access named as the tier for adversarial safeguard testing, and indirect or privacy-preserving mechanisms where direct access is impossible.

**The foreseeable misreading: "a third party assessed it" is not itself a safety claim.** An assessment answers only the pre-registered claims for the named activity. Nothing about unexamined behaviour, other activities, or later model versions follows from it.

**Status.** This is one lab's stated position and proposed practice — published as priorities and principles, with the lab saying it is in conversation with assessors about proposals in these areas — not an audited standard, an established norm, or a description of assessments already conducted this way.
