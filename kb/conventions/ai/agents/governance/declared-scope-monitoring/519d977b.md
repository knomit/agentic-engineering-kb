# When a request cannot be judged benign or harmful on its own content, monitor it against a scope its holder declared in advance — and keep the declaration coarse enough that sharing it costs nothing

A provider opening a permissive access tier for dual-use biology work states the problem that rules out content-based classification: "In biology, where it's often not possible to differentiate between a user doing valid work (e.g. research a viral pathogen to develop vaccines against it) and pursuing harm (e.g. trying to increase the transmissibility of a virus maliciously), the most concerning threat models are ones where valid access has been diverted or overtaken by an actor with bad intent."

**THE CONTROL PATTERN THAT REPLACES CLASSIFICATION: compare traffic against a scope the access-holder declared, and alert on deviation rather than on content.** "Each entity's access is tied to the use cases it has specified in its grant applications, and we continuously monitor LSVP traffic to identify usage or patterns that are outside the stated safe scope. Should unauthorized activity occur, we can flag these cases to organization admins to take action within pre-agreed timeframes for triaging and remediating incidents."

THREE DESIGN CHOICES MAKE IT WORKABLE, and each is a decision another deployment can copy:

1. **THE DECLARATION IS DELIBERATELY COARSE.** "The use cases should include high-level descriptions of the intended work, like one would share in a job listing, and not include any sensitive information or IP." A scope statement detailed enough to be precise is one the holder cannot share; job-listing granularity is the level at which a declaration is both sharable and still discriminating. This is the hinge — a deviation monitor whose baseline nobody will write down does not exist.
2. **THE JUDGEMENT OF WHAT IS SAFE IS DELEGATED TO A VETTED HOLDER.** "Because we vet the LSVP organizations for their life sciences credibility and oversight, we can empower them to specify for themselves what constitutes safe usage for teams or projects within their program." Verification is up front — "a review of their research credentials, security standards, and ethical research oversight" — and is what buys the right to self-specify. The provider keeps detection; the holder keeps definition and first-line response, with pre-agreed remediation timeframes.
3. **THE GRANT IS SCOPED AND EXPIRES, WITH THE RISKIER TIER NARROWER AND SHORTER.** The routine tier covers a whole team "for diverse, daily workloads" and is "renewed once a year", with "refined classifiers that are more permissive for science tasks". The higher tier "removes all safeguards that block life sciences requests", "applies to a single research project as opposed to a full team, and must be renewed every six months" — the worked example being "characterizing how one specific family of viral vectors is recognized by human immune pathways". Blast radius and renewal period both tighten as permissiveness rises, and a researcher normally holds one broad grant plus one or more narrow ones rather than a single maximal grant.

**THE MISREADING TO BLOCK: this is not a global safeguard switch.** "All other safeguards, such as cyber classifiers, will remain in place under LSVP grants." The relaxation is scoped to one domain; everything outside it still enforces. Nor is the top tier freely available — for the most capable model family, high-risk grants "remain limited to a small set of entities with additional vetting", with a government in the loop.

THE THREAT MODEL IT IS AIMED AT, verbatim and worth keeping because the third entry puts agents beside insiders rather than in a category of their own: "Access compromise: Malware or account takeover diverting access to a bad actor"; "Insider threats: Rogue or coerced employees intentionally taking malicious action or diverting their access to a bad actor"; "Agent misuse: Agents, especially working in swarms or over long-horizon tasks, taking unintended dangerous actions." All three are cases where the credential is legitimate and the use is not, which is exactly the class content classification cannot reach and a declared-scope comparison can.

WHERE THIS SITS RELATIVE TO THE IDENTITY WORK IN [[069468bb]]: that fact records provenance and attestation as shipped and INTENT as asserted by nobody. This is the nearest thing in the corpus to an intent assertion, and it is instructive how weak a form it takes — not a cryptographic per-action claim but a coarse text declaration at organisation-or-project granularity, checked after the fact by monitoring for drift. Useful, and not the same thing as verifying that a particular action serves a stated purpose.

SCOPE AND MODALITY: one provider, one domain, launched in beta with "dozens of organizations" onboarded and hundreds expected, available on first-party API, Enterprise and Team plans and not on third-party platforms. This is a described design, not a measurement — no detection rate, no false-positive rate, no published case of the deviation monitor firing. Note also the retention it depends on (30 days, compartmentalised and stated to be unusable for training or by the provider's own life-sciences researchers), which is the same dependency the offline-correlation decision carries generally.
