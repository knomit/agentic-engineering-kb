# A RAG eval built from generated queries cannot detect context rot — generation puts every case in the high-similarity regime where degradation is smallest

Two measurements in this pack, taken by the same lab for different purposes, compose into a conclusion neither states: A RAG EVAL BUILT FROM NAIVELY GENERATED QUERIES IS STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE OF DETECTING CONTEXT ROT. Passing it tells you nothing about how your system behaves at length.

The two halves:

- CONTEXT ROT DEGRADES LEAST WHEN QUERY AND ANSWER SHARE VOCABULARY. Degradation scales INVERSELY with needle-question semantic similarity. This is why classic needle-in-a-haystack benchmarks, which use near-verbatim needles, flatter models badly — real queries share little vocabulary with the answer and degrade much faster.
- NAIVELY GENERATED QUERIES SIT AT HIGH SIMILARITY BY CONSTRUCTION. LLM-generated eval queries echo the source document's wording: naive queries averaged 0.716 query-document similarity against 0.628 for deliberately distinct generation, and 11.91% of naive query pairs sat above 0.9 cosine similarity to each other.

Compose them. Generating your eval queries from your documents places every test case in the HIGH-similarity regime — precisely the regime where long-context degradation is smallest. The eval is not merely optimistic about absolute scores, which is the known and already-documented problem; it is blind to a specific failure mode, and blind to it in a way that does not improve as you add test cases. You can grow such a suite indefinitely and it will never surface context rot, because every case it generates is drawn from the distribution where context rot does not bite.

WHY THIS COMPOUNDS RATHER THAN AVERAGING OUT. Long-context RAG failures are model-specific and qualitatively different, not just lower accuracy — each model breaks in its own way at its own threshold (measured saturation points ranged from ~4k for Mixtral-8x7b-Instruct to ~125k for GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet at the time of that study), and the failure TYPES include copyright refusal, instruction non-compliance, degenerate repetition and irrelevant content. So the eval you cannot trust for detection also cannot be used to infer your threshold or to predict which failure shape you will get. And if an LLM judge is scoring that suite, its own position, length and self-preference biases sit on top — uncancelled, they inflate the numbers further in the same direction.

WHAT TO DO:
1. Steer generation away from document vocabulary deliberately — the two-stage method is to filter the document set with an LLM judge aligned on relevance, completeness and intent, then generate with contextual steering plus example REAL queries so output matches the production phrasing distribution. Measured against Weights & Biases production data, context-guided generation reached KL divergence 0.159 against the true query distribution versus 0.207 for naive.
2. Test long-context behaviour with a SEPARATE instrument built for it — vary input length and needle position explicitly, include distractors (a single topically-related distractor drops performance below the needle-only baseline; four compound it), and do not expect your query-generated suite to cover this.
3. Prefer designs that keep the accumulated context small rather than designs that assume length is free. Training a search agent to PRUNE retrieved documents mid-search — discarding whole documents rather than summarising all of them — improved output recall 0.361 to 0.641 over its own base model, and preserves evidence verbatim where fidelity matters.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. Synthetic benchmarks are not useless: relative rankings of embedding models were CONSISTENT between ground-truth and generated queries, so a naive suite still works for CHOOSING between retrievers — just never for setting an expected production number or for clearing a system on long-context safety. Nor is this a claim that high query-document similarity is inherently wrong to test; it is a real slice of production traffic. The claim is that it must not be the ONLY slice, and naive generation makes it the only slice silently. Scope caveats carry from the sources: the context-rot study covers 18 named models with a stated method; the Databricks thresholds are mid-2024 models several generations old and should not be read as current for any specific model; and the generative-benchmarking validation rests on a single real dataset, does not capture queries with no matching document, and its LLM judges showed high sensitivity to minor prompt changes.
