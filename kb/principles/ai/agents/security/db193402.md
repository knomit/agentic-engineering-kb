# Every agentic security artifact states its own limit, and the limit is always the same shape — it tells you RELEVANCE, and you will read it as SUFFICIENCY

Six OWASP artifacts in this pack — a control crosswalk, a gap analysis, a threat taxonomy, a threat-modelling method, a top-ten ranking, and an incident corpus — each carry an explicit statement of what they are NOT. The statements are in the documents, usually in one sentence, and they are the part readers skip. Put together they describe one recurring failure: **these artifacts produce a coverage or priority SIGNAL, and teams consume it as a coverage or priority ANSWER.** Each of the six fails in its own way, so the check is per-artifact rather than general.

**1. A BROAD REQUIREMENT IS AN AGGREGATE, AND SATISFYING ONE PART READS AS SATISFYING ALL.** In the AIUC-1 crosswalk the two most broadly mapped requirements each cover 8 of 10 threats. The crosswalk attaches its own warning to the broadest: B006 "aggregates multiple distinct control functions (scope enforcement, tool-use restriction, privilege control, inter-agent constraints, and runtime containment)" and implementations "should address each control function independently rather than treating B006 as a single checkbox. **Coverage of one function does not imply coverage of others.**" The structural point generalises past this standard: the requirement mapping to the most threats is broad *because* it is vague, and breadth of mapping is what makes it look like the highest-value box to tick. A team implements scope enforcement, marks it satisfied, inherits a claimed mitigation against eight threat classes, and never builds runtime containment or inter-agent constraints. The audit passes; the coverage is fictional for six of the eight. **The rule: when one requirement maps to most of your threat model, that is a DECOMPOSITION SIGNAL, not a coverage win.**

**2. A MAPPING IDENTIFIES RELEVANCE, NOT SUFFICIENCY** — the crosswalk's own words. A Primary mapping means the requirement addresses the threat's core risk, NOT that the requirement as written provides complete mitigation. Implementation depth, testing coverage and operational maturity are separate evaluations the mapping does not perform.

**3. A SERIOUS STANDARD CAN OMIT WHOLE CONTROL SURFACES.** OWASP's crosswalk of AIUC-1 published **eight agentic areas the standard does not cover** that the ASI prevention guidelines treat as essential — four with no dedicated requirement at all (inter-agent communication security; agent identity attestation and containment; cascading-failure containment; agent tool-use infrastructure controls) and four needing expansion (supply-chain attestation; runtime agent security monitoring; resource and cost abuse; input/output schema controls and determinism). **Certifying against a standard is not evidence you cover the agentic threat model**, and this list is useful precisely because it is a peer-reviewed enumeration of what a serious standard forgot — which means your own checklist probably forgot it too.

**4. A TAXONOMY IS A CHECKLIST; ONLY A LAYER WALK IS A GENERATOR.** Applying MAESTRO's seven-layer-plus-cross-layer walk to one concrete system produced **thirteen threats (T26–T38) not in the ASI taxonomy at all**, because they were properties of that system rather than of agents in general. **If your threat model produced only the entries already on the published list, you performed a mapping exercise, not a threat model.** Two structural consequences: the same taxonomy entry recurs at several layers with different mechanisms and different mitigations, so a tracker giving each threat one owner closes it where it was easiest to see and leaves the other instances open; and cross-layer is a separate category precisely because a per-layer review cannot reach it, so budget an explicit cross-layer pass.

**5. A RANKING ENCODES BELIEF, WEIGHTED.** The LLM Top 10 2026 is 75% community vote and 25% incident data, and OWASP states both halves of the reason — "one noisy year of data does not get to overturn the judgment of the people doing the work", and "a quarter weight is enough to move an entry a tier when the gap between belief and evidence runs wide." So a rank is mostly the collective belief of practitioners, adjusted at the margin. That is a defensible construction and **it is not a frequency ranking.** Treat the ordering as a prioritisation aid, not a measurement.

**6. AN INCIDENT COUNT IS A LAGGING INDICATOR BIASED TWO WAYS AT ONCE** — by how hard a category is already being defended, and by whether failures in it produce the kind of artifact that lands in a vulnerability database at all. Prompt injection ranked #1 by practitioners and "falls out of the top 10 entirely" on raw incident record. **Do not prioritise your own security work by counting public incidents or CVEs per category.**

**THE OPERATIONAL RULE.** Before using any of these artifacts, find and read its own statement of limits — every one of the six has one, and it is short. Then convert the artifact into work rather than into a status: decompose broad requirements into their constituent functions and track each; treat a published gap list as a starting checklist for your own; run the layer walk against your actual architecture and expect it to produce entries the taxonomy lacks; and reserve the ranking for sequencing attention, never for claiming that a category is handled.

**WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN — carried from the sources, because each blocks a real misreading.**

- **Not an argument against using frameworks.** They are peer-reviewed, they encode work you would otherwise repeat, and the LLM Top 10's Appendix A carries coverage matrices to ASI 2026, DSGAI v1.0, MITRE ATLAS v2026.06, ATT&CK v19.1, CWE 4.20, NIST AI 600-1, NIST AI RMF, CSA AICM v1.1 and AIVSS v0.8 — real cross-framework mapping work that does not need redoing by hand.
- **Not a claim that a low incident count means a category is safe, nor that it means it is dangerous.** OWASP reads the prompt-injection gap as a defence effect; a second independent operator (OpenAI, November 2025) reported not yet seeing significant adoption by attackers, which corroborates the low COUNT but offers a different hypothesis about it. Both readings argue against deprioritising, for different reasons. Do not cite the operator observation as agreeing with the defence-effect explanation.
- **Not a claim that more detection substitutes for prevention.** The same crosswalk that makes logging the only control mapped to all ten threats also states when it is Primary versus Secondary: Primary only when the threat is persistent, cross-session or multi-agent AND operationally invisible without that specific detection mechanism. For the six Secondary threats, reaching for more logging instead of a preventive control is treating a symptom.
- **Source-strength varies across these six and matters.** The red-teaming landscape is a vendor document with gold and silver sponsors whose capability taxonomy doubles as a market map, and offers no measurements, thresholds or efficacy data anywhere; the governance survey's regulatory windows are reported second-hand rather than read from the instruments; and the identity-gap percentages are vendor-published. Read structure and stated limits with more confidence than any number.
