# A standalone function-calling loop cannot rank models for your product harness: the same plugin's pass rate moved +14 points for one model and −21 for another

A vendor-published plugin evaluation ran the same cases two ways — in a minimal standalone function-calling loop, and inside the product harness the plugin actually ships in (Codex) — and the harness-versus-standalone delta had **opposite signs for two models**: one model scored 0.88 under the harness against 0.74 standalone (+14 points), another 0.43 under the harness against 0.64 standalone (−21 points). Same plugin, same cases, same grader; only the surrounding loop differed.

The consequence is specific and it is not "the harness moves the score". It is that a cheap standalone eval cannot be used to CHOOSE a model for a harness, because the ordering it produces is not the ordering you will get — and the source's own instruction is to "Run the product harness for each new model instead of assuming the two approaches agree". A standalone loop remains useful for what it is good at: the source's three-level ladder puts direct tool tests first (free, deterministic, and a failure there "points at the resolver's own logic"), the standalone loop second at moderate cost and fidelity, and the product harness third at the highest fidelity "short of the ChatGPT desktop app itself". Use the cheap levels to localise defects, not to rank candidates.

The mechanism is visible in the same run. With no guidance from the plugin's server, Codex scored 3/5 on a five-case example and 5/5 with instructions, while the standalone loop scored 5/5 either way — so part of what a harness changes is how much the model relies on the tool's own prompting, which is a per-model property. A model that follows a terse tool description unaided is unaffected by a harness that adds noise; one that needs the guidance is helped or hurt by whether the harness preserves it.

WHAT TO MEASURE ALONGSIDE PASS RATE, because the same table shows why a single number hides the decision. Over 108 questions the production figures were 0.94 at 4.02 tool calls and 19 reasoning tokens per question, 0.94 at 3.74 and 14, and 0.88 at 5.54 and **1,229** reasoning tokens per question. The third model is a fraction worse on accuracy and roughly sixty times more expensive in thinking per question, and nothing in the pass rate says so. Track tool calls per question and reasoning tokens per question next to pass rate, and compare "task completion, input-token usage, and latency" on representative requests before fixing a default.

SCOPE, AND HOW THIS SITS AGAINST THE OPPOSING MEASUREMENT. [[8193c07b]] and [[c2624a17]] carry a result that looks contrary: comparing three mature PRODUCT harnesses at a fixed turn cap, scaffolding and tool-surface differences moved success rate only about ±2% on SWE-bench Lite and ±5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 while cost diverged roughly 2×. The condition separating the two is what is being compared. Product-harness against product-harness, at a matched budget, moves the bill more than the score; a BARE function-calling loop against a product harness is a different instrument altogether, and there the score moves by tens of points with a model-dependent sign. Do not generalise either result past its own comparison class.
