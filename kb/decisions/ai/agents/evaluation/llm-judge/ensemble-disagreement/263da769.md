# Running several judges over one transcript buys a triage signal, not a verdict — disagreement directs inspection, agreement establishes nothing, and majority-voting discards the usable half

The decision is what to do with an ensemble of LLM judges, or with repeated judgements from one judge, over the same agent transcript. Two credible sources push in the same direction from different evidence, and they decide it against the obvious design.

**AISI, shipping Transect (2026-10-07)**, states the asymmetry verbatim: "Agreement does not establish that a label is correct, but disagreement can help direct closer inspection." The tool is built to match — when several judges or repeated judgements are used, it *surfaces* the disagreements rather than reducing them away, and offers optional model review of uncertain labels.

**The separating condition, and why the obvious design is wrong.** A majority vote or a mean score consumes the disagreement to produce a single label, and that single label carries exactly the property AISI says it does not have: it looks like a verdict. The information the ensemble actually generated — *which items the judges split on* — is destroyed by the aggregation step. So the right output of running N judges is a ranked disagreement set routed to human review, and the wrong output is one confident label per item.

**Where agreement still earns its keep, and this is the condition that decides it.** Agreement is the right target when you are CALIBRATING a judge against a reference, not when you are VALIDATING a label. [[0cc69d27]] measures a judge matching human raters more often (59% exact) than humans matched each other (35%), which is why inter-rater agreement — not correctness — is the calibration ceiling. Read the two together: agreement is a meaningful *calibration* statistic across a labelled set, and not an *evidentiary* one for any individual label. Confusing the two is how a 90%-agreement headline becomes a claim that 90% of labels are right.

**Operational consequence.** Budget judge calls for coverage of the disagreement set, not for consensus on every item. Report the split rate alongside any rate you derive from judge labels. Never let an aggregation step sit between the judges and the reviewer without also storing the pre-aggregation judgements.

**What this does NOT mean.** It does not mean judge labels are unusable or that ensembles are a waste — AISI ships an ensemble deliberately. It also does not mean disagreement marks the *incorrect* items; it marks the *uncertain* ones, and the sources claim only that it directs attention, with no measured precision for disagreement as a defect detector.
