# Detecting misuse spread across sessions and accounts is a data-retention requirement, not a classifier-quality problem — one operator moved enforcement from per-request blocking to offline monitoring and made 30-day retention mandatory to pay for it

An operator states the structural limit of stateless monitoring in its own words, twice, in two separate product announcements, and in both cases it is the stated reason for a retention policy rather than an abstract observation.

ON WHY PER-INTERACTION ANALYSIS IS NOT ENOUGH: "because the most sophisticated misuse can involve many tasks spread across multiple sessions and accounts, it is not sufficient to run automated analysis on each interaction separately and then instantaneously discard the data. Effective detection requires storing data for a meaningful period of time so that it can be correlated across time and accounts. For this reason, we introduced 30-day data retention starting with Fable 5."

ON WHAT MOVING THE CONTROL COSTS: "Serious misuse is often spread across many requests and sessions to look disconnected and evade detection. ... we are shifting safeguards from real-time blocking, where we reject potentially harmful access at the time of each request, to offline monitoring, which allows us to more clearly identify potential misuse across patterns of behavior. Shifting enforcement from real-time blocking to offline monitoring allows legitimate work to proceed with fewer interruptions, but it requires us to retain data associated with flagged activity for review." The programme in question "requir[es] data retention for 30 days to be able to do this monitoring effectively."

*** THE DECISION THIS FRAMES, AND THE THING THE SLOGAN DROPS. *** Per-request blocking and cross-session detection are not two quality levels of one mechanism; they are different mechanisms with opposite dependencies. Blocking needs no history and prevents the single action it sees. Correlation prevents nothing in the moment and needs history to see anything at all. **So an architecture with no retention window has, by construction, no ability to detect a campaign — however good its classifier is — and "our monitoring is stateless and privacy-preserving" is a statement about which threats the system cannot see, not a statement about its strength.** Conversely, moving a control from inline to offline to reduce false-positive friction is not free: it converts a prevention capability into a detection capability and creates a retention requirement where there was none.

WHAT TO ASK, AND WHAT TO STATE. For any monitoring claim: over what window is data correlated, and across which identity boundaries (session, account, organisation)? A vendor that answers "we analyse every request" has answered a different question. And for anything built this way, the retention window is now a security parameter with a stated purpose, so it needs a stated purpose — the same post is at pains to say the retention "was not motivated by a desire to train on enterprise data", which is the inference customers drew.

THE THREAT THAT MOTIVATES IT, named: "Some of these instances involve theft or misappropriation of enterprise customers' credentials, which are difficult to detect without the ability to monitor traffic and detect abnormal behavior." Stolen legitimate credentials produce individually well-formed requests; only the pattern is anomalous.

RELATIONSHIP TO WHAT THE PACK ALREADY HOLDS. [[7502f0bb]] measures the attack side of this — a monitor scoped to one session caught 30% of attacks split across two sessions at a 1% false-positive threshold — and names correlating transcripts across sessions as the expensive remedy. This is the operator pricing that remedy: the cost is a retention window plus somewhere to put the data. [[97fde212]] gives the same operator's internal online/offline split, sorted by whether a harm is fast and irreversible; the second sorting axis appears here — whether the request can be judged at all on its own content, and what interrupting a legitimate one costs.

SCOPE AND MODALITY. Both statements are operator self-descriptions of policy and architecture, not measurements: no detection rate, no false-positive rate, and no evidence that 30 days is the right number rather than a chosen one. The enterprise product "will be rolling out to customers in phases", so its version of this is a stated design rather than a deployed one; the life-sciences programme is launched in beta with "dozens of organizations" onboarded, so its retention requirement is live. Neither post claims the correlation window catches anything in particular.
