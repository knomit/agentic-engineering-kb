# One prompt was hardened to survive full adversarial re-optimisation where another could not — the separating condition is whether the adversary chooses among several mechanisms or optimises within one, and the successful hardening needed an oracle the defender may not have

Two studies from the same research group reach opposite-looking conclusions about whether a single prompt can be made robust to an adversary, and the condition that separates them is worth having explicitly, because the wrong one generalises badly.

**"NO SINGLE PROMPT COVERS EVERYTHING."** Against an eleven-category monitor blind-spot benchmark, every targeted monitor prompt "improve[d] detection on the categories they target, but degrade[d] performance on at least one other", and the recommended direction was an ensemble of specialised prompts. See [[19218a92]].

**"A SIMPLE BLUE-TEAM PROMPT MODIFICATION CAN WITHSTAND STRONG ADVERSARIAL PRESSURE."** On the fuzzy task of scoring ML experiment proposals, an adversarial loop alternating blue-team prompt search with red-team attack search produced a weak-scorer prompt that then survived the full red-teaming process: "The subversion Pareto frontier on the figure above is essentially diagonal" — i.e. the red team could no longer depress true quality without also losing weak score.

*** THE SEPARATING CONDITION — and this is pack analysis over the two sources, not a claim either makes. *** In the monitor case the adversary PICKS AMONG ELEVEN DISTINCT MECHANISMS, and a prompt is a finite attention budget: spending it on state-tracking is spending it away from something else, so hardening is movement along a frontier. In the scorer case the adversary optimises WITHIN ONE mechanism — gaming a rubric on one task — so the hardening has no rival category to steal attention from, and the defence can close in on it. **The question to ask before budgeting for an ensemble is not "how strong is my adversary" but "how many distinct mechanisms can my adversary choose between". One mechanism admits a hardened prompt. Several do not.**

**AND THE HARDENING THAT WORKED REQUIRED AN ORACLE.** The blue-team search optimises the shape of the subversion frontier, which means scoring candidate defences against the ground-truth proxy. The authors state the limit twice: "our method for finding robust blue-team prompts requires access to the ground truth proxy scorer, which the blue team may not have in practice", and "in practice the blue team may struggle to discover sufficiently robust prompts when it only has limited information about the ground truth score." So the positive result is a proof that a robust prompt EXISTS in that setting, not a method a defender without a privileged scorer can run. Their own expectation is a mix of human and AI red-teaming against the reward model instead. This is the same shape as [[9b0c8c78]] from the other side: there, a robustness number measured against your own trained adversary bounds nothing; here, a robustness result obtained with privileged access does not transfer to a defender without it.

ONE FURTHER CONDITION THE STUDY ESTABLISHES, because it decides whether the attack exists at all: the successful attacks were ADAPTIVE. "Importantly, the attacks are also adaptive, i.e. discovered by the red team with the knowledge of the blue-team strategy (in our case, the rubric used by the weak scorer)", and the paper reports that "the adaptive nature of the red team is necessary for a successful attack." A rubric that the optimised model can see is part of the attack surface — which is [[931d9507]]'s finding about behaviour following the agent's model of the scorer, arriving in the design of the scorer rather than in an incident.

MODALITY: the scorer result rests on one task, one scorer pair, and prompt optimisation standing in for training — no model was fine-tuned. The monitor result rests on 40 synthetic transcripts across eleven categories on one monitor model. Neither publishes a robustness number for the other's setting, so the separating condition above is an explanation of two results, not a measured boundary.
