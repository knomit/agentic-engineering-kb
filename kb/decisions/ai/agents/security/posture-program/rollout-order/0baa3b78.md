# An AI data-security posture programme has four hard capability dependencies that fix the rollout order, and its published KPI targets are the document's own illustrative planning benchmarks — not measured industry baselines

An OWASP GenAI Security Project playbook lays out an AI data-security posture programme (AI-DSPM) as thirteen capabilities, and makes two claims that matter more than the capability list itself.

THE DEPENDENCIES ARE LOAD-BEARING, and the document says so in terms: "The dependency structure of §3.2 is not optional." Four chains are given: Inventory then Classification then DLP; Lineage then Erasure then Compliance; Integrity detection then Incident response; Observability posture then all detection. The practical instruction that follows is explicit — "Organizations should not attempt to deploy 13 capabilities in parallel." A DLP deployment without classification, or an erasure capability without lineage, is not early progress; it is a control with nothing underneath it. The suggested sequencing is days 0 to 90 foundation, 90 to 180 hardening, 180 to 365 maturity and resilience, and retrofitting the programme onto GenAI deployments already in production "should expect this sequence to take 12–18 months rather than 12 months". Operating tempo includes a "Quarterly: lineage and classification audit".

THE KPI TARGETS ARE NOT MEASUREMENTS, and this is the part most likely to be misused. The document's own conventions section states that its quantitative targets are "illustrative planning benchmarks drawn from practitioner experience and expert consensus, not measured population statistics", and that named vendors are "representative examples". The targets themselves, as published: asset inventory ">90% automated within 12 months"; classification ">95% within 12 months"; lineage ">80% within 12 months"; non-human-identity credential rotation "p95 ≤ 24 hours"; DLP "FPR <5%"; vector-store tenant scoping and at-rest encryption "100% within 6 months"; integrity-detection MTTD "<24 hours for high-confidence signals"; trace redaction "100% within 6 months"; connector governance "100% within 12 months"; lifecycle and erasure "≤ 30 days (GDPR ceiling)"; training privacy ">50% within 12 months for sensitive workloads"; resilience "100% annually"; shadow AI "remediation < 14 days"; model provenance "100% within 12 months"; non-human-identity least privilege ">90% within 12 months"; egress control "100% within 6 months".

SO: these numbers are usable as a target to argue about with a security team, and they are not usable as an industry baseline, a benchmark your programme is behind, or evidence of what peers achieve. Citing them as measured attainment rates misrepresents the source, which anticipates exactly that and calls itself a living document that should be checked against the project site before being cited in an audit.

ONE CITATION TRAP IN THIS DOCUMENT. Its regulatory dates do not agree with each other: a footnote gives high-risk obligations "from 2 Dec 2026" while two other sections give Article 10 obligations "from 2 December 2027". Do not take either date from this source without checking the regulation itself.
