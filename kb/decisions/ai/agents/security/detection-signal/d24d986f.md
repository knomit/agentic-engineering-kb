# Three measured agent intrusions put the detection signal in three different places — rate, sequence and timing — and the deciding condition is whether the agent already holds valid credentials

Three documented agentic intrusions, three different places where the automation gave itself away. The three are not in conflict; read together they say the signal moves with the attack phase, and the phase is decided by one thing — whether the agent has valid credentials yet.

**No credentials yet: RATE is the signal.** Storm-0940 driving ARTEX produced 151,000+ failed logons across 327 accounts in eight organisations, peaking at 2,900 failures per minute, with lockouts at nearly 98% of all activity and no successful authentication at all ([[f2df901b]]). No human campaign generates that profile. Rate thresholds and lockout-ratio alerting catch it cheaply.

**Credentials in hand: rate disappears and SEQUENCE is the signal.** In the same campaign, stolen multi-tenant application credentials produced 200 authorised sign-ins in under four minutes and privileged access in under three hours — authorised traffic, no failures, nothing for a volume threshold. In the Hugging Face agent intrusion, the activity was almost entirely reconnaissance and staging, and detection tuned to exfiltration volume saw 0.3% of it ([[56cefe53]]). What distinguishes these is the ORDER and BREADTH of authorised actions, not their count.

**Credentials in hand and acting destructively: TIMING is the signal.** In the Storm-3168 cloud-destruction case the automation stayed inside the identity's existing role assignments, so authorisation checks saw nothing wrong; what gave it away was two tokens live in the same 70 seconds ([[f4911519]]).

THE DECISION THIS BEARS ON, and the asymmetry that makes it uncomfortable. A detection programme has to choose where to put its effort, and the loud phase is the attractive place to put it: the signal is unmistakable, the alerts are easy to tune, and a dashboard of blocked sprays looks like coverage. In the one campaign where both phases are measured, the loud phase achieved nothing and the quiet phase reached privileged access. Volume-based detection on an agentic adversary can therefore be fully functional and still have bought no coverage of the path that succeeds.

What to do with that: treat rate-based rules as cheap and worth having, and treat them as covering only the pre-credential phase. The post-credential phase needs rules whose input is the shape of authorised activity — concurrent sessions on one identity, improbable action ordering, credential and privilege changes, breadth of read across resources — because by construction nothing there is unauthorised and nothing there is high-volume.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It is not a claim that one of these signals is better, and not a claim that agentic attacks are always loud first and quiet later — the ConnectWise-derived credential path did not depend on the spray succeeding, and the two ran as independent attempts by one actor. Nor is it an argument against rate limiting: in the measured case the lockouts are plausibly why the spray failed. The claim is narrower and only about where to LOOK: the three measured cases do not share a detection surface, so a programme grounded in any one of them has a documented blind spot.
