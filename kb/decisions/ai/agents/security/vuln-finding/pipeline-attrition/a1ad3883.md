# An agentic vulnerability-finding pipeline is bounded by human triage and duplicate suppression, not by detection — Anthropic triaged about 6,000 of over 29,000 candidates, and Microsoft reports that adding auditors can raise candidate volume without raising the rate of validated findings

Two operators of agentic vulnerability-discovery pipelines independently report the same binding constraint, and it is not the one the architecture discussion usually assumes.

ANTHROPIC'S OSS SCANNER: "We have discovered over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities" and "have only been able to manually review and triage approximately 6,000 of these"; "we have sent nearly 5,000 reports directly to maintainers". Precision is not what stopped the remainder. A validation exercise "check[ed] 97 critical and high-severity vulnerabilities from the scanner across 48 projects": "Of these, 85 (88%) met the bar for our CVD process", "11 were real but duplicated known issues or other findings from the scan", and "only one was invalid, i.e. a \"false positive.\"" So in that sample the attrition was almost entirely DUPLICATION and review capacity, not invalidity.

MICROSOFT'S FRONTIER AI VULNERABILITY RESEARCH states the same constraint as a scaling property: "Adding auditors can increase candidate volume without increasing the rate of validated findings", because duplicates consume the reviewer attention that is what converts a candidate into a shipped fix — so a larger search budget can lower useful throughput. Its remedies are to deduplicate BEFORE the expensive model steps (one internal project using deterministic AST-based methods "reduced about 45% duplicate findings across multiple scans of the same code"), to make the optimised unit a reproducible finding rather than a scan or a report, and to instrument the whole pipeline — arrivals, duplicates, rejections, queue age, time to fix. It also asks for structured reason codes on failed validations (unreachable path, missing precondition, insufficient attacker control, duplicate) so a rejection returns to the scanner as machine-usable evidence instead of dying in a ticket.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE: instrument and staff the review stage before buying more search. A pipeline that doubles finder throughput with no dedupe stage and no triage queue measured in reviewer-hours converts the increase into backlog, and its candidate count stops being evidence of anything. The metric to report is validated fixes weighed against both model cost and human review time.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It does not mean unreviewed scanner output is trustworthy: Anthropic states its reports are "fully model-generated, without human review or triage", warns they may be incorrect or invalid, and notes maintainers reporting inflated severity ratings and a misread threat model. The 88% figure is from a hand-checked sample of CRITICAL and HIGH severity findings across 48 projects, not from the 29,000 candidates, so it is not the scanner's overall precision. The 45% duplicate reduction is one internal project's result, not a general one.
