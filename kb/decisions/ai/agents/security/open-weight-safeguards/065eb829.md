# Stripping an open-weight model's refusals cost about 2,200 GPU hours and $4,400 — so an open-weight release's safeguards are a deployment convenience, never a control you can put in a threat model

Anthropic abliterated GLM-5.3 itself and published the price. "Abliterating the model took our team—which had never previously attempted this task—about 2,200 GPU hours at a computation cost of roughly $4,400." The smaller variant was cheaper still: "Abliterating GLM-5.3-Flash took about 600 GPU hours." The effect: "The edit took GLM-5.3's refusal rate from above 90% to about 3% and 2% on the first two benchmarks (JailbreakBench and HarmBench) and to 12% on the third (StrongREJECT)."

THREE NUMBERS AND A TEAM DESCRIPTION, AND THE TEAM DESCRIPTION IS THE LOAD-BEARING ONE. The cost is low in absolute terms, but what makes it decision-relevant is that it was paid by operators with no prior experience of the technique. A control removable for four figures by first-time practitioners is not a barrier a defender can price an adversary out of. Anthropic also notes third parties did it faster than the vendor: abliterated builds were public "within days of the model's release."

THE DECISION THIS SETTLES, AND THE CONDITION THAT DECIDES IT. When you are choosing a model for a surface where refusal behaviour is part of your safety story — a user-facing agent, an automated pipeline a customer can steer, anything where you would point at the model's declining to do X as a reason you need no other control — weights you cannot restrict mean that story is unavailable, and the mitigation has to be environmental: scoped credentials, egress limits, action allowlists, human gates. Where the model runs on infrastructure you control and your own controls do the work, this says nothing against open weights; the claim is about load-bearing refusals, not about open weights being unsafe to use.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It does not mean closed weights make refusals a sound control. Anthropic's stated asymmetry is narrow — "Claude models cannot be abliterated because their weights aren't publicly released or customizable" — and it removes exactly one attack, not the class: the same document measures 92% engagement against GLM-5.3 via prefilled reasoning, which needs no weight access at all ([[see the prefill measurement]]). Abliteration is the floor of the bypass ladder, not its only rung.

THE CAPABILITY CONTEXT, which is why this matters now rather than in principle: on the same evaluations GLM-5.3 sat near a frontier model, not far below it — "50 of 410" attempts (12%) versus 56 of 410 (14%) for Claude Mythos Preview on a Chrome V8 exploit benchmark, and 4% versus 6% full control-flow hijacks on an internal binary-exploitation benchmark where earlier models scored 0%. Capability parity with no retained restraint is the combination, and either half alone would be unremarkable. Anthropic states its own conclusion as a prediction, hedged: GLM-5.3 "will likely give malicious actors access to capabilities that will allow them to find and exploit cyber vulnerabilities without meaningful restrictions." Note also the obvious interest: the organisation publishing this competes with the model it measured.
