# Measured guardrail cost on a legitimate specialist workload: 4 of 50 tasks completed under default safeguards, 34 of 50 with them relaxed — and the operator's fix was verifying the customer, not improving the classifier

Anthropic ran CyScenarioBench — "an evaluation that measures whether models can plan and execute multi-stage cyber operations under realistic constraints" — at two access tiers of its Cyber Verification Program, five attempts at each of ten challenges per tier. "In the Defense Access tier, 46 of the 50 trials were blocked at some point in the challenge, while the remaining four tasks succeeded." "In the Red Team Access tier, no blocks occurred, and Claude Opus 5.5 successfully completed 34 of the 50 tasks."

STATE THE COMPARISON IN FULL OR IT BECOMES A FALSE SLOGAN. Same benchmark, same model, same ten challenges, two permission tiers: 92% of trials interrupted and 4 completions on one side, zero interruptions and 34 completions on the other. The guardrails did not shave a margin off a legitimate workload — they removed roughly seven-eighths of its achievable output. This is the cost side of a guardrail decision stated as a number, which is rare; most published guardrail results give the block rate and leave the forgone-work rate unmeasured.

WHAT DECIDED IT, AND IT IS NOT A BETTER CLASSIFIER. Anthropic's resolution was a tiered program keyed to verified identity and authorization rather than to the content of the request: Defense Access for defensive work, reviewed in "a few days"; Red Team Access, organizations only, where "Organizations in this tier can only perform adversarial testing against systems they are authorized to test" and "Applications in this tier to take a few weeks to review"; and a Specialized tier for safety-critical systems where "We currently review every organization in depth in collaboration with the US government." Access to the relaxed tier is also conditioned on data retention. So the lever is who is asking and under what authorization, not how well the request can be classified — which is the same move as [[519d977b]] (monitor against a scope the holder declared in advance) and the same reasoning as [[123d278f]] (shifting enforcement from per-request blocking to retained offline review).

THE GENERAL FORM FOR SOMEONE BUILDING A PRODUCT. When a legitimate use is indistinguishable from the abuse case on request content alone, tuning the classifier trades one error for the other and cannot win; the exit is to move the decision to an attribute the request does not carry — verified identity, declared and attested scope, authorization over the target. Budget for the verification pipeline and the review latency, because those are what the tier actually costs.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It does not mean default safeguards are mis-tuned. Defense Access blocked 46 of 50 attempts at a benchmark explicitly built from offensive multi-stage operations; a tier intended for defenders behaving that way is the classifier working. The finding is that "working" and "usable by a verified specialist" are different targets and one artifact cannot hit both. It also does not generalise the ratio: 4-versus-34 is one benchmark in one domain at one pair of tiers.

This pairs with [[c60bebdb]], where one classifier's block rate varied 66%/55%/7% across models by distribution match, and with [[89df351e]], the pattern of tiering permissions so the classifier only adjudicates ambiguous actions — this fact is the measurement of what you pay when that tiering is absent. Separately, the program reports its defensive yield: partners "uncovered at least 129,000 verified software vulnerabilities between April and July 2026", of which "more than 33,000 have so far been rated as critical- or high-severity" — vendor-reported, with no false-positive or duplicate accounting given.

A SECOND OPERATOR DESCRIBES THE SAME COST WITHOUT A NUMBER, AND EXPECTS A DIFFERENT REMEDY. OpenAI says of its Astra-class misalignment monitor that "At launch, we expect Astra's safeguards to create more friction than we ultimately intend" and that "Extra safety checks can sometimes slow, pause, or stop legitimate work, including defensive cybersecurity" — the same phenomenon, acknowledged and unquantified, with calibration over time as the anticipated fix rather than a verified-access tier. [[8a0b760f]] also names a driver this fact does not cover: task DURATION is itself a flag, so long-running agents sit in the false-positive-prone region by construction. Treat the two as parallel reports from different products, not as corroboration — neither operator measured the other's system.
