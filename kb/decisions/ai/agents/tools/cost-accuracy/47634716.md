# On a tool-calling leaderboard that publishes a cost column, equal-accuracy entries differ in cost by one to two orders of magnitude — rank is not a budget

BFCL V4 publishes a dollar cost beside each entry's accuracy, and reading the two columns together separates entries the ranking treats as neighbours. From the 2026-04-12 revision:

- three entries inside one point of each other on overall accuracy span a 64-fold cost range: Claude-Sonnet-4-5 (FC) 73.24 at $43.73, Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (Prompt) 72.51 at $298.47, GLM-4.6 (FC thinking) 72.38 at $4.64
- cost does not even order within a model family: Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 (Prompt) scores 52.15 at $3.12 while Qwen3-32B (Prompt) scores 46.78 at $199.47 — a lower score at roughly 64 times the cost
- Claude-Haiku-4-5 (FC) scores 68.70 at $14.23 against Grok-4-0709 (FC) at 61.38 for $355.17

Operational consequence: when a leaderboard publishes cost, the accuracy column alone is the wrong read — take the cheapest entry that clears your accuracy bar, because the frontier of that curve is nowhere near the top of the ranking. When it does not publish cost, treat the ranking as telling you nothing about what the system will cost to run.

This does NOT mean the cheap entries are equivalent choices: the cost figure is this benchmark's own run cost under its own token mix, latency is a separate published column with its own three-orders-of-magnitude spread, and neither transfers to a different workload.

Scope: scores and costs as published on 2026-04-12 for BFCL V4.
