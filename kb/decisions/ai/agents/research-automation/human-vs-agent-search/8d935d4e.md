# Automated researchers beat 28 experienced humans' one-shot ideas on every alignment failure the humans worked on, typically within 6.4 hours — but the humans could not iterate, and the agent's number is a maximum over about 150 noisy evaluations

30 ideas were collected from 28 researchers with at least one year in technical AI safety (2.5 years on average) and a prior paper on at least one of the ten alignment failures studied; each idea was implemented faithfully, trained with three random seeds, and scored through the same pipeline as the agents' methods. On all seven failures where humans proposed ideas, the best agent-found method closed more of the safety headroom than the best human idea, reaching that point after 6.4 hours of hill-climbing on average; on the four failures where a capability-passing human idea scored above zero, the crossing took 8.6 hours on average. Cost comparison as stated: about $4 per hour in API inference against $150 per hour for the human researchers.

THREE CONDITIONS THAT A ONE-LINE SUMMARY DROPS, AND EACH ONE CHANGES WHAT THE RESULT LICENSES. The humans were not allowed to iterate on their submissions, so this is a one-shot idea against a search, not researcher against researcher. The agent's figure is the best of roughly 150 scored methods and is therefore biased upward by taking a maximum over noisy evaluations. And a human chose or built the benchmarks every method was scored on — the agents optimised a target they did not define. The authors' own reading is a division of labour rather than a replacement: humans define the measurable objective, agents supply candidate methods at a volume humans cannot match, humans refine them.

SEEDING A RUN WITH A HUMAN IDEA DID NOT HELP. 30 fresh runs anchored to one human-written idea each (implement it faithfully first, then iterate, then bring in other ideas freely) reached similar performance to 30 runs where the agent chose its own direction, on the same seven failures and target models; assigning five agents different human-guided directions did not help either. Note what this does and does not compare: both arms read the same literature survey in the review phase, so the test is who sets the research DIRECTION, not whether human ideas contribute anything.

A RELATED DATA POINT ON DATA VOLUME, with its caveat attached: a Claude Sonnet 5 agent (Epoch Capabilities Index 156) post-training an early Claude Opus 4.8 checkpoint (ECI 158) against a Petri audit covering all ten failures reached 65% over about 60 hours and more than 50 tested solutions, against 72% for the released model after full production alignment training, using about 2,400 training examples — two to three orders of magnitude less than published open-weight pipelines (Tulu 3's ~300,000 preference pairs, Llama 2-Chat's 1.4M+ human comparisons). That is not a claim that 2,400 examples suffice for alignment: only the ten studied failures were mitigated and measured, the agent could only create training data and not propose new training methods, and the score is a behavioural audit rather than deployment evidence.
