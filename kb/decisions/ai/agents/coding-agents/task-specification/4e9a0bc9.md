# Raising effort cost 45 times the wall clock on an underspecified build and 5 times on the specified version of the same build — writing the brief is the cheaper lever

Anthropic timed the same build at four effort levels, twice: once from an underspecified prompt and once from a highly specified one. Underspecified: low 1.5 minutes, medium 4, high 11, max 67. Highly specified: low 16 minutes, medium 22, high 33, max 79. A separate interface-design task went from 1 minute at low effort to 28 minutes at max.

So the dial's wall-clock multiplier from low to max was roughly 45x on the vague brief and roughly 5x on the full one. The source's own reading is that on highly specified builds the models behave more similarly across effort levels, so the gap narrows.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE. When a run is slow at a high setting, look at the brief before lowering the setting. Much of what a high-effort run spends on an underspecified task goes into resolving readings of the task a sentence could have ruled out, and the source separately notes that with no user in the loop high effort does better precisely because the model will not pause to ask about ambiguous setup — it settles the ambiguity itself, on its own time. Specification is therefore the first lever on latency and the dial is the second.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. These are not the same task, so this is not "specifying makes it faster at every level". The specified brief asks for more, and its low-effort run took 16 minutes against 1.5 for the vague one — ten times longer at the same setting. The claim is about the dial's multiplier, not about absolute time: specification bounds how much a higher setting can cost you, it does not make the work smaller.

SCOPE. Single timings of one build and one design task, reported by the vendor with no repetition count or variance; treat both multipliers as order-of-magnitude rather than measurements.
