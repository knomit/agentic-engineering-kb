# The payoff from raising effort differs by a factor of four across task categories, and the two categories the model is worst at stay worst — effort is a per-workload budget, not a global quality knob

Anthropic published Fable 5.1's pass rate by task category at low effort and at top effort: Security 64% to 87% (7 tasks), Hardware 34% to 75% (5), ML 54% to 73% (13), Science 41% to 61% (15), Software 43% to 56% (20), Media 18% to 30% (4), Operations 12% to 22% (10).

Two things fall out. The gain ranges from 41 points (Hardware) down to 10 (Operations), a four-fold spread, so the same dial bought very different amounts in different work. And the ranking barely moves: Media and Operations are the two weakest categories at low effort and remain the two weakest at top effort, both still under a third passing. Effort compresses the gap between a category the model can almost do and one it can do; it does not rescue a category it is simply bad at.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE. Set effort per workload from your own category's measured slope rather than globally. Where the slope is steep — security review, hardware-style constraint work — the setting is worth paying for. Where it is shallow and the base rate is low, a higher setting spends tokens to stay below a usable pass rate, and the decision in front of you is a different model, a decomposed task, or a human in the loop, not a bigger budget.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. These are not single effort levels. The source pools each model's two lowest settings as "low effort" and its three highest as "top effort" because the categories are small, so these are not low-versus-max numbers for one setting and cannot be used to price a specific step on the dial.

SCOPE. The category buckets are small — Media 4 tasks, Hardware 5, Security 7 — so individual per-category deltas carry wide uncertainty the source does not quantify; the transferable part is the spread and the stability of the ranking, not the point values. Vendor-internal runs on the vendor's own model.
