# A newer model at low effort beat an older model at high effort on both accuracy and cost, and an eval-driven prompt fix, not the model swap, closed the last ten points

Measured on a customer-support ticket benchmark of 44 cases, 30 used for the search and 14 held out, driven by an automated eval-and-hillclimb loop. The sequence in the source's own order:

- start: Opus 4.8 at default (high) effort, 74.4% decision accuracy on the search tickets, 4.6 cents per ticket
- Opus 5.5 at low effort: 87.8%, 1.9 cents per ticket, "less than half the starting cost"
- Sonnet 5 at low effort: 88.9%, 1 cent per ticket
- Sonnet 5 with an improved prompt (routing rules and a refund-cap cross-reference): 98.9% "at about the same cost"
- on the 14 held-out tickets: 90.5% for the final configuration against 78.6% for the original, at about one fifth of the cost

Part of the saving is pricing rather than behaviour: on Opus 5.5 "input and output tokens cost 20% less than on Opus 4.8, and cache reads cost 60% less".

Operational consequence: when a model generation lands, the cost lever is to drop the model tier AND the effort level together and spend the freed budget on prompt work measured against an eval — not to treat tier and effort as quality dials that have to stay high. Both halves are load-bearing: the downgrade alone stalled around 89%, and the prompt fix is what reached 98.9%.

Scope and traps: one task, one vendor's models, a 14-case holdout, and a search set of 30 — the direction is the finding, the magnitudes are not transferable. The 98.9% is a search-set figure and the 90.5% a holdout figure for different sets; they are not two readings of one number.
