# A compiled wiki over a document corpus bought nothing on single-hop lookups and about 5x accuracy on multi-hop questions — and the citation inconsistency it left needed a typed evidence graph to fix

LangChain compiled a document corpus into a linked wiki (OpenWiki) whose every statement rests on a claim citing specific source lines, then measured an agent answering questions with search alone versus with wiki access, holding model, instructions and documents constant.

The result splits hard by question depth. On single-hop lookups the wiki bought nothing and cost latency: "Results were similar for search and wiki, respectively (0.84 vs 0.83 accuracy; 0.72 vs 0.74 citation[ overlap])" "while latency increased from 9 to 12 seconds." On questions requiring several hops, "the wiki improved accuracy roughly 5x, from 0.10 with search alone to 0.52", "cutting cost per correct answer from about $0.55 to $0.19."

The wiki alone did not make the reasoning stable, and that is the finding a summary drops. "Citation overlap dropped from 0.74 on simple lookups to 0.25 on connected questions." — the same answer was reached by different evidence paths run to run. Adding a typed evidence graph over the claims (deterministic paragraph IDs, with cite, supersede, refers_to, amends and attaches_to edges built from explicit document references and wiki citations, needing no extra model calls) is what closed that: "Accuracy rose 27%, citation consistency doubled, median answer time fell 40%", plus a further cost reduction. So the compiled wiki and the evidence graph are two separate interventions buying two separate things — accuracy at depth, and reproducibility of the path.

The ingestion economics are stated separately from the answer costs and are not included in them. "OpenWiki compiled the 111-document library into a wiki roughly one-tenth its original size for $4.15" and "Adding 17 documents later cost about $0.09 each." That per-document figure is the marginal cost of a LATER addition, not an average of the build.

What this does NOT mean. It is not "give your agent a compiled wiki". On single-hop retrieval the wiki is a pure cost — equal accuracy, worse latency, plus the build — so the intervention only clears if the questions actually traverse dependencies and the compiled memory is reused across enough of them to amortise ingestion and maintenance. Scope: one vendor's own synthetic corpus of 111 long-form insurance documents scored against "a ledger of 972 facts", on "80 simple lookup questions and 50 connected questions" asked three times each, deliberately seeded with outdated versions, contradictions, indirect reference chains and varied wording. Edge quality also depends on the accuracy of the model-generated wiki citations the graph is built from, and the citation-consistency metric measures stable evidence, not correct answers.
