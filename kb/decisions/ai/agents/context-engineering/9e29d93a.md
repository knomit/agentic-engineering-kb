# Position (Lost in the Middle) vs length and confusability (Context Rot): which framing to act on

Two credible findings about why long contexts fail, and they prescribe different fixes.

Lost in the Middle (Liu et al., 2023; multi-document QA and key-value retrieval) found a U-shaped curve: accuracy is highest when the relevant passage is at the start or end of the context and drops when it sits in the middle. Its prescribed fix is positional — re-rank so the best evidence lands at the edges.

Context Rot (Chroma, 2025) re-ran the question on 18 current models and found degradation is not primarily positional. What predicts failure is total input length, low semantic similarity between query and answer, and the presence of confusable distractors — with the surprising result that a logically coherent haystack is WORSE than a shuffled one.

How to decide: treat position as a cheap second-order tiebreak, not the mechanism. If you are choosing between putting evidence at position 3 or position 30, edges still help. But if your fix for a long-context failure is re-ordering rather than shortening and de-confusing, you are optimising the wrong variable — Context Rot is the newer measurement, on current models, and it says cutting distractor chunks beats moving them. Do not read Context Rot as refuting positional effects outright; it re-frames them as one term in a larger degradation, so the older U-curve advice is not wrong, just insufficient on its own.
