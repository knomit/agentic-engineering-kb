# Position (Lost in the Middle) vs length and confusability (Context Rot): which framing to act on

Three credible positions on why long contexts fail, and they prescribe different fixes.

Lost in the Middle (Liu et al., 2023; multi-document QA and key-value retrieval) found a U-shaped curve: accuracy is highest when the relevant passage sits at the start or end of the context and drops when it sits in the middle. Its prescribed fix is positional — re-rank so the best evidence lands at the edges.

Context Rot (Chroma, 2025) re-ran the question on 18 models and found degradation is not primarily positional. What predicts failure is total input length, low semantic similarity between query and answer, and the presence of confusable distractors — with the surprising result that a logically coherent haystack is worse than a shuffled one. Chroma's own wording: "structural coherence consistently hurts model performance", and models "perform better on shuffled haystacks than on logically structured ones". They note this remains unexplained mechanistically. On distractors: even a single one reduces performance relative to baseline, multiple compound, and the effect amplifies with input length.

MODEL VINTAGE, because it bounds how far to trust the numbers: Chroma's 18 models were the frontier of mid-2025 — GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5 and Qwen3. Every one has since shipped a successor. The phenomena above are mechanism-level and have not been contradicted, but treat the magnitudes as measured on a superseded generation and re-measure before quoting a specific degradation figure for a current model.

Anthropic's current prompting docs keep prescribing a positional rule for inputs past roughly 20k tokens, and attach a number to it: put longform documents at the TOP, above the query, instructions and examples, because queries placed at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in their tests, especially on complex multi-document inputs. That is a vendor measurement, and it is a larger effect than "second-order tiebreak" would suggest.

How to decide. Ordering is cheap, so do it: long inputs first, query last, each document wrapped in its own tags with a `<source>`. But if your response to a long-context FAILURE is to re-order rather than to shorten and de-confuse, you are still optimising the wrong variable — Context Rot's finding is that cutting distractor chunks beats moving them, and no amount of ordering rescues a context stuffed with near-miss passages. The two are compatible: position is a free win on a well-curated context, and no substitute for curating it.

A third lever the same Anthropic guidance adds, orthogonal to both: for long-document tasks, have the model first quote the relevant passages into a `<quotes>` block and then answer from those. That converts a long-context problem into a short-context one at the cost of one extra pass.
