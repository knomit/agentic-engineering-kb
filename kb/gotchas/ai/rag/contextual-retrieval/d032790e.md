# Contextual Retrieval's headline reductions are relative moves on a 5.7% base failure rate, and the variant that works is per-chunk context — document summaries were measured and rejected

Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval results are reported as 1 minus recall@20 — the share of relevant documents that fail to appear in the top 20 chunks — averaged across four knowledge domains (codebases, fiction, arXiv papers, science papers) with the top-performing embedding configuration (Gemini Text 004):

- Contextual Embeddings alone: 5.7% -> 3.7% (reported as a 35% reduction)
- Contextual Embeddings plus Contextual BM25: 5.7% -> 2.9% (49%)
- the same plus reranking (Cohere reranker; retrieve top 150, rerank, keep top 20): 5.7% -> 1.9% (67%)

Every headline percentage is a RELATIVE reduction on a failure rate that starts at 5.7%. The absolute improvement for the full stack is 3.8 percentage points. Quote the relative figure together with the base rate; "reduces failed retrievals by 67%" on its own reads as a far larger effect than the measurement supports, and the measurement is of the retrieval step only, not of answer quality.

THE MECHANISM IS THE PER-CHUNK PART, AND THE CHEAPER-LOOKING VARIANT WAS ALREADY TRIED. What is prepended before embedding and before BM25 indexing is 50-100 tokens generated per chunk by a model that was shown the whole document and asked to situate that specific chunk within it. The source reports the neighbouring approaches failing: prepending generic document summaries to chunks gave "very limited gains", and summary-based indexing showed "low performance". So a pipeline that computes one document-level summary and prepends it to every chunk of that document is not a cheaper implementation of this technique — it is the variant that was measured and rejected.

THE COST FIGURE HOLDS ONLY UNDER FOUR ASSUMPTIONS PLUS CACHING. The stated one-time cost is $1.02 per million document tokens, "assuming 800 token chunks, 8k token documents, 50 token context instructions, and 100 tokens of context per chunk", and it depends on prompt caching so the reference document is loaded once rather than re-sent with every chunk. Without caching the whole document is re-read once per chunk and the arithmetic is different in kind, not in degree.

THE PRIOR STEP IS NOT TO BUILD IT. The source recommends skipping RAG entirely when the knowledge base fits in the prompt. Its instance of that threshold was 200,000 tokens ("about 500 pages of material"), the then-current Claude context window; the durable rule is whether the corpus fits the window, and the number moves with the model.

Retrieval depth: top-20 outperformed top-10 and top-5 in these tests, with the source noting that "more information can be distracting for models so there's a limit to this". This is a retrieval-recall result and is not a claim that longer contexts are better for generation.
