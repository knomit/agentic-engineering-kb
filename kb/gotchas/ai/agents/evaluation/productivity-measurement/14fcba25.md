# A session-level agent-productivity estimator validated at r_log 0.74 is an aggregate instrument only: 2–3x errors on a single session are common and about half of sessions land within a factor of two

Cognition published the construction and validation of an estimator that predicts, per Devin session, how many engineering hours the same work would have taken a human. The usable part is the gap between its aggregate and its per-session accuracy, both measured on the same held-out set.

THE MEASUREMENT. "On the held-out evaluation set of 233 sessions, our estimator has an r_log of 0.74 and r_log^2 of 0.54", significant at "F(1,231) = 279.9, p < 10^-5". Per session, though: "Around 50% of sessions fall within a factor of 2 of the true estimate" and "2–3x errors in either direction are common". The authors' own rule for why the aggregate is still usable is that "because errors are roughly unbiased and independent, they cancel as session count grows".

CONSEQUENCE. Never attach such a number to one session, one engineer or one review decision; the instrument is built for a portfolio total. And the cancellation argument is conditional on independence, which this dataset partly contradicts: "Roughly half the residual disagreement lies between users rather than within a user's own sessions (ICC = 0.58)", so a total dominated by a few users does not get the full benefit of averaging.

WHAT THE GROUND TRUTH IS. "Our ground truth is self-reported" — users estimated how long each completed session would have taken them without the agent. There is no timed human baseline and no with-and-without control. The model is also deliberately biased: it was "calibrated to underestimate rather than overestimate delivered output", and even after a log-space calibration fit (h = 2.28 x m^0.923) "the total of the human estimates remains 1.4x the total of the corrected model estimates". The dataset is 258 sessions from 126 users across eight deployments, with 1–20% of sessions removed by a productivity filter "depending on the customer".

THE CEILING WORTH KNOWING. On the comparison the article cites, human developers estimating the same 1,000 Jira tickets reached only r_log = 0.67, against 0.46 for a model given the ticket title and description. Human estimates are themselves a noisy instrument, so an estimator's correlation against them is bounded by their own reliability rather than by the truth.

NOT ESTABLISHED. No cost or dollar figure, no aggregate hour total, no measurement window, no post-merge defect accounting. The authors state it directly: "Hours don't account for quality", and "Predicting real ROI is hard."
