# A hallucination rate is uninterpretable without the answer rate beside it — on the Vectara leaderboard a 4.3% rate at a 62.7% answer rate outranks a 4.5% rate at 99.9%

The Vectara hallucination leaderboard publishes four columns per model, and the ranking is on one of them. Rows from the table last updated May 11, 2026 show why the other three have to be read with it: snowflake-arctic-instruct sits at a 4.3% hallucination rate with a **62.7% answer rate**, ranked above mistralai/mistral-large-2411 at 4.5% with a **99.9% answer rate**; microsoft/Phi-4 is at 3.7% with an 80.7% answer rate. A model that declines a third of the documents is being scored on the two-thirds it chose, so abstention buys rank. The leader on that table, antgroup/finix_s1_32b at 1.8% / 99.5%, and openai/gpt-5.4-nano-2026-03-17 at 3.1% / 100.0%, are the rows where the rate means what it looks like.

Average summary length is the second confound in the same direction: the rows range from 64.6 to 172.4 words, and a shorter summary asserts less and therefore has less to be wrong about. Comparing a 95-word summariser's rate with a 172-word summariser's is comparing two different amounts of claim.

What the benchmark is. Factual consistency when a model summarizes a document, judged by HHEM-2.3 — described on the page as "Vectara's commercial hallucination evaluation model", i.e. the leaderboard operator's own product, which is a conflict to note rather than a defect to assume. The corpus is held back to avoid overfitting: it "Contains over 7700 articles" ranging "as short as 50 words to as long as 24K words", and because it is not public you cannot do error analysis on a result or check what your own domain looks like inside it. Temperature 0 "except where that was impossible or not available".

WHAT THE MAINTAINERS THEMSELVES RULE OUT, and it is the sentence most often dropped when this table is cited: "We are **not** evaluating the quality of the summaries, only the **factual consistency** of them", "This does not definitively measure all the ways a model can hallucinate", and they "do not recommend using this as a standalone metric". So a low rate here supports "grounded in the provided document when it answers" and not "hallucinates less" in general, and certainly not "summarizes better".
