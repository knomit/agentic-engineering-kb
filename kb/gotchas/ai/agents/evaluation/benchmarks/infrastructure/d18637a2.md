# Agentic coding eval scores move ~6 points on CPU and RAM allocation alone — a small leaderboard gap deserves skepticism until the configuration is documented and matched

Anthropic ran the same agentic benchmark across six resource configurations, 'from strict enforcement of the per-task specs (1x), having them as both floor and ceiling, to completely uncapped', and measured how much the score moved on infrastructure alone.

**Terminal-Bench 2.0: 'the gap between the most- and least-resourced setups on Terminal-Bench 2.0 was 6 percentage points (p < 0.01)'** — the model, the scaffold and the tasks held constant, only CPU and RAM allocation varying.

The mechanism is visible in the infrastructure error rate, which fell from **5.8% at strict enforcement to 0.5% when uncapped**; the drop from strict enforcement to 3x headroom alone (5.8% to 2.1%) was significant at p < 0.001. Under-resourced runs are not producing worse reasoning, they are producing crashes, timeouts and OOM kills that the harness scores as task failures.

**THE MAGNITUDE IS BENCHMARK-SPECIFIC — DO NOT CARRY THE 6-POINT FIGURE ACROSS. Corrected 2026-08-11.** The same experiment on SWE-bench moved far less: scores 'again increased monotonically with RAM, but were only 1.54 percentage points higher at 5x than 1x'. Terminal-Bench 2.0 is the sensitive case, plausibly because terminal tasks are more infrastructure-bound than repository-patching tasks. The direction generalises — more resources, monotonically better scores, on both benchmarks — but the size does not.

**PRECISION ON THE RECOMMENDATION, corrected in the same pass.** An earlier revision of this fact rendered the guidance as 'treat sub-3-point leaderboard gaps as noise'. The source does not say they are noise. It says such differences 'deserve skepticism until the eval configuration is documented and matched'. The distinction is load-bearing: a sub-3-point gap between two runs whose configurations you have documented and matched is a real difference and you may act on it. What you may not do is read a small gap off a leaderboard that does not publish its resource configuration and conclude anything at all. The remedy is to demand the configuration, not to discard the number.

Practical consequences: publish your eval's CPU/RAM allocation alongside the score, or the score is not reproducible; when you cannot match a competitor's configuration, treat their margin as unverifiable rather than as either real or noise; and when your own score moves after an infrastructure change, suspect the infrastructure before you suspect the model. Note also that this sits underneath every other comparability problem — resource allocation is one more harness variable, alongside scaffold, tool set, retry policy and step limit.
