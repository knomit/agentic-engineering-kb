# A cyber-capability score measured on static, known targets overstates live novel performance by roughly 2x to 8x, and on one benchmark 37.1% of passes never used the intended route

An OWASP GenAI Security Project report measured the gap between agentic security scores on static, already-public targets and scores on live, novel ones, and the gap is large enough that the two numbers are not comparable quantities.

The paired figures, as the report's own table prints them: CyberGym-E2E 82.3% static/known against 19.2% live/novel under a $10 and 90-minute cap, an overstatement of about 4.3x; AutoPenBench 27% against 9%, about 3x; PentestEval 41% against "3-6% fully autonomous", about 8x; static CTF against live CTF, about 2x. One model (Gemini 3 Pro) scored "11 of 12" on hard static challenges and "0 of 13" on simulated multi-stage environments. Related single-number results in the same section: Cybench "approximately 100% at pass@30 on a 37-task subset by February 2026", CVEBench "in the low tens of percent", ExploitGym "around 18%".

A second, independent inflation mechanism was measured on the same family of benchmarks. A July 2026 audit of 1,518 traces across 22 frontier models on Cybench found that "37.1% of passes reached the flag by an unintended route", with scores "inflated by up to five times". So a pass is not evidence the task was solved as designed, and the two mechanisms compound: a static target raises the score, and an unintended route raises it again.

The operational consequence is the report's own rule, stated verbatim: "Never accept a cyber score without k, trial count, compute or cost budget, and harness". Both the sampling parameter and the budget are load-bearing, because the same subset reaches ~100% at pass@30 that is far below that at low k.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It is not the claim that static benchmarks are worthless, and it is not a single blanket discount factor — the measured ratios run from about 2x to about 8x across four benchmarks, so there is no one number to divide by. It also does not mean the live figure is the agent's capability ceiling: the report says its own constraints were operational and that "our testing did not probe the model's capability ceiling directly".
