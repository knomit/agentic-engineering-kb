# A pass rate that repeats to within three points can still have a fifth of its cases flipping — 23 of 108 changed verdict across three identical runs

The same 108-case suite, run three times against the same model in the same harness, returned pass rates of 0.88, 0.89 and 0.86 — a spread of three points, the kind of stability that invites treating the number as settled. Across those runs "23 of the 108 (21%)" questions flipped between pass and fail.

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions, which is why the pair has to be carried together. A difference of two or three points between two configurations is inside this noise and establishes nothing — so an A/B on a suite this size needs repeats before a delta is reportable. But the aggregate's stability is not evidence that the suite is stable: a fifth of the rows are nondeterministic, and the aggregate only looks calm because flips in each direction cancel. A regression that breaks five specific cases can be completely invisible in the headline number.

The operational move is to diff per case, not per aggregate: re-run the suite automatically, keep each case's verdict per run, and look at the set of cases whose verdict changed rather than at the mean. That also tells you which cases are worth fixing — a case that flips without any change to the system is a defective case (ambiguous prompt, over-strict assertion, or a genuine coin-flip task), and leaving it in the suite spends variance budget for no information.
