# LLM judges have position and length biases you must actively cancel

When scoring with an LLM judge, prefer pairwise comparison of two outputs over asking for a single-output Likert score — relative judgements are far more stable than absolute ones. But pairwise introduces its own artefacts that will silently inflate your numbers unless you cancel them:

- Position bias: the judge favours one slot regardless of content. Cancel it by running each pair twice with the order reversed and only counting consistent verdicts.
- Length bias: judges prefer longer responses. Control for it explicitly, or you will "improve" your system by making it more verbose.
- Allow ties. Forcing a winner injects noise on genuinely equivalent pairs.
- Ask for chain-of-thought reasoning before the verdict, not after.

The applied-llms authors report taking judge-to-human agreement from 68% to 94% over three iterations of this loop — the useful implication being that an unrefined judge sitting at ~68% agreement looks perfectly plausible in a dashboard while being wrong about a third of the time.

Related trap from the same source: over-optimising a single metric degrades others. Focusing on needle-in-a-haystack recall measurably reduced extraction and summarization performance. Evaluate on realistic tasks, not synthetic recall probes.
