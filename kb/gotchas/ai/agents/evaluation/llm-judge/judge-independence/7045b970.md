# A judge that is also one of the models under test cannot establish a ranking, and a refusal benchmark with no over-refusal arm cannot establish safety — one published suite did both at once

Two independent structural defects can make a well-executed eval suite unable to support the direction its conclusion points, and both are visible from the method section alone, before any result is read.

THE JUDGE AS CONTESTANT. In Cognition's open-source trustworthiness evaluation, the refusal benchmark graded responses with "a strong judge (GPT 5.5)" and the differential-security benchmark graded with "a frontier American model (GPT 5.5)" — and GPT 5.5 is also one of the models being compared. Self-preference is a documented judge bias, so a comparison in which one contestant scores the field cannot separate that contestant's advantage from its quality. The remedy is not a better prompt: it is a judge drawn from outside the compared set, or a published agreement check against human labels.

THE MISSING ARM. The refusal benchmark scored "Mean COMPLY across all scenarios; higher is worse" — that is, it measured only compliance with problematic requests. Nothing measured refusal of legitimate ones. A model that refuses everything wins such a benchmark outright. So "refused the identical request every time" supports a claim about compliance and supports nothing about usability, even though the suite's own motivation names over-refusal as a concern.

THE GENERAL FORM. A one-sided safety metric is maximised by a useless system, so every such metric needs its opposing arm measured in the same run, or its result stated only in the direction it covers. Reporting one side and concluding about the whole is the error, and it survives review because the measured side is usually real.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. Neither defect makes the underlying measurements wrong — the compliance behaviours reported (including a model volunteering identity, camera location and timestamp fields in a surveillance task "without being prompted to do so") are observations that stand on their own. What does not stand is the comparative ranking and the safety verdict drawn from them. The suite also publishes no scenario count, no task count, no statistical test and no confidence-interval values in its text, so its effect sizes cannot be checked at all.
