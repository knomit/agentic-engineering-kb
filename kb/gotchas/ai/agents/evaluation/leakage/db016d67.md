# In an automated optimisation loop the leak is the optimiser itself: it may read failing transcripts but must not copy failure content into the prompt

The stated rule for an automated hillclimber is "If the hillclimber reads the failing transcripts, it should never paste the failure content into the prompt." The line is between a general fix inferred from a failure and the failure's own content carried into the artefact under test. The second scores on exactly the cases it was copied from, transfers nothing, and can still clear a train/test guard, because the copied cases sit in the train split where the optimiser was allowed to read them.

A second channel is the environment rather than the prompt: "leftover state from an earlier trial (a file, a git history) can hand the agent the answer." That one surfaces as run-to-run variance rather than as an obviously inflated score.

Operational consequence: keep answers out of the model's reach and reset trial state between runs, and treat the two channels separately. A train/test split catches neither on its own — it bounds how far a leak can be banked, not whether one happened.
