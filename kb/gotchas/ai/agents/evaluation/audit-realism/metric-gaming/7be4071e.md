# Three attempts to push an audit-realism number up, and each moved the tell rather than removing it — two were abandoned by their own authors

A realism metric is a detector, and a detector optimised against becomes a description of what you stopped doing rather than of what you fixed. One research programme reports three instances of this inside a single post, two of them as directions it abandoned.

(1) RESAMPLING ON VERBALIZED EVAL AWARENESS — ABANDONED. The auditor was instructed to watch for the target verbalizing eval awareness and, when it appeared, to roll back to before it, rewrite the message to be more realistic, and resample the target's action. Across roughly 111 seeds (3 audits each, Opus 4.5 auditor, Sonnet 4.5 target) the protocol "was effective in reducing verbalized eval awareness in the final branch". The authors dropped it anyway, and their reason is the finding: "Based on our human judgement, the new audits weren't actually more realistic. The auditor wasn't very good at rewriting more realistic prompts / scenarios, and would sometimes even give up if the target repeatedly showed eval awareness. It's plausible that the target still had unverbalized eval awareness, despite the decrease in verbalized eval awareness." The metric fell; the property it stood for did not move.

(2) NEEDLE-IN-A-HAYSTACK REALISM — BUILT AND DROPPED. The natural generalisation of pairwise comparison is to hide one eval transcript among N-1 deployment transcripts and ask the judge to find it, which is tunable to any difficulty. It failed for two reasons. COST AND CONTEXT: agentic transcripts are long, so the evals either hit context-window limits or exhausted rate limits on input tokens. And THE JUDGE CHEATS: because the haystack is drawn from a narrow slice of deployment, the judge can pick the odd one out on spurious shared features. The tell they caught it using was LENGTH — the judge noted in its reasoning that the audit transcript was much longer than the others, and most WildChat transcripts are short. A judge that finds the needle by distribution artifact marks your eval unrealistic for a reason that has nothing to do with your eval.

(3) THE FIDELITY INTERVENTION ITSELF TRADES ONE TELL FOR ANOTHER. Classifying the judge's stated reasons for correctly identifying the eval across four named features, giving the auditor real deployment resources "substantially reduce[d] the rate that the grader mentions that the eval transcript is 'too clean'", and reduced unrealistic user behaviour and artificial pressure — while causing "a slight increase in inconsistent tool results, likely because the environments with resources are more complicated, and thus harder to track for the auditor". Richer environments are harder to keep internally consistent, so fidelity bought three tells and cost a fourth.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE: an improvement in one realism metric is evidence of realism only if the other metrics AND the judge's stated reasons are re-measured alongside it, including the features the intervention was not aimed at. This is the same shape [[19218a92]] records for targeted monitor prompts, and [[5536f138]] states the condition under which it bites: when the detector has several distinguishable features to key on, closing one redistributes mass onto the others, and a programme that re-measures only the feature it attacked records a win every time.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN, and the post is the evidence against the wrong reading: it is not an argument against improving realism. The same programme's fidelity intervention moved all three of the metrics it measured in the same direction — realism win rate, post-hoc realism rating and verbalized eval-awareness rate all agreed that resources helped. The failure mode is optimising a single proxy directly, which is the same distinction [[0c3c2d6a]] draws between measuring a channel and acting on it.
