# Frontier rank on general agentic benchmarks did not transfer to research judgment: most models scored within two standard deviations of chance, including two that lead elsewhere

TASTE scores a model on how often it picks the same AI-safety research proposal that experienced human researchers preferred. Against an estimated 77% human agreement rate, the best model reached 60% (Fable 5), and almost all models evaluated fell within two standard deviations of chance. Two models at the frontier of general agentic benchmarks — Opus 5 and GPT-5.6-Sol — performed near chance.

**The operational consequence is about picking a judge.** The usual selection rule for an LLM judge is "use the strongest available model", with strength read off general agentic or coding leaderboards. On this task that rule had no predictive power: leaderboard position and judgment agreement came apart, and the model that did best was not the one the leaderboards would have nominated. Before trusting a judge on a task whose answers cannot be verified, measure it on that task against human labels; do not infer its judgment from its capability elsewhere.

**Scope, and it is narrow.** One benchmark, 92 pairs, a single hard-to-verify domain (empirical AI-safety research proposals). Per-model confidence intervals span roughly ±10 percentage points, so the benchmark cannot rank models against each other — which is itself the point worth carrying: it establishes that most models are near chance here, not which model to choose. It does not show that judgment capability is absent in general, and the authors expect future models to close the gap.
