# A model-quality regression can originate entirely below the weights — routing, compiler and precision bugs degrade a pinned model version while your evals stay green

Anthropic's September 2025 postmortem describes three concurrent, independent infrastructure bugs that degraded Claude output quality without any change to model weights: a context-window routing error, a TPU token-generation corruption introduced by a runtime performance optimisation, and a latent XLA:TPU miscompilation of approximate top-k. Pinning a model version protected against none of them.

The detection story is the actionable part. Anthropic states plainly that "the evaluations we ran simply didn't capture the degradation users were reporting." The reasons they give generalise to anyone running an agent on a hosted model: the bugs overlapped, so user reports were "confusing and contradictory"; symptoms varied by serving platform, which obscured the root cause; the August 29 load-balancing change that triggered the worst of it looked routine, so the spike in negative reports was not connected to it; and their standing evals were noisy enough that no clear link could be drawn to any individual change.

What this means for a consumer: a benchmark run samples the model, not your deployment. An error affecting a minority of requests, or only requests with certain shapes, is invisible to an eval suite that scores aggregate accuracy on a fixed dataset — the eval passes because most requests are fine. If your agent's quality is reported as degraded and your evals disagree, the eval is not evidence of health; check per-request outcome distributions and the tails, and correlate against your provider's deployment timeline rather than only your own. Note this is a different failure axis from harness-induced eval noise (CPU/RAM allocation, trial isolation): here the harness is constant and the serving stack moved underneath it.
