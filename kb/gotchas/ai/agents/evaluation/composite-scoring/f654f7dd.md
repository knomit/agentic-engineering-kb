# A composite eval can drop when the model gets better — specialised training regressed the report-writing sub-score, not the capability

OpenAI's Vulnerability Discovery and Report Writing eval awards points for three different things at once: finding severe and actionable vulnerabilities, developing a working proof-of-concept, and submitting a high-quality vulnerability report.

GPT-5.6-Cyber — trained specifically to be better at finding vulnerabilities, and measurably better than GPT-5.6 Sol on ExploitGym and on a separate zero-day severity-and-calibration benchmark — scores WORSE than Sol on this one. OpenAI's stated explanation is not that it finds fewer vulnerabilities: it is that the model 'sometimes produc[es] shorter, less detailed vulnerability reports.' The capability went up and the aggregate went down, because the rubric bundles the capability with the write-up.

**The failure this causes is a wrong decision, not a wrong number.** A team reading only the aggregate concludes the specialised model is worse at vulnerability discovery and ships the general one — the exact opposite of what the sub-scores support. The regression is in a presentation component that a post-processing step or a report-formatting prompt could recover for free.

What to do about it: when a rubric scores several criteria, record and report the per-criterion breakdown, not just the total, and require a sub-score decomposition before acting on any aggregate regression. Be especially suspicious when the model under test was fine-tuned for exactly one of the bundled criteria — targeted training reliably shifts output style (length, verbosity, hedging) as a side effect, and style is what rubric items like 'high-quality report' are actually measuring. Ask of any composite eval: which of these criteria am I optimising, and which am I merely hoping stays constant?

The generalisation beyond this case: any eval that mixes a capability criterion with a communication criterion will move for reasons that have nothing to do with the capability. Verbosity-sensitive rubrics drift with every fine-tune, every system-prompt change, and every reasoning-effort setting.
