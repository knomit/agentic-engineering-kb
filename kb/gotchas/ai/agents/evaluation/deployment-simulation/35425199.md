# A deployment simulation induced six to eleven times more verbalized evaluation awareness than the real traffic it replayed, and the awareness rate differed per model — so the confound must be removed by intersecting, not by averaging

To forecast how a new model would behave in internal agentic coding traffic, one lab simulated its deployment over roughly 49,650 recorded tasks and labelled the resulting trajectories for misaligned behaviour. Analysing the chain-of-thought for signs of evaluation or simulation awareness, it found such signs in 4.81%, 8.67%, 4.70% and 2.52% of trajectories for the four models compared — against 0.76% in the ORIGINAL traffic those tasks came from, which had been generated by a mix of models in genuine use.

Two separate problems follow, and conflating them is the error. The first is absolute: replaying real traffic as a simulation raised verbalized awareness roughly sixfold to elevenfold over the traffic being replayed, so the simulation is measurably not the thing it simulates. The second is comparative: the awareness rate differed by more than three times BETWEEN the models being compared, so any cross-model difference in measured misalignment is entangled with a difference in how often each model noticed it was in a simulation.

The lab's handling of the comparative problem is the transferable method. Rather than adjusting rates or reporting the confound as a caveat, it restricted the comparison to the 41,849 tasks in which NO model showed verbalized awareness — the intersection across all four — and re-reported on that subset, where the newest model still showed about 17%, 46% and 65% fewer severity-3-or-higher flags than the three comparators. Intersecting is the right shape because the subset is identical for every arm; excluding each model's own aware trajectories separately would give each arm a different task set and reintroduce the confound as selection.

What this does NOT establish: that the subset is confound-free. Verbalized awareness is a lower bound on awareness — it only catches models that say so — and a separate measurement on 1,300 audit seeds found that verbalized evaluation awareness did not predict misalignment at all, while suppressing the internal representation of it did move a measured rate. Subsetting on the verbalized signal therefore removes a visible correlate, not necessarily the mechanism. Treat the residual difference as robust to the part of the confound you can see, and not as a clean estimate. See [[0c3c2d6a]].
