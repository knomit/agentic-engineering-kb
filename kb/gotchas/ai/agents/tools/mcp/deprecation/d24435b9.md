# MCP's HTTP+SSE removal clock cannot be computed from either published page — the registry anchors it to the one event the policy says it is not measured from

If you still run the HTTP+SSE transport, you cannot derive a migration deadline from MCP's own documents. Both pages were read in full on 2026-10-11 and they do not compose.

**The policy's rule.** The feature lifecycle and deprecation policy (adopted via SEP-2596) requires a deprecation SEP to specify a minimum window of "the number of months, at least twelve, that the feature must remain Deprecated before it is eligible for removal", and fixes the measurement point explicitly: the window "is measured from the release of the specification revision in which the feature is first marked Deprecated, not from the date the SEP reaches Final." Earliest removal is then "the first specification revision released as Current on or after the window elapses".

**Four of the six registry rows follow that shape.** Roots, Sampling, Logging and Dynamic Client Registration were each Deprecated in revision `2026-07-28` and each carry the same earliest removal: "First revision released on or after 2027-07-28" — twelve months, counted from the revision, expressed as a revision rather than a date.

**Two rows do not, and HTTP+SSE is the one with operational consequences.** Its earliest removal reads, verbatim, "Three months after SEP-2596 reaches Final". (The other exception, `includeContext: "thisServer"` / `"allServers"`, reads "Follows Sampling (SEP-2577)", which is at least resolvable to a date.) Three independent problems stack on that one HTTP+SSE cell:

1. It is anchored to the SEP reaching Final — the exact event the policy says the window is *not* measured from.
2. Three months is below the policy's twelve-month floor. The only mechanism the policy provides for going below twelve months is expedited removal, which requires "a vulnerability with a published security advisory or documented in-the-wild exploitation for which no in-place mitigation exists" plus Core Maintainer approval under the governance decision process, and must still leave "at least ninety days between the feature becoming Deprecated and its earliest removal". Neither page records such a finding for HTTP+SSE, and three months is at that ninety-day floor rather than comfortably above it.
3. Neither page publishes the date SEP-2596 reached Final, so even taking the row at face value there is no date to count three months from.

**The registry offers a carve-out that does not resolve.** It states: "The HTTP+SSE transport and the `includeContext` values were already described as deprecated before the lifecycle policy existed; SEP-2596 reclassifies them as Deprecated under its transition provisions", and links the phrase "transition provisions" to the feature-lifecycle policy page. That page contains no transition-provisions section and no mention of pre-policy deprecations at all. The citation points at a document that does not carry the provision it cites, so the exception is asserted without a published rule behind it.

**What to do.** Treat HTTP+SSE as removable on short notice with no computable deadline, and migrate to Streamable HTTP now rather than scheduling against a date. HTTP+SSE has been described as deprecated since `2025-03-26`, so by any reading its window is long since open.

**What this does NOT mean.** It is not evidence that MCP shortened its deprecation window, and the three-month figure must not be generalised. The twelve-month floor still governs every feature deprecated *under* the policy — the four 2027-07-28 rows are the policy working as written. What is unverifiable is the earliest removal of the two features that were already deprecated before the policy existed and were swept into it retroactively.

**Scope.** This is an inconsistency between two published pages observed on one date, not a claim about maintainer intent, and not a prediction that HTTP+SSE will be removed soon. The registry itself says it is "a derived view kept consistent with the per-feature deprecation notices and changelog entries, which are the normative records" — so the per-feature notice for HTTP+SSE, not the registry, is where a resolution would appear.
