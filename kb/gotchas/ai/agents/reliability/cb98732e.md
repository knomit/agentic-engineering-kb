# There is a hallucination floor, but its size is set by the benchmark: ~1-2% on HHEM summarization, ~8-12% on harder faithfulness graders

The original practitioner claim (applied-llms.org, published 2024-06-08, re-verified live and unchanged 2026-07-26) was a baseline factual-inconsistency rate of 5-10%, with below 2% described as challenging even on a task as constrained as summarizing a provided document.

STALENESS CHECK 2026-07-26 — the specific numbers are superseded, and how they are superseded is the actually useful finding.

On Vectara's HHEM grounded-summarization leaderboard (7,700+ articles; the model must summarize a source document faithfully and a detector flags unsupported content), frontier models in 2026 sit at roughly 1.0-2.5%, down from 3-8% in 2023. So the '2% is hard' half of the original claim no longer holds on that benchmark.

But Vectara's harder FaithJudge grader puts the SAME models far higher — one model measured at 0.7% under HHEM scores about 7.6% under FaithJudge, and an older frontier model sits near 12%. The benchmark methodology, not the model, is what determines the headline number.

What to take from this, which is unchanged and is why the fact is kept rather than retracted:

1. There IS a floor, it is above zero, and no prompt gets you to zero. Budget for it.
2. Any hallucination rate you are quoted is a statement about a grader, not about a model. A vendor number near 1% and a research number near 10% can both be honest measurements of the same system. Ask which detector produced it before comparing two figures or before adopting one as your own expectation.
3. Therefore measure YOUR floor on YOUR task with YOUR acceptance criteria. Inheriting either number is wrong.

The design consequence survives intact: a product whose correctness requirement is stricter than its measured floor cannot be met by generation alone. You need a downstream factual-consistency check as a separate component, and the surrounding workflow — citation, verification, human confirmation on the consequential step — has to absorb a residual error rate that will not go away.

Confidence note: the 2026 figures here come from secondary reporting of the Vectara leaderboards rather than from a direct read of the leaderboard itself, which is JS-rendered. Re-derive from the live leaderboard before quoting a specific model's number.
