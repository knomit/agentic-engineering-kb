# There is a hallucination floor, but its size is set by the benchmark: ~1-2% on HHEM summarization, ~8-12% on harder faithfulness graders

The original practitioner claim (applied-llms.org, published 2024-06-08, re-verified live and unchanged 2026-07-26) was a baseline factual-inconsistency rate of 5-10%, with below 2% described as challenging even on a task as constrained as summarizing a provided document.

STALENESS CHECK 2026-07-26 (second pass, now from a DIRECT source read) — the specific numbers are superseded, and how they are superseded is the actually useful finding.

SOURCE CORRECTION: the Vectara hallucination leaderboard had been recorded here as JS-rendered and unreadable, so earlier numbers in this fact came from secondary reporting. That was wrong about the source, not just the numbers. The canonical leaderboard is the README of github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard, which is server-rendered and reads fine with an ordinary fetch. Use that URL.

Direct read, leaderboard last updated 2026-05-11, grader HHEM-2.3 (Vectara's commercial detector; HHEM-2.1-Open is the open variant). The lowest hallucination rates on the board run roughly 1.8% to 4%: finix_s1_32b 1.8%, gpt-5.4-nano 3.1%, gemini-2.5-flash-lite 3.3%, Phi-4 3.7%, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo 4.1%.

Two corrections to what this fact previously said:

- The earlier '~1.0-2.5% for frontier models' range was too low and too flattering. The observed best is 1.8%, and nothing is below it.
- The top of this leaderboard is NOT the frontier flagships. It is dominated by small and efficient models — a 32B model, a nano, a flash-lite. Grounded-summarization faithfulness is not the same axis as general capability, and ranking on it does not transfer. Do not read this board as a capability ranking.

So the '2% is hard' half of the original 2024 claim is now marginal rather than clearly false: one model is under 2%, most are not.

The grader-dependence point stands and is the reason this fact exists. Vectara's harder FaithJudge grader places the same models far higher — a model near 0.7% under HHEM scoring around 7.6% under FaithJudge, and an older frontier model near 12%. NOTE: the FaithJudge figures were NOT re-verified from a direct source this run; only the HHEM board above was. Treat the FaithJudge numbers as indicative of the size of the gap, not as current measurements.

What to take from this:

1. There IS a floor, it is above zero, and no prompt gets you to zero. Budget for it.
2. Any hallucination rate you are quoted is a statement about a grader, not about a model. A vendor number near 1% and a research number near 10% can both be honest measurements of the same system. Ask which detector produced it before comparing two figures or adopting one as your own expectation.
3. Therefore measure YOUR floor on YOUR task with YOUR acceptance criteria. Inheriting either number is wrong.

The design consequence survives intact: a product whose correctness requirement is stricter than its measured floor cannot be met by generation alone. You need a downstream factual-consistency check as a separate component, and the surrounding workflow — citation, verification, human confirmation on the consequential step — has to absorb a residual error rate that will not go away.
