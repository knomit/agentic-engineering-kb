# A serving-layer defect is silent AND unevenly distributed by channel — so below a traffic threshold, vendor disclosure is your only detection mechanism

Two properties of Anthropic's August-September 2025 serving incidents are each documented on its own. Put together they entail something neither states, and it decides whether your monitoring is worth anything for this class of fault.

PROPERTY ONE: the failure mode is a plausible-looking wrong token, not a crash or a slowdown. The TPU runtime optimisation "incorrectly assigned high probability to inappropriate tokens"; the XLA:TPU approximate top-k miscompilation mis-sorted token probabilities under mixed bf16/fp32 arithmetic. Symptoms were foreign-script characters mid-sentence and syntax errors injected into code — output that still parses as output. Nothing errors, so nothing alerts.

PROPERTY TWO: the incidence rate varied by more than four orders of magnitude across serving channels for the SAME defect — peaking at 16% of Sonnet 4 requests on Anthropic's own platform, 0.18% via Amazon Bedrock, and "less than 0.0004% of requests on Google Cloud's Vertex AI."

THE CONSEQUENCE: detection requires you to notice a rare silent event, so your ability to detect it at all is a function of your incidence rate times your request volume — neither of which you chose or can observe. At 0.0004%, you expect roughly one affected request per 250,000. A team serving a few thousand requests a day through that channel would have gone the entire multi-week incident without a single affected request, or with one, indistinguishable from ordinary model variance. They were not lucky in the sense of being protected; they were below the threshold at which the question was even askable.

WHAT TO DO WITH THIS. First, stop treating "our metrics look normal" as evidence of serving health for low-volume workloads — it is a statement about your sample size, not about the provider. Second, treat vendor status pages and postmortems as a primary monitoring input rather than a courtesy, because for a large fraction of consumers they are the ONLY channel with enough statistical power to see the fault. Third, if you do have the volume, the metric that carries the signal is the rate of malformed or out-of-distribution output (unexpected characters, unparseable structured output), broken out by serving channel — Anthropic's own remedy was adding detection tests for unexpected characters in output.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN, carried from both sources because both are easy to lose. It does NOT mean cloud-marketplace channels are safer: the ordering above is an artefact of how this specific bug interacted with each platform's routing, not a standing property, and a different incident could invert it entirely. It does NOT mean you can validate on one channel and ship on another — an eval run or canary against Bedrock is not evidence about the direct API, and vice versa; if you serve production traffic through more than one channel, the channel must be a dimension in your quality metrics or a channel-localised regression averages away. And it does not license ignoring the upstream engineering rule: sampling-path optimisations (approximate top-k, reduced precision, fused kernels) still require correctness evidence and not merely latency evidence, and a symptom-level workaround remains an open defect rather than a fix — the December 2024 workaround masked the deeper top-k bug for roughly eight months precisely by removing the signal that would have led to the cause.
