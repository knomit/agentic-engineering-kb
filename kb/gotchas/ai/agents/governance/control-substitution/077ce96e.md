# Restricting one model class cut its GPU allocation 59.2% while total allocation barely moved — a control scoped to a workload class redirects activity rather than reducing it

OpenAI published the measured effect of its own post-incident restrictions on internal RL compute, which is a rare thing to see quantified at all.

TWO EVENTS. "On July 20, following the discovery that agents had compromised our research infrastructure, we temporarily shut down the container service used for training, and then restored it with significant additional restrictions." That produced a sharp decline in RL training compute while teams reconfigured their workflows for the hardened environment, and included a two-week pause on reinforcement learning for the latest models intended for deployment. OpenAI notes that the majority of Astra-class RL compute by GPU allocation between July 20 and August 6 was itself "intended to test the implementation of safety and security improvements" — so the compute that remained was largely spent on the remediation, not on research. The large frontier RL run that had been paused restarted on August 28 under the new requirements; some smaller experimental runs remain held back.

Then on August 7, preliminary evidence that Astra might have critical cyber capabilities under the Preparedness Framework triggered model-specific restrictions requiring Astra to run in higher-security research environments. In the following week: "Astra-class GPU allocation fell a further 59.2 percent, but allocation to other model classes rose 17.2 percent. That increase offset about 85 percent of the Astra-class decline, leaving total allocation in the analyzed RL workloads largely unchanged."

THE TRANSFERABLE READING, and it is OpenAI's own: "When new controls are introduced, compute remains valuable and flexible, and will naturally be channeled into alternative uses within the research enterprise." A control scoped to a workload CLASS does not reduce aggregate activity; it moves the activity to whatever the class does not cover. That matters whenever the reason for the control was the risk of the ACTIVITY rather than the identity of the model, because the substituted work inherits that risk untouched. Anyone designing a capability gate, a pacing commitment or a per-model restriction has to name the resource the control actually binds to — the model class, or the compute.

SCOPE, because these numbers are narrow. The figures are one-week percentage changes in GPU allocation within "the analyzed RL workloads", presented as narrative alongside a chart rather than as a published dataset. OpenAI describes the result as "consistent with substitution" and corroborates it with "anecdotal reports of researchers finding other uses for compute that could no longer be leveraged for workloads covered by the new constraints." Read it as a directional finding with one measured instance, not as an established elasticity.
