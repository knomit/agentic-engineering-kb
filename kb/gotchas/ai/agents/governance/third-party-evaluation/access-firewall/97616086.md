# A third-party predeployment evaluation carried its headline acceleration estimate from a differently-cleared team inside the same evaluator, which could not share the evidence — and the publishing team states that it does not defend the claim

Access tiering in third-party evaluation is normally discussed as a boundary between the developer and the outside evaluator — that is what [[03fa7976]] tiers and what [[c262a592]] pushes to its limit. One published predeployment evaluation shows the same boundary running INSIDE the evaluator, and states the consequence itself.

THE FIREWALL, in the evaluator's own words. The report draws on "A highly experimental and preliminary report from a separate METR assessment of AI R&D acceleration inside Anthropic. This report was written by a separate METR team with elevated access compared to the team that wrote this content. As a result of this difference in access, the separate METR team shared its conclusions with us, but was not able to share the supporting evidence or details of their reasoning." The publishing team then draws the line explicitly: "Thus we use the evidence in the provided AI R&D report as an input to our assessment but do not argue directly in defense of its claims." A further input is used and not identified at all: "We also made use of an additional source of information which we are not able to disclose at this time."

THE CLAIM THAT ARRIVED THROUGH THE FIREWALL IS THE ONE A READER WILL QUOTE: "~1.5X overall acceleration in capabilities due to AI (i.e. 1.5 years in 1 year), with perhaps 30% chance of 2X acceleration." The publishing team bounds it in the next sentence: "because the preliminary report did not specify the time period for this estimate, it is unclear whether this estimate applies to the development of Claude Opus 5.5 or another period." So the figure has no stated time denominator, and the team publishing it has not seen the working behind it.

THREE MORE TERMS THAT A "AN INDEPENDENT EVALUATOR ASSESSED IT" SUMMARY DROPS.
- EDITORIAL AND FUNDING ARRANGEMENT: "This evaluation was conducted under an unpaid agreement for AI R&D assessment. We drafted the initial summary, and then Anthropic had the opportunity to review and edit the text. We signed off on this final text from the Claude Opus 5.5 system card." Unpaid, subject-reviewed-and-edited, evaluator-signed-off, and published inside the developer's own system card. That is a different independence shape from a directly funded engagement, and both are in circulation at once.
- SCOPE EXCLUSIONS, stated twice and up front: the work "was not meant to verify claims about compliance with any specific threshold from Anthropic's policies", and "does not attempt to assess whether Claude Opus 5.5 has or does not have particular alignment properties". A predeployment evaluation by a named third party can cover neither policy-threshold compliance nor alignment.
- ACCESS GRANTED: "Capability testing, conducted via API access granted over a period of 10 business days", across five named tasks — Budget NanoGPT Speedrun, Language Model Conceptual Argumentation, Train a Program, Gaming Bot, and Sunlight — plus a questionnaire and an interview with a developer researcher.

WHAT TO DO WITH THIS. When an external assessment is offered as assurance, ask three questions of each CLAIM in it rather than of the report as a whole: which team's access produced this claim, is the evidence behind it available to the people who published it, and what does the report say it did not examine. A claim that fails the second is hearsay with a logo on it.

WHAT THIS IS NOT: not an accusation of bad practice. Every term above is disclosed in the report's own opening paragraphs, and disclosure at that level is precisely what makes a third-party evaluation auditable — the arrangement is legible only because the evaluator wrote it down. The defect is introduced by the reader who repeats the 1.5X figure without the two sentences that bound it.
