# A "low risk" verdict that rests on the model lacking the capability says nothing about whether your safeguards work — and it expires the moment capability arrives

When a risk assessment concludes that misalignment risk is low, the conclusion has two possible load-bearing supports and they have opposite shelf lives: the model cannot cause the harm, or the controls would stop it. A reader who does not separate them inherits a guarantee that was never offered.

An independent evaluator reviewing a frontier lab's August 2026 risk report agreed with its "low" verdict while naming the first support explicitly: "We view risk as \"low\" mainly because we don't expect covered models to have the capabilities to cause hard-to-reverse catastrophic harm." In the same review it judged the report's methodology "far from adequate for confidently ruling out misalignment". Both statements hold at once, and that combination is the thing to carry: the verdict was right, and the evidence that the safeguards work was not there.

The operational consequence for anyone deploying on top of such an assessment is that a capability-grounded verdict transfers nothing to a setting where the capability is present — a longer-horizon deployment, broader tool access, a more capable successor model, or an attacker supplying the capability the model lacks. It also means the verdict is not a control validation, so it cannot be cited as evidence that monitoring, permissions or review are sufficient. Read a risk report for which of the two supports each claim rests on, and re-derive the ones resting on absent capability whenever capability moves.

Note the direction of travel in the same report, which is why this matters in practice: the lab's prior reports rated similar risk "very low", the initial draft shared with the reviewer on 24 July 2026 said "very low", and the published version said "low".

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: a capability-grounded low-risk finding is not a weak or dishonest finding. Absent capability is the strongest kind of safety argument there is while it holds, because it does not depend on a control behaving correctly. The error is treating it as durable or as transitive to your own deployment.
