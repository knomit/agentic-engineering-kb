# Context rot: long-context degradation is non-uniform and worsens with low query-needle similarity and distractors

Chroma tested 18 models (Claude Opus 4 / Sonnet 4 / 3.7 / 3.5 / Haiku 3.5, o3, GPT-4.1 + mini/nano, GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-3.5 Turbo, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, 2.0 Flash, Qwen3-235B-A22B/32B/8B) across needle-in-haystack variants (8 input lengths x 11 needle positions), LongMemEval (306 conversational QA prompts at ~113k tokens), and a Repeated Words task. Finding: performance varies significantly with input length even on trivial tasks — the assumption that the 10,000th token is processed as reliably as the 100th does not hold for any model tested.

The three specifics that change what you build:
- Degradation scales inversely with needle-question semantic similarity. Classic NIAH benchmarks use near-verbatim needles and therefore flatter models badly; real queries share little vocabulary with the answer and degrade much faster.
- A SINGLE distractor — topically related but not the answer — drops performance below the needle-only baseline; four compound it. Distractors are not uniformly harmful, and the spread between them widens as context grows, so per-distractor behaviour cannot be averaged away.
- Counterintuitively, shuffling the haystack to destroy local coherence consistently IMPROVES performance over a logically-flowing haystack. Concatenating well-structured documents is not the safe choice it looks like.

Consequence: retrieval precision (fewer, less-confusable chunks) buys more than recall does. Passing everything and trusting a large window is a measured regression, not a neutral default.
