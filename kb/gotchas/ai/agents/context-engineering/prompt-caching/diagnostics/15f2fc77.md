# Nine named reasons a prompt-cache prefix missed, reported one at a time — and one of them is a miss that never physically happened

On OpenAI's Responses API, for GPT-5.6 and later, setting `prompt_cache_options.comparison_response_id` to an earlier completed response's `id` makes the reply carry `prompt_cache_diagnostics`, whose `type` is one of `cache_hit`, `cache_miss`, `comparison_response_not_found` or `unavailable`, with a `reason` on a miss. The nine reasons are `model_changed`, `prompt_cache_key_changed`, `service_tier_changed`, `tools_changed`, `text_format_changed`, `reasoning_effort_changed`, `verbosity_changed`, `context_compacted` and `input_changed`. Reuse itself "requires an exact prefix match and compatible request settings", and the prefix "must meet the model's minimum cacheable length, which is 1,024 tokens for GPT-5.6 and later".

Four properties change how the surface is used.

It reports one cause per comparison, not a list: diagnostics give only the first classified reason, so the workflow is fix that one and repeat the comparison. A single clean report therefore does not mean a single cause was all there was.

`prompt_cache_key_changed` can be a miss that did not physically happen: changing the key may show as a miss in `usage` while no reuse was actually lost. A team that sets a cache key for per-tenant accounting should expect this reading and not chase it as a regression. The guide's own advice is to omit the key unless separate accounting is needed, and otherwise keep it stable within each group.

Diagnostics are not the metric. Their counts — `comparison_reusable_tokens` (the baseline's reusable prefix) and `cache_missed_tokens` (an estimate of what was not reused) — can differ from usage counts, and the guide directs you to `usage.input_tokens_details.cached_tokens`, with `cache_write_tokens`, for reuse and billing. Diagnostics are best effort and "may not classify every miss"; `unavailable` "does not indicate a hit or miss"; and records "expire after a short period", which returns `comparison_response_not_found` even when the response itself is still retrievable through the API.

A hit is not an absence of work: `cache_hit` means the prefix was reused, and "New input can still require processing."

Two things it costs nothing to get wrong about: setting `comparison_response_id` "only requests diagnostics" — it does not load the earlier conversation, change caching behaviour, or retrieve or persist the earlier response's content — and diagnostics add no charge and do not count separately against rate limits, though any extra baseline or retry request you issue to produce a comparison is billed and rate-limited normally. For multi-turn use, save each completed response's `id`, pass it as the comparison on the next turn, and omit it on the first.

The operational consequence: treat this as a debugger for a specific regression, not as a dashboard. The dashboard number is `cached_tokens`; the diagnostic tells you which single compatibility axis broke the prefix this time. See [[68d5055b]] for the ways to vary a request without breaking the prefix at all.
