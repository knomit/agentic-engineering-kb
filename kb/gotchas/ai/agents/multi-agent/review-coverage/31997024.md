# Two rounds of agent review passed a defect the project had already named as a known issue — a human caught it by looking at the output image

A team of parallel agents, orchestrated by Claude Science under high-level human instruction, built an all-sky ultraviolet map from four public surveys. Residual atmospheric glow in individual GALEX observations left circular brightness artifacts in dim fields. The failure was anticipated — "Claude had listed this as a known issue at the start of the project" — and it still survived review: "the map had still passed two rounds of review by other agents without the problem being caught". The author found it by visually inspecting the processed images, after which "Claude then corrected for that glow in all 38,000 observations".

Two separate things failed, and the second is the transferable one. A risk the pipeline itself had identified up front was never converted into something a reviewer could evaluate, so briefing a reviewer on a risk did not make the reviewer check for it; and two review passes over output whose defect is visible rather than assertable produced no detection at all.

Operational consequence: for every risk you name in a plan, write the check — an explicit assertion, a numeric diagnostic, or a rendered image the reviewer must look at and answer a question about. Where the output is an image, a map, a chart or any artifact whose defects are perceptible but not expressible as a test, schedule human visual inspection as a distinct stage with its own owner, and do not let the count of agent review rounds stand in for coverage of the risk list.

What this does not mean: not that agent review is worthless on this kind of work, and not that more review rounds would have caught it. The rounds were not the scarce resource; a check for this specific named risk was.

Scope: one project account published by its own author. It reports no measurement of how often agent review misses defects of this class.
