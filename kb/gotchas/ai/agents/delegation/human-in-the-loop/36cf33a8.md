# Having the human commit to a choice BEFORE the agent speaks does not protect the decision: analysts who had already stated a preference switched 60% of the time when the agent disagreed, against 6% when it agreed

The obvious mitigation for an agent steering a human's judgment call is to make the human decide first: ask for their view, then let the agent argue. Anthropic's randomized trial of 50 analysts (published 2026-10-27) measured that case incidentally and it does not hold up.

In 38 cases an analyst had expressed a preference on one of the analytical decisions BEFORE the agent expressed its (coin-flipped) lean. When the lean went against the choice they had already stated, they switched 60% of the time; when it agreed, they switched 6% of the time. The gap is 54 percentage points with a 95% confidence interval running from 24 to 82 points.

THE CONSEQUENCE FOR A DESIGN. Elicit-then-advise is a real pattern, and the paper itself floats it as something agent builders could do — ask the expert for their own view before offering one. But on this evidence eliciting the view first does not make the view stick; the agent's disagreement still moves most of them. If a workflow needs the human's independent position on the record, the position has to be captured as a committed artifact that the later decision is compared against, not merely asked for in conversation before the agent replies. Treating the elicited answer as the human's judgment, when the submitted answer came after the agent disagreed, records the agent's view under the human's name.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH. 38 cases is a small sample and the interval is correspondingly wide — anywhere from 24 to 82 points. The analysts' prior preferences were volunteered in the course of the work rather than systematically collected before the task, so the comparison is observational within a randomized trial, not itself randomized, and the paper does not claim causation for it. Switching may also be the correct response when the agent's argument is good: the study measures movement, not whether the moved-to answer was better. The leans were deliberately strengthened, so this is not a measurement of an agent with no assigned preference. Strength of the analyst's preference was not measured, so a weakly held view and a firmly held one are pooled.
