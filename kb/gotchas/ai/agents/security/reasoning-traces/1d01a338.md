# A client-held reference to a model's reasoning replays across sessions and accounts — whether it is an encrypted blob or a bare lookup key — and two providers' controls have now been defeated the same way

Frontier APIs return the model's reasoning as a value the client stores and passes back. Two designs are in use and BOTH have been defeated by replay: an encrypted blob containing the reasoning, and a bare reference that the provider looks up server-side. Because the value is opaque to the client, harnesses persist it in ordinary session files — "For Codex, session files are stored under directories such as: ~/.codex/sessions/2026/08/14" — and those files get shared, committed and attached to bug reports as if they carried nothing readable.

THE ARCHITECTURAL POINT, WHICH IS SHARPER THAN THE EXPLOIT. Nobody breaks the ciphertext, and in the second design there is no ciphertext to break. The value is replayed into a model on the same provider that CAN resolve it, and that model is then talked into reading the contents out with an injection along the lines of asking it to transcribe the thought. So the confidentiality of the trace is enforced by the receiving model's willingness to refuse, not by cryptography — and a model's willingness to refuse is the one control this pack has repeatedly documented failing. Any design that treats a provider-opaque field as a security boundary has picked a boundary made of model behaviour. The generalisation to any opaque client-held handle is in [[33aed84c]].

THE CROSS-ACCOUNT DEMONSTRATION, in the author's own words: "I created a reasoning trace containing a known password using GPT-5.6 Sol under one OpenAI account. I then attempted to recover that trace using GPT-5.6 Luna from a different account", and "I was able to recover reasoning content from OpenAI traces across separate sessions and accounts, including a password." A DIFFERENT model, a DIFFERENT account, and a weaker one at that — so possession of the value is closer to sufficient than tenancy suggests.

*** AND IT IS NO LONGER ONLY A CONSTRUCTED ATTACK. IT IS IN AN OPERATOR'S ENFORCEMENT DATA, AGAINST A DIFFERENT PROVIDER'S DIFFERENT DESIGN, AT INDUSTRIAL SCALE. *** Anthropic's September 2026 threat report describes its own control and its defeat in one passage: "When responding, Claude returns a reference to its raw thinking as a 'thinking signature' instead of the raw thinking to mitigate the risk of unauthorized distillation. This is used by our API to look up the raw thinking trace in subsequent calls to the API. Moonshot was able to circumvent this control and extract these reasoning traces by saving the reasoning signature from Claude's response, starting a new session, and eliciting Claude to convert the reasoning signature back into the full reasoning trace. These cross-session replay attacks allowed entities responsible for illicit distillation to harvest CoT reasoning transcripts." A second named operator, DeepSeek, "used the same cross-session replay attack used by Moonshot to extract CoT transcripts and circumvent our technical controls" and "used this technique to exfiltrate reasoning traces that would have otherwise been summarized."
WHY THAT DESIGN DIFFERENCE MAKES THE FACT STRONGER RATHER THAN NARROWER: Anthropic did not ship the reasoning to the client at all. It shipped a lookup key and kept the trace server-side — which is the obvious structural fix for the blob version — and the key was still replayed from a new session by a different principal, because the key carries no binding to the session or the account that created it. Withholding the payload does not help when the reference to it is bearer-grade. Anthropic states a remediation intent, not a completed one: "We're introducing new methods to strengthen our defenses against these tactics."

*** THE CAUSAL MECHANISM FOR THE BLOB DESIGN IS INFERRED INDEPENDENTLY BY TWO PARTIES, AND BOTH HEDGE. *** The blog author's version: "This seems possible because providers likely use shared encryption keys across users, sessions, and models." The cited paper reaches the same conclusion from systematic testing "against major API vendors including OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic": "providers appear to be using a single global key to encrypt and authenticate every reasoning block." It measures the consequence directly as three named compatibility forms — in- and cross-session, cross-user, and cross-model. NOTE THE HEDGES ("likely", "appear to be"): no provider is quoted confirming key management, so the mechanism remains inferred from behaviour, but it is inferred by two parties from different evidence.

SCALE, AND WHAT IT IS AND IS NOT A RATE OF. The large numbers are the paper's, not the blog author's: "By decoding 315,320 reasoning blocks scraped from public repositories, we recovered 367 Personally Identifiable Information (PII) artifacts and 182 credentials." The paper separately states "We collected 6,708 publicly available agent trajectories from GitHub and Hugging Face." THE PAPER DOES NOT SAY the 315,320 blocks came from exactly those 6,708 trajectories, so no ratio between the two figures is asserted here and none should be derived.
What matters is the population: PUBLIC material — blobs in repositories people had already committed — so 367 and 182 are hits across a large public corpus, not a recovery rate against a targeted victim. This establishes that the leak is real and already at scale in the open; it establishes nothing about your odds if someone comes after your session files specifically.

*** VENDOR RESPONSE: PARTIAL, AND THE FIELD EVIDENCE NOW SETTLES WHICH WAY TO READ IT. *** Disclosure happened, and "As of August 2026, the results presented in Figure 1 are no longer reproducible with attacks described in Section 2.4 and Appendix C because of mitigations implemented by providers following our disclosure."
READ THE SCOPE OF THAT SENTENCE CAREFULLY, because it is narrower than it first sounds. It says one figure's results, obtained by two named sections' attacks, no longer reproduce. It does NOT say the architecture changed or that the class is closed. AND THE ORDERING CUTS AGAINST THE COMFORTABLE READING: the blog post is dated 2026-08-16 and CITES this paper, so it post-dates it — and it reports a successful cross-account password recovery using a plain transcription request rather than the paper's extraction machinery. The September threat report then documents the same replay class working in production against a different provider. THESE ARE NOT IN CONTRADICTION: they describe different attacks and the paper's claim is explicitly scoped to its own. The class is open.

WHY THE PROTECTION EXISTS AT ALL, which explains why this is unlikely to be fixed the way a user would want: the stated purposes are "Preventing model behavior cloning and distillation; Protecting proprietary model behavior; Limiting information leakage from internal reasoning". The first two are the vendor's interest, not yours — and the threat report confirms it, describing the thinking signature as a control against distillation rather than against disclosure. The protection was never primarily a user-confidentiality control, so do not expect it to be hardened as one. The paper's own mitigation list is consistent with this: its only structural fix is to stop shipping traces or references to clients at all, which costs the providers the statelessness the design was chosen for.

WHAT TO DO, and the author's own framing: "Encrypted reasoning blobs should not be treated as harmless opaque data. If you share session files containing them, you may be sharing significantly more information than you realize." Concretely, treat an agent session file as containing cleartext secrets for every purpose that matters — retention policy, repository hygiene, support-bundle redaction, and what you attach to a ticket — and apply the same treatment to a bare signature or handle, which looks even more inert and is not. THIS PACK'S ADDITION, marked as inference: the failure is worse than a transcript leak, because reasoning is exactly where a model restates credentials, internal hostnames and intermediate conclusions that never appear in the visible output, so the protected field is systematically the MOST sensitive part of the record and the part everyone assumes is inert.

THE FLOW RUNS BOTH WAYS. This fact covers secrets leaking OUT. The same replayability carries instructions IN — an injection sealed inside a block is received as the model's own prior reasoning, invisible to any text-reading monitor. That is [[a7863d24]], and it is the more serious of the two for anyone building agents rather than handling session files.
