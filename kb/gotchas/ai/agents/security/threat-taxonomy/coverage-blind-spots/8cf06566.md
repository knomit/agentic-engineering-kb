# In OWASP's own agentic incident tracker, the two threat classes with the fewest documented real incidents are the two with the weakest control coverage — absence of incidents here means nobody is looking, not that it is rare

Appendix D of the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 is an Exploits & Incidents Tracker: roughly 21 dated real-world agentic incidents from about March to October 2025 (Gemini, Amazon Q, EchoLeak, ForcedLeak/Salesforce Agentforce, postmark-mcp, several Cursor and MCP issues, Replit, Copilot Studio, ToolShell RCE and others), each tagged with the ASI entries it exercises.

**Counting how often each ASI entry is tagged across that tracker — 67 tags in total:**
ASI02 Tool Misuse 11 · ASI05 Unexpected Code Execution 10 · ASI01 Goal Hijack 10 · ASI04 Supply Chain 8 · ASI07 Inter-Agent Communication 7 · ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning 5 · ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse 5 · ASI09 Human-Agent Trust 4 · ASI08 Cascading Failures 4 · ASI10 Rogue Agents 3.

**The inversion.** ASI10 (3) and ASI08 (4) are the two least-represented entries in OWASP's own incident record. They are also two of the three entries that OWASP's AIUC-1 crosswalk flags as having NO dedicated control requirement in that standard — inter-agent communication security (ASI07, ASI08, ASI10), agent identity attestation and containment (ASI03, ASI10) and cascading-failure containment (ASI08) are all in the "no requirement at all" bucket. So the categories with the thinnest incident evidence are the categories nobody has built controls for.

**Why that reading rather than the reassuring one.** A separate OWASP finding — that prompt injection fell out of the LLM Top 10 on raw incident count because defenses now catch it — establishes that a low incident count CAN indicate a well-defended category. That reading does not transfer here, and the deciding variable is whether controls exist: prompt injection is heavily instrumented and still counted low, whereas ASI08 and ASI10 are barely instrumented at all. Cascading failure and rogue-agent drift are also the two classes whose detection requires cross-agent, cross-session correlation, which is exactly the telemetry the crosswalk says is missing. Low count plus no controls plus no telemetry is an observation gap; low count WITH controls is a defense effect. Do not read a sparse row in an incident tracker as a safe row without checking which of the two you are looking at.

**Load-bearing caveats.** The tracker is a curated appendix, not a census — it samples published incidents and skews toward what vendors disclosed and researchers wrote up, which independently biases it toward the developer-tooling categories (ASI02, ASI05, ASI01) that dominate the counts. The counts above are token frequencies over the whole tracker, deliberately chosen because pdftotext interleaves multi-column table cells: the TOTAL number of times each ASI label appears survives that interleaving, whereas which incident each label pairs with does not. Per-incident mappings should not be quoted from this extract. The ~21 incident figure is from counting date cells and is approximate for the same reason.
