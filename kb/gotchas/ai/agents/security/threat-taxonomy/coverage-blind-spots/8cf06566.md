# The two agentic threat classes with the fewest documented incidents are the two the control crosswalk flags most often — a sparse row can mean nobody is looking, not that the class is rare

Appendix D of the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (document lines 2138-2750 of the pdftotext extract) is an Exploits & Incidents Tracker: **21 dated real-world agentic incidents, all in 2025, spanning March to October** (Gemini, Amazon Q, EchoLeak, ForcedLeak/Salesforce Agentforce, postmark-mcp, several Cursor and MCP issues, Replit, Copilot Studio, ToolShell RCE and others), each tagged with the ASI entries it exercises.

**How often each ASI entry is tagged across the tracker — 57 tags total:**
ASI02 Tool Misuse 10 · ASI05 Unexpected Code Execution 9 · ASI01 Goal Hijack 9 · ASI04 Supply Chain 7 · ASI07 Inter-Agent Communication 6 · ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning 4 · ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse 4 · ASI09 Human-Agent Trust 3 · ASI08 Cascading Failures 3 · ASI10 Rogue Agents 2.

**The inversion.** ASI10 (2 tags) and ASI08 (3) are the least-represented entries in OWASP's own incident record. They are also the two entries named MOST OFTEN across the eight control-gap areas in OWASP's AIUC-1 crosswalk: **ASI10 appears in 4 of the 8 gap areas, ASI08 in 3** — more than any other entry, where the next cluster (ASI01, ASI02, ASI03, ASI05) sits at 2 apiece and ASI04, ASI06 and ASI07 at 1. So the two classes with the thinnest incident evidence are the two whose controls the crosswalk finds most consistently absent or partial.

*** DO NOT RE-DERIVE THIS FROM THE "NO DEDICATED REQUIREMENT" BUCKET — IT DOES NOT SUPPORT THE CLAIM. ***
An earlier revision of this fact argued the inversion from the crosswalk's four "no requirement at all" gap areas. That is wrong and worth stating so nobody repeats it: those four areas between them touch SIX entries — ASI02, ASI03, ASI05, ASI07, ASI08, ASI10 — and two of them (ASI02, ASI05) are the MOST common entries in the tracker. Counted that way the correlation vanishes. Only the frequency-across-all-eight-gap-areas measure shows it.

**A counter-example that keeps the claim honest.** ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation is also sparse (3 tags) but appears in ZERO gap areas — the crosswalk does not flag it. So low incident count does NOT imply missing controls as a general rule. The claim here is specifically about ASI08 and ASI10, and ASI09 is the reason it must stay specific.

**Why the alarming reading rather than the reassuring one.** A separate OWASP finding — that prompt injection fell out of the LLM Top 10 on raw incident count because defenses now catch it — establishes that a low incident count CAN indicate a well-defended category. The deciding variable is whether controls exist: prompt injection is heavily instrumented and still counted low, whereas ASI08 and ASI10 are the entries the crosswalk most often finds uninstrumented. Cascading failure and rogue-agent drift are also the two classes whose detection requires cross-agent, cross-session correlation, which is exactly the telemetry the crosswalk says is missing. Low count WITH controls is a defense effect; low count WITHOUT them is an observation gap. Do not read a sparse row in an incident tracker as a safe row without checking which of the two you are looking at.

**Load-bearing caveats.**
- The tracker is a curated appendix, not a census. It samples published incidents and skews toward what vendors disclosed and researchers wrote up, which independently biases it toward the developer-tooling categories (ASI02, ASI05, ASI01) that dominate the counts.
- The counts are token frequencies over the appendix, deliberately chosen because pdftotext interleaves multi-column table cells: the TOTAL number of times each label appears survives that interleaving, whereas which incident each label pairs with does not. **Per-incident mappings must not be quoted from this extract.**
- COUNTING RANGE MATTERS, and this fact got it wrong once. A first pass counted lines 2149-2900 and reported 67 tags. That range runs past Appendix D into the Acknowledgements section, whose entry-lead roster lists ASI01-ASI10 once each — adding exactly one phantom tag to every entry. The error was UNIFORM, so the ranking (which is what the finding rests on) was unaffected, but every absolute number was inflated by one and the total by ten. When counting tokens over a PDF region, bound the region on the appendix that follows it, not on an eyeballed line number.
