# Summarizing rather than publishing full reasoning cost a distilling model 8.6 points of SWE-bench Verified (80.4% to 71.8%) — a visible reasoning channel is a capability-transfer channel, and its fidelity is the dial

Anthropic reports a direct measurement of what reasoning visibility is worth to someone copying your model. Training a model on Claude transcripts whose reasoning had been summarized before being shown to users, "with only the summarized transcripts, it achieved 71.8%" on SWE-bench Verified. "When training on the full transcripts, the distilled model achieved 80.4%". Same extraction pipeline, same target; the only change is the fidelity of the reasoning the product exposed.

The operational consequence for anyone who exposes reasoning, plans, scratchpads or intermediate tool traces from their own product or API: that surface is a training corpus for a competitor, and it is not a binary. You do not have to choose between publishing traces and hiding them, because degrading the trace degrades the transfer — summarization, redaction of intermediate steps, and emitting conclusions without derivations are all dials on the same channel. Note what happened next in the source's own account: once passive collection paid less, attackers moved to ACTIVE extraction, prompting the model to reveal its hidden reasoning, which is a different attack needing different controls.

What this does NOT mean. It does not mean summarization defeats distillation: 71.8% is still a strong score, so the measured effect is a cost imposed on the attacker, not the removal of the channel. It does not speak to distillation from FINAL OUTPUTS with no reasoning at all, which the source does not address. And it does not establish that summarization is free for legitimate users — what a summarized trace costs your own debugging, evaluation and trust surfaces is unmeasured here, so treat it as unknown rather than zero.
