# Two shipped blocking monitors for coding agents were bypassed on over 55% of attacks, and pointing the attacker at known blind spots took it to 43% and 72% — while both held benign false positives under 1%

Anthropic's Alignment Science team red-teamed two PRODUCTION blocking monitors — Claude Code's Auto Mode and OpenAI Codex's Guardian — with an adversarial coding agent (mainly Opus 5), against a snapshot of September 2026 monitor versions. A blocking monitor is the synchronous kind: it must decide before the action runs, because for irreversible harms such as data exfiltration asynchronous review is too slow.

The headline, verbatim: "Production monitors fail to block over 55% of attacks." Directed at the monitor's blind spots rather than attacking blind, the same setup reached "43% Attack Success Rate (ASR) against Auto Mode" and "72% ASR against Guardian".

EVERY COMPONENT OF THE MEASUREMENT CONDITION MATTERS, and dropping any one of them turns these numbers into a different claim:
- each trial let the attacker keep trying "before incurring 20 blocks from the monitor", so ASR is a budgeted-attacker figure, not a per-action evasion rate;
- the credible estimates come from live rollouts in two environments — an "arena" of Claude Code in Docker with deterministic side-task checkers, and a "simulation" where an LLM dungeon master generates tool outputs — not from transcript replay or prompt review, which were also used;
- the authors treat the monitor as TRUSTED and do not study monitor–attacker collusion, even though some monitor models are close in capability and lineage to the attacker (Opus 4.8 monitoring Opus 5);
- Guardian's sandboxing features were not red-teamed at all.

TWO CHANGES MOVED THE NUMBER, in opposite directions and for different reasons. Monitoring every in-directory edit rather than only executed commands produced "Guardian Strict (31% ASR)", which closed the execute-then-write path. Hardening the prompt and adding stages gave Auto Mode ++, where "Attack success rate falls by over 50 percentage points when we use our new Auto Mode ++ monitor."

The benign cost stayed low throughout: "All monitors block under 1% of the 4,000 benign SWE-chat actions."

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It does not mean Auto Mode is twice as good as Guardian: the authors attribute Guardian's higher ASR to the weaker model behind it, so the comparison is between two deployed configurations at one moment, not between two designs. It does not mean a blocking monitor is cheap because false positives are under 1% — the latency and token cost of the stages that bought the recall is the price, and the multi-context defences that worked were the slowest. And because production monitors change constantly, these are snapshot figures: cite them as September 2026 versions, not as the current state of either product.
