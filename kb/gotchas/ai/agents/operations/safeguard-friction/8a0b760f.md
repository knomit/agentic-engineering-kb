# A misalignment monitor that pauses a task degrades differently per surface — interactive users get a review prompt, the API just stops — and long-running agents are a named false-positive driver

OpenAI is deploying misalignment monitoring in production for Astra-class models: "a system of classifiers which check the model's reasoning and actions for unauthorized behavior and automatically stop potentially unauthorized activity", described as resembling its monitoring for internal deployments. Three consequences it states plainly, all of which you design around rather than discover.

1. THE SURFACE DECIDES THE FAILURE MODE. "If the misalignment monitor pauses a task, users in ChatGPT or Codex may be asked to review the action before continuing. When using other surfaces like the API, the task will stop." The same classifier at the same threshold is a human-in-the-loop checkpoint on an interactive surface and a hard termination on an API call. If you build on the API, a safeguard trip is an unrecoverable end-of-run rather than a prompt, and your retry, checkpointing and idempotency design has to assume a task can be terminated mid-flight for a reason your code cannot inspect.

2. LONG-RUNNING AGENTS ARE FLAGGED AS A CATEGORY. The system "may occasionally flag legitimate activity as potential cyber misuse or unauthorized behavior, leading to it inadvertently being slowed, paused, or stopped. This can include work that does not appear directly related to cybersecurity or tasks in which an agent is running for an extended period." Duration itself is a risk feature. Long-horizon autonomous agents therefore sit in the false-positive-prone region by construction, and that is a property of the safeguard rather than of the workload.

3. THE FRICTION IS ACKNOWLEDGED, DELIBERATE AND MEANT TO BE TEMPORARY. "At launch, we expect Astra's safeguards to create more friction than we ultimately intend in order to protect against potential misuse", and "Extra safety checks can sometimes slow, pause, or stop legitimate work, including defensive cybersecurity." Access to advanced cybersecurity workflows begins with a small group of alpha testers and expands through Daybreak Blue.

WHY THE POSTURE IS THIS AGGRESSIVE. OpenAI designated Astra as meeting the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework — the first model it has placed at that level — which it says requires stronger safeguards during development and before release. The framework's Critical threshold is met if EITHER the model "can identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention", OR it "can devise and execute end-to-end novel strategies for cyberattacks against hardened targets given only a high level desired goal."

WHAT THE MONITOR IS AND IS NOT FOR, in OpenAI's words: "These safeguards cannot replace good alignment of our models as capabilities increase, and our goal is for future models to be aligned well enough that these safeguards are never triggered." It is a second layer with an intended trigger rate of zero, which is also why its false positives are treated as a calibration problem rather than as the cost of doing business.
