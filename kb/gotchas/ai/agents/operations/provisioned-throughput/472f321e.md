# In a provisioned-throughput partitioned store the enforced unit is the physical partition, not the key you reason about — so documented per-key limits are unreachable and accumulating data alone halves your throughput ceiling at every split

A managed store that bills and limits by provisioned throughput, and shards by a key you choose, has an enforcement unit you do not see. Three consequences follow, and the third is the one that bites a team whose request rate never changed.

THE ENFORCED UNIT IS COARSER THAN THE DOCUMENTED ONE. "While documented data size and throughput limits apply to logical partition keys, the physical partition is actually where limits are enforced. In practice, this means logical partitions are unable to reach 10,000 RU/s. Instead, they share the physical partition's limits with every other logical partition key colocated on that physical partition." So a published per-key ceiling is an upper bound you cannot plan against, and co-located keys interfere: the operator reports "workloads operating on data for niche product features disrupting core login and signup flows".

THE PER-PARTITION CEILING IS A QUOTIENT, NOT A CONSTANT. "The container's provisioned throughput is distributed roughly evenly across the physical partitions for that container." Adding partitions therefore divides the same budget further.

THE THIRD IS THE TRAP: STORAGE GROWTH ALONE DEGRADES THROUGHPUT. Partitions split on a storage threshold, and "Each child receives half the throughput capacity" — published as before-split 10K RU/s becoming "5K RU/s capacity × 2 partitions". The operator states the failure mode directly: "For our most storage-heavy, blob-like use cases, this creates a failure mode where storage growth alone reduces throughput capacity for active workloads even while request rates and usage remain stable." The workload named it "weak partitions".

WHY THIS MATTERS PARTICULARLY FOR AGENT STATE: conversation history, memory stores, transcripts and tool-call logs are exactly the storage-heavy, blob-like, append-mostly shapes described. An agent product whose per-user data grows monotonically while its QPS is flat is on the exact curve where capacity silently falls toward demand, and the first symptom is throttling errors on a workload nobody changed.

WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT: monitor provisioned throughput against physical partition COUNT, not against request rate — the operator ran "a script for managing weak partitions by synchronizing the total throughput provisioned for each container with its number of physical partitions". The structural fix is a mode where splits are driven by throughput usage as well as storage: the vendor built one allowing "every physical partition to scale up to 10K RU/s" with "RU-usage-based splits in addition to storage-usage-based splits", which let them "stop monitoring and scaling up containers manually". Note the earlier off-the-shelf serverless mode was not sufficient because it "scaled based on storage usage only and limited RU usage to 5K RU/s per physical partition" — it fixed weak partitions at the cost of capping throughput-heavy workloads.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: the figures above are one vendor's limits at one point in time, not a general law. The transferable claim is the shape — enforcement at an invisible coarser unit, a ceiling that is a quotient of a fixed budget, and a split rule keyed on a dimension other than the one you are throttled on.
