# Raising reasoning effort removes verification failures and leaves ambiguity failures standing — across 370 attempts per setting the pass rate rose by 74 while the wrong-reading bucket nearly doubled

Anthropic ran one task (html-js-filter, writing an HTML/JS sanitizer) 370 times at low effort and 370 times at max with Fable 5.1, and published the per-bucket failure counts. Passes went from 140 to 214 of 370. Median tokens went from 73k to 222k per attempt.

The buckets that shrank are all verification failures — the model not checking its own work hard enough: "a bug its tests missed" 40 to 14, "wrong or incomplete fix" 31 to 10, "misread a requirement" 45 to 26, "overfit to the examples" 7 to 3, "close, but not exact" 10 to 6, "works on my machine" 2 to 1.

Two buckets did not follow. "Got the domain rule wrong" moved only 32 to 24. "Picked the wrong reading" ROSE, from 25 to 47 — in absolute attempts out of the same 370, not merely as a share of a smaller remainder. Both are failures of resolving what the task meant, not of executing it.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE. Treat effort as a lever on verification, not on interpretation. If your failures are the model shipping unverified work, effort buys them back and the post-mortem is about budget. If your failures are the model resolving an ambiguous requirement the wrong way, a higher setting can buy you a more thoroughly executed wrong answer, and the fix is to disambiguate the spec or add an explicit ask-before-building step — not to turn the dial up. A failure report that reads "we were already at max effort and it still got it wrong" is evidence about the requirement, not about the budget.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. It does not mean max effort is worse: it won 74 more of 370 attempts. It means the failures that survive at high effort are composed differently from the ones at low effort, so the triage question changes with the setting rather than the volume of failures simply shrinking.

SCOPE. One task, one model, from the vendor's own internal runs, with its production safety interventions switched off for this model; the source states that in Anthropic's shipped products that model's safeguards hand some security requests to a different model. The counts are the vendor's published figure for this task, not an independent replication, and the buckets are its own labels rather than a standard taxonomy.
