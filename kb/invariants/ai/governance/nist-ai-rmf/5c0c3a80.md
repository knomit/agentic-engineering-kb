# NIST AI RMF requires incident response and decommissioning to cover third-party AI explicitly — the clauses agent builders skip

Most of the NIST AI RMF GOVERN function is ordinary governance, but four subcategories bind directly on anyone building an agent on top of somebody else's model or tool server, and they are the ones typically absent from an agent team's actual runbooks.

GOVERN 6.2 requires verifying that incident response plans ADDRESS THIRD-PARTY AI SYSTEMS specifically, and that vital systems have considered redundancy mechanisms. Concretely: your IR plan almost certainly covers your own service failing, and almost certainly does not name what happens when a model provider changes behaviour on a version rollover, deprecates an endpoint, or an MCP server you mount starts returning different content. Redundancy here means a second provider you have actually tested against, not one named in a doc.

GOVERN 6.1 requires transparency into third-party systems — training data, algorithms, stated assumptions and limitations — plus thorough testing of them and clear usage instructions, applied across the supply chain and full product lifecycle. Note the tension this creates in practice: closed model providers do not supply most of that, so the compliance artifact ends up being your own measured characterisation of the dependency rather than a vendor disclosure.

GOVERN 1.6 makes the AI system inventory a named-owner artifact with defined attributes — documentation, source-code links, incident response plans, data dictionaries, and contact info for responsible people — with the stated preference that ALL systems are inventoried, and high-risk systems as the floor.

GOVERN 1.7 requires decommissioning procedures that preserve ancillary data and artifacts specifically so the system can be understood later, including for legal or forensic investigation, and that document how the shutdown propagates to dependent systems. For agents this is the clause that says you cannot simply delete the trace store and prompt history when you retire a version.

These are framework requirements, not measured claims — the framework asserts what an organisation should have, and does not evidence that having it reduces incidents.
