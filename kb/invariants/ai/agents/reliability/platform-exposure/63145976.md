# Your exposure to a provider incident is a property of the serving channel, not the model — the same bug hit 16% of first-party requests and 0.0004% on Vertex

In Anthropic's August-September 2025 routing incident, the same underlying defect produced impact rates differing by more than four orders of magnitude depending on where the request was served: it peaked at 16% of Sonnet 4 requests on Anthropic's own platform (August 31), 0.18% of Sonnet 4 requests via Amazon Bedrock (from August 12), and "less than 0.0004% of requests on Google Cloud's Vertex AI between August 27 and September 16."

The rule this establishes: model identity does not determine reliability behaviour. A given model name served through a different channel is a different deployment with a different infrastructure surface, its own routing and load-balancing layer, and its own rollout schedule for both the bug and the fix — Anthropic's fix landed September 4 but the rollout only completed September 16-18.

Two misreadings to avoid. This does NOT mean cloud-marketplace channels are safer; the ordering is an artefact of how the specific bug interacted with each platform's routing, not a standing property, and a different incident could invert it. And it does not mean you can validate on one channel and ship on another: an eval run or a canary against Bedrock is not evidence about the direct API, and vice versa. If you serve production traffic through more than one channel, the channel must be a dimension in your quality metrics, or a channel-localised regression averages away.
