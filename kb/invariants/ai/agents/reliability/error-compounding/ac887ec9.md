# Per-step accuracy compounds exponentially: 95% per step is 60% at 10 steps and 0.6% at 100

Chip Huyen's arithmetic, verbatim, under her heading "Compound mistakes": "If the model's accuracy is 95% per step, over 10 steps, the accuracy will drop to 60%, and over 100 steps, the accuracy will be only 0.6%." Multiplication, not addition. It is the number that should govern how you scope agent tasks.

WHAT THIS CHANGES (this pack's analysis — the source states the arithmetic and does not draw these consequences): chasing the last few points of per-step accuracy has enormous leverage on long tasks, while adding steps is far more expensive than it looks in a design doc. A plan that reads as "just a few more tool calls" can move a feature from usable to unusable. It also means the way to make long-horizon agents work is to break the multiplicative chain — checkpoints that verify and correct mid-run, decomposition into independently verifiable segments, or a human confirmation at the step where errors would otherwise propagate.

THE CAVEAT THAT KEEPS THIS FROM BEING FATALISTIC, also this pack's and not the source's: the multiplication assumes independent steps with no recovery. Real agents retry, re-read and self-correct, which is exactly why reflection and verification loops earn their token cost on long tasks even though they look like pure overhead on short ones. Use the arithmetic to decide where to spend on verification, not to conclude long agents are impossible. Stated as a property of the model rather than a measurement: nobody in this corpus has published the recovery-adjusted curve.

RELATED FROM THE SAME SOURCE, and these are her section headers verbatim, under "Planning failures" rather than under evaluation: "Invalid tool" (not in the inventory), "Valid tool, invalid parameters", and "Valid tool, incorrect parameter values". The third is the hardest to detect and the one output-only scoring misses entirely — a structurally well-formed call with the wrong value in it passes every schema check you have. (The observation about output-only scoring is this pack's; the taxonomy is hers.)
