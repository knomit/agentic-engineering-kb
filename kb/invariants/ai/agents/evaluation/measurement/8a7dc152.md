# Every agent quality number is a joint property of the system and its measuring apparatus — six measurements show the apparatus moving it more than the model does

A recurring error in agent engineering is adopting somebody else's number as your expectation. Six independent measurements in this pack say you cannot: in every case, changing only the MEASURING APPARATUS moved the number by more than a model upgrade would, while the system under test was unchanged.

The instances, each naming what part of the apparatus moved:

- THE GRADER. Vectara's HHEM-2.3 board (last updated 2026-05-11) puts the best grounded-summarization hallucination rates at roughly 1.8%-4.1%. Vectara's harder FaithJudge grader places the SAME models far higher — a model near 0.7% under HHEM scoring around 7.6% under FaithJudge. A vendor number near 1% and a research number near 10% can both be honest measurements of the same system. (The FaithJudge figures are indicative only; they were not re-verified from a direct source.)
- THE QUERY DISTRIBUTION. Naively LLM-generated eval queries echo the source document's wording — 11.91% of naive query pairs sat above 0.9 cosine similarity to each other, and naive queries averaged 0.716 query-document similarity against 0.628 for deliberately distinct generation. High document similarity makes retrieval look easy in a way real queries never are.
- THE HARNESS IMPLEMENTATION. The same model scores significantly differently on MMLU under the HELM implementation, the EleutherAI implementation, and the original paper's, from prompt formatting, answer extraction and scoring choices alone.
- THE RESOURCE ALLOCATION. Terminal-Bench 2.0 moved ~6 percentage points (p < 0.01) across six resource configurations with model, harness and task set held CONSTANT. Below 3x headroom the movement is reliability noise; above it, limits were silently blocking the agent from invoking heavyweight tools at all.
- THE AGGREGATION RULE. tau-bench's pass^k (all k trials succeed) versus the familiar pass@k (at least one succeeds) are opposite metrics. At k=3 and a 75% per-trial rate, pass^k is about 42%. Published retail figures fell from pass^1 0.692 to pass^4 0.462.
- THE JUDGE. LLM judges carry position, length and self-preference biases; measured self-preference was a 25% higher win rate for Claude-v1 on its own outputs and about 10% for GPT-4.

WHAT FOLLOWS, and it is three separate obligations:

1. NEVER INHERIT A NUMBER AS A TARGET. Measure your floor on your task with your acceptance criteria. Any rate you are quoted is a statement about an apparatus, not about a model.
2. WHEN COMPARING TWO NUMBERS, ESTABLISH THE APPARATUS FIRST. Do not compare a number you measured to one someone published unless you ran the identical harness; cite the implementation alongside any vendor or competitor claim. Treat leaderboard differences below 3 percentage points with skepticism unless configuration is documented and matched.
3. FREEZE YOUR OWN APPARATUS BEFORE TUNING. A harness change and a model change produce the same-looking delta on your dashboard. Specify guaranteed allocation and hard kill threshold as two SEPARATE numbers, run at multiple times on multiple days, and report enforcement methodology with the score.

THE SALVAGE, which keeps this from being nihilistic: relative orderings survive apparatus changes far better than absolute values do. Chroma found that the relative ranking of embedding models was CONSISTENT between ground-truth and generated queries — so synthetic benchmarks are usable for CHOOSING between retrievers even when their absolute numbers are inflated. Use borrowed apparatus to rank options; use your own to set expectations.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN. Not a claim that published numbers are worthless or dishonest — most of these are careful measurements whose scope was stated and then lost in transmission. Not a claim that all apparatus effects are noise either: the Terminal-Bench spread above 3x headroom was real capability being unlocked, not variance, and the pass^1/pass^k gap is a real reliability property rather than a measurement artifact. And each source carries its own scope: the hallucination figures are grounded-summarization specific and the top of that board is dominated by small efficient models rather than flagships, so it is not a capability ranking; the Databricks long-context thresholds are mid-2024 models and several generations old; the tau-bench absolute values are superseded by tau2-bench; and the judge self-preference magnitudes are 2023-era, where the DIRECTION of the bias is durable but the size must be re-measured on whatever judge you deploy.

This is adjacent to, but distinct from, the finding that harness causes should be checked before model causes when diagnosing underperformance. That one is about DIAGNOSTIC ORDERING. This one is about NUMBER PORTABILITY — even a correctly-diagnosed system produces a figure that does not travel.
