# A safety eval result does not transfer across the language it was run in: on one 145-question suite every model tested scored worst in Simplified Chinese and best in English, with Traditional Chinese between

Running a behavioural safety eval in English and shipping to a non-English market is an untested extrapolation, and the size of the gap has been measured on a published suite.

THE MEASUREMENT. Cognition ran 145 politically sensitive questions, five independent samples each, in English, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese, across seven models, grading each response against a ground-truth reference on six axes including "active propaganda rate, CCP narrative alignment, refusal rate, deflection rate, completeness, and factual accuracy". The reported shape: "Across all models, propaganda rates were highest in Simplified Chinese and lowest in English, with Traditional Chinese generally in between." The direction held for every model in the published per-language tables, and the spread within one model was large — one model's propaganda rate ran 0.6% in English, 6.9% in Traditional Chinese and 29.2% in Simplified Chinese. Factual-accuracy scores also degraded with the same ordering for the frontier models tested.

CONSEQUENCE. The language of the prompt is an eval dimension, not a presentation detail, and the per-language result is not derivable from the English one even for the same model. Any suite whose verdict is used to gate a deployment has to be run in the languages that deployment serves, including script variants of the same language, which this measurement separates.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is not a claim that the ordering is universal or that the cause is known; the published set is one topic domain (politically sensitive questions about one state), three languages, and models predominantly from one region, so the language effect is not separated from the training-data provenance of the models tested. It is evidence that the effect is large enough to invalidate single-language generalisation, not a model of why.

MEASUREMENT CAVEATS. All per-language scores are in the source's appendix tables rather than its prose. The grader for this axis is described only as "a strong judge model" and is not named, and no inter-rater agreement or judge validation is published; the sample counts per model-language cell also vary below the nominal 725 for two models with no stated explanation.
