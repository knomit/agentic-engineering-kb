# A system card is revised in place after publication and its comparison columns may hold later versions of the comparator models — so a cited figure needs the model version AND the card revision date, and a successful re-fetch of the same URL proves nothing

A frontier-model system card is a living document, and the GPT-6 Astra card published 2026-09-03 carries a change log proving it: five dated revisions inside 26 days, of four different kinds. One CHANGED PUBLISHED NUMBERS — "For the HealthBench evaluations, we revised the values in the main text for GPT-6 Astra to correct for a misconfiguration in the previous evaluations" (Sep 22). One RENAMED AND REDEFINED A METRIC and deleted evidence — the Verbalized Metagaming section was renamed and re-scoped, and "We also removed a previous metric comparison plot to reduce confusion" (Sep 9). One noted that "we have updated some of the evaluations" and put the results on the NEW versions in an appendix (Sep 22). Two ADDED APPENDICES covering models that did not exist when the card was published (Sep 22, Sep 29). So the card's own numbers, its metric definitions, and its scope all moved after publication.

THE COMPARATOR COLUMN MOVES TOO, AND THE CARD SAYS SO TWICE. "For previously launched models, the values published at launch reflect the versions evaluated at that time. The comparison values for previously launched models that are shown here may reflect later versions of those models, and may vary from the values published at launch." And: "Because policies, graders, datasets, evaluations, and other measurement details evolve over time, scores from previous system cards not included in the table below should generally not be considered directly comparable to these most recent results." The baseline a card compares against is therefore not the model as the earlier card reported it, and the earlier card's own number for that model is a DIFFERENT quantity.

THE OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE, in three parts. (1) Cite a system-card figure with both the model version and the revision date of the card you read, because the version alone no longer identifies the number. (2) Never build a trend line by taking one figure from each of several cards; the only within-card-comparable numbers are the ones printed in the same table, and the card explicitly disclaims the rest. (3) When a figure matters, record the surrounding sentence, not just the value, because a renamed or re-scoped metric can keep a similar name while measuring something else.

THE FORESEEABLE MISREADING, AND IT IS THE DANGEROUS ONE: the URL does not change when the card is revised. Re-fetching the same address and getting HTTP 200 with a page that still discusses the same topic is not evidence that the claim you took from it still stands. A revision can correct a number, drop the plot the number came from, or redefine the metric, all at the same address. Verify by re-reading the specific sentence, and read the change log first — on this card the change log is where every one of those edits is disclosed.

This also bounds a benign-looking convenience: these cards are the most authoritative public source for per-model measurements, and nothing here says not to use them. It says an undated citation to one is not a citation.
