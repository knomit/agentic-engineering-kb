# Chain-of-thought monitoring rests on two separable mechanisms and only one is robust: necessity (the model cannot reach the goal without verbalising) survives adversarial pressure, propensity (it verbalises anyway) does not

Redwood Research separates the two reasons a readable chain of thought is worth monitoring, and the separation is the useful part because the two have different failure behaviour under optimisation pressure.

"Necessity: AIs lack the capability to achieve their goals without verbalizing their plans in CoT." The mechanism underneath it is a hard architectural bound — "CoT is the only means by which models can reason for a greater number of serial steps" than one forward pass affords. A task needing more serial depth than that must leave its steps in text, whatever the model would prefer.

"Propensity: AIs tend to verbalize their plans in CoT even when this is not necessary for achieving their goals." This is a disposition, not a bound, and Redwood states that "we consider propensity-based arguments for CoT monitorability to be weaker" because "this mechanism is less robust to adversarial pressure".

THE TEST TO APPLY TO YOUR OWN MONITORING, and it decides how much weight the channel can carry: is the reasoning you are reading in the transcript because the model could not have done the work otherwise, or because it happened to write it down? The first holds against a model selected against the monitor. The second erodes exactly when it matters, which is what the corpus already records empirically — the channel degrades as soon as the model merely knows it is monitored ([[491d1039]]), and it holds only while nothing optimises the chain of thought ([[b446bef6]]). This fact names the mechanism that makes those two results predictable rather than surprising.

THE ARCHITECTURAL CONSEQUENCE, which is Redwood's actual argument: anything that lets a model do more serial reasoning without writing it as text removes the necessity half. COCONUT-style continuous latent thoughts replace the text chain outright; a full-bandwidth transformer keeps the text chain but adds a parallel latent channel, raising no-CoT capability; a looped transformer keeps a text bottleneck but adds serial depth between bottlenecks, which Redwood expects to degrade monitorability less severely but still to degrade it. Their recommendation is that "AI developers should have a strong default presumption to avoid them", and that a model not trained to use its latents may behave like a standard transformer but that this is hard to verify without serious elicitation, including training the model to use them.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN: it is not a claim that propensity-based monitoring is worthless — it is a claim about which half of the guarantee survives pressure, and an action-trajectory monitor is the tier that does not depend on either half ([[737460a3]]). Nor is it a measurement: the post reports no experiment of its own and argues from other people's results — time-horizon data, a published system-card graph, a diffusion-model paper, reported model behaviour — so treat the ranking of the three architecture families as reasoning over cited evidence rather than as a finding. Its own comparative wording is hedged: looped transformers' opaque depth is "less concerning than the full opaque recurrence in COCONUT or full-bandwidth transformers, but it still seems likely to degrade monitorability". Note also that forbidding reasoning in one channel has been measured to move it to another observable channel rather than remove it ([[9a8a1590]]); latent channels are the case where the destination is not observable.
